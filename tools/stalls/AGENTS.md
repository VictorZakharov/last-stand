# `tools/stalls`

The stalls harness (`README.md`): the game played in the installed Chrome on this machine's GPU, traced, for the stalls the lab's Chromium never shows (Chrome on Windows holding its GPU process up while it builds a Skia program the page needs).

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md); each directory under this one has an AGENTS.md of its own.

- **It runs at the priority it's started at** (`stalls: running at ... priority`, its first line), unlike the lab, which lowers itself: below normal, the desktop's own work would read as the game's stalls.
- **Look at the machine first.** Stalls of 1.4 to 1.9 s in the owner's Chrome went away with a reinstall of the GPU driver; another agent's browser on the same GPU is in no trace of this one.
- It starts Chrome on a fresh profile of its own: a Chrome it started outlives a lost connection, so make sure it's closed afterwards.
- Why page elements moved every frame stalled the lobby, and what replaced them: the overlays gotcha in `src/ui/overlay/AGENTS.md`.
- **Set the page up as the report's player had it** (`--hero`, `--stash`, `--view`, `--biome`, `--scale`, `--perf`): a first boot's lobby has an empty stash, and hovering it lit nothing, so four measures of #267 read 145 frames a second where the owner had 44. A report gives the mode, the view, the pixel ratio and, in its late shaders, the weapon.
- **A variant is a script and its argument** (`variants.mjs`): `styled` adds a stylesheet, so a cause is A/B'd on production with no build (the glow's loops held still, each alone, on a layer of its own).
- **Measure what the player did, the way they did it, at their pace** (`--item-ms`: the owner hovers "pretty quickly", 100 ms an item; 250 was gentler): moving between items isn't gliding over them. #269's first preview read 143 frames a second in every glide and 72 in the sweep, which the owner saw (34 in their overlay). A scenario for each way a complaint describes, before calling it fixed.
- **A/B a change against production in rounds, both in one run** (`--url this,https://...` with `--rounds`): each URL's page is set up once and they take turns, so the GPU's other users slow both alike. Measured one after the other, minutes apart, this tree's frames to beat read 121 a second and production's 135, and the A/B said nothing; in turns, 141 and 141. The page waiting its turn is paused in the debugger (`stalls.mjs` `freeze`): the page lifecycle's frozen state (`Page.setWebLifecycleState`) went on drawing its game, 300 to 450 ms of work a second in the other's measure, which the report now names (other pages' renderers, by thread and task).
- **Time a boot with `--scenario boot`, a first visit and a return, this tree beside production** (`--url this,https://...`, `--rounds 3`), or beside a commit before a change (`--url this,ref:<git ref>`: production is no yardstick for one step, and the machine's own load moved both by 20% from one run to the next): a first visit is the cold case (Chrome's shader cache empty: ANGLE compiled for 94 s of its threads' CPU), a return what most players meet. Each step is read off the loading screen, so production is timed without marks of its own. A step's work in all says what the page waited on (the workers' maps, ANGLE's compiles, a long task of its own); `--js` names the page's functions in it. It says how the loading screen left (the logo blown away, cut short by its watchdog, a plain fade and why), and the maps the workers made; `--stall-blow 600` is the watchdog's canary (one stall on a fast machine: the blow must stay, where production's switched itself off for good). The lobby shows before the fight's programs are warm: the report says when the fight was warm (Start pressed on a first visit, its bar's time over the rounds) and the warm-ups' longest steps, and `the lobby revealed`'s frames are the lobby's as they warm behind it. The lobby's frames a second are counted as the perf overlay counts them (`lobbyRateOf`), as the owner read 17 off it: a return's lobby drew 18 to 36 in its first second while its fight's parts warmed back to back behind it, and 0 to 6 for 3 s where the fight was never compiled (`--cold-return`, which the harness never reached before: its first visit always pressed Start); `--stall-lobby 50` is its canary (16 to 17). The screen's rate is the fastest median frame of a step of 30 frames or more: the machine's screen went from 143 Hz to 60 between two boots of one run, so read a run's rates beside its own `the screen's N`. `--angle` lists ANGLE's work on the GPU process's main thread in each step (`D3DCompile` there is a shader compiled at a draw; `MainLinkLoadEvent::wait` and `CompileEvent::wait` its waits for a link and a translation).
- **A boot's exit is filmed with CDP's screencast** (`--film`, `film.mjs`), each frame acknowledged as it comes and timed by the wall's clock, put on the page's by its `performance.timeOrigin`: a trace's screenshots stop at 450 (a first visit's were all of its loading screen), and a browser-wide trace takes none (they're a page target's). A film is a JPEG a frame for the browser to make: time a boot without it. Its flickers are frames that stand out from both sides where those agree; a step from one picture to another (the loading screen gone at once) is none, which is why each boot says when the loading screen was gone: main's plain fade was cut off 0.15 to 0.6 s into its 0.9 by its parts' `transitionend`, bubbling up. Desktop Chrome at a phone's size, its perf overlay shown, filmed no flicker where the owner's phone flickered the loading screen as it left (Android's compositor, not Chrome's on Windows): a phone's own result is the owner's.
- **The loading screen's exit is checked where it draws as the window changes** (`--resize WxH`, `windFit.mjs`): the window goes to that size and back, the first time held till the exit is made ready, and at each of the exit's draws the places its uniforms hold are set against the live boxes. Its uniforms are read by wrapping the WebGL calls of the context that asks for `uHomeAt` (the game's fire has a `uBox` of its own). The window is sized with CDP's `Browser.setWindowBounds` (a real resize, its outer size: 1920x1080 is a 1902x984 page headless), and brought back to the bounds it started with. An exit placed as the lobby was laid out read 384 px off (the canvas) and 106 px (the letters) after five trips. Its places all exact, the owner still saw the logo jump as the particles handed over to it: that is a picture's, measured by the lab's `wind`, which the film (`--film-logo`) only shows.
- **Every wait on the browser has a limit** (`within.mjs`): once Chrome stopped a trace and never sent `tracingComplete`, and a run sat 14 minutes with nothing on screen. A lost trace is played again in rounds.
- **Compare a phase with its own run's away phase**, never with another run's: on a machine shared with other agents, one run's `noFocusFilter` read 7.0 ms, the next run's 13.9 with its away phase at 6.9 in both. `--scenario sweep` starts over the world for that.
- **Read frames a second and the share missed, never a median alone:** #269's sweep read a median of 7.0 ms (every refresh) at 99 frames a second, the owner's 105.
- **Compare variants by the browser's work, over `--rounds`:** frames a second hang on the whole GPU, which the owner's own browsers share (another game's preview open beside it moved one variant from 102 to 124). The browser's work for the page held within a tenth; a variant that gains frames but no work (the glow's fade cut) may be noise. The screen off or locked paces every frame at 60, as does the owner's KVM switched to its other machine (one 60 Hz monitor left): a run whose away phase reads 60 measured at 60, and its work isn't comparable with a run at 144.
- **Read a frame's work before trying variants** (each phase's breakdown, `analyze.mjs` `workIn`): it says which thread pays and for what (the README's Reading it), where variants only say whether a guess was right. Six variants of #269's sweep missed what one breakdown showed: the number of looping layers, not any one loop.
- Chrome is started with `--mute-audio` (the page's sound still runs, its cost measured): a browser a harness starts must never play into the room.
- **Read what each change costs from the report's changes of the page's look** (`analyze.mjs` `looksChanged`: every change the page's scripts made at once, by kind, the costliest first, each with the work in the time after it over the quiet phase's and the share of the frames there that missed). It found what no variant had guessed: the tooltip's comparison taken apart and stacked again on every mouse move (150 `attr class on div.tt-cards` a sweep, 10 to 16 ms of work after each). A change's time runs to the next change, so a fade's cost lands on the small changes after it: read the kinds side by side, and confirm with a variant.
- **`--js` names the page's own work** (`profile.mjs`, from V8's sampler in the same trace, the build unminified and named from its source maps): the trace's tasks only say `FunctionCall`. The lobby's frame was 4.7 to 4.9 ms of JS (three's draws 3.5, their programs' uniforms 1.65 of it, the shadow map 0.8; the warrior's pose 1.0), at 144 Hz a frame's 6.9: the page has about 1 ms a frame for anything else. Two runs' `FunctionCall` read 5.8 ms a frame with the perf overlay shown and 4.7 without, but under `--js`, the overlay shown, the game's frame read 4.7 to 5.0: `FunctionCall` moves run to run, so time the frame by function before believing a cost. Each listed stall names its own JS (`--listed`): a phase's mean hid the hero screen's 200 ms reopening, which its stall named at once (`--scenario heroes`). `--js-under <function>` breaks one function's time down by what it calls, from its innermost call (three draws the post chain's quad by the same function as each object, the scene's pass inside it): WebGPU's lobby spent its 5 ms of objects about 20 µs an object, in three's bindings (1.45 ms), node uniforms (0.87), the render object's lookup (0.43) and the draw (0.70).
- **`--builds` lists a WebGPU boot's node builds by step** (`builds.mjs`; the game records them behind `?builds`, `recordNodeBuilds` in `src/core/webgpu.ts`): how many, those reusing another's shaders and those a draw made, the new shaders and their WGSL, the JS they took, and the kinds with the most new WGSL. Each new shader is a compile of Chrome's on every visit (its cache keeps about 6 MB of a page's), so a step's new shaders are what it waits for. Its sums over the frames (`__webgpuRenders`, `__webgpuFull`, `__webgpuDraws`) start again as a scenario starts (`recordFromNow`), so a `--read` of them is the scenario's frames alone: from the boot, the loading screen's and the lobby's rise read as a steady lobby's. Each record names what sets its material's shaders apart (`features`: its maps, skinned, instanced, colours, sides, blending, alpha test, flat, shadows received): read them raw with `--read "window.__webgpuBuilds"`. Each combination of those is one shader pair.
- **`--before` makes a change on the page just before its end is pictured** (awaited: give it time for a pipeline made beside the frames), and `--read` reads it back after: a WebGPU draw switched one way, a mesh hidden, its picture beside the run without. A draw that reads as right in every buffer and still shows nothing is told apart so: the crypt's obelisks on the meshlets path drew with another batch's uniforms (`src/core/AGENTS.md`). A change whose effect is racy needs two runs a side: one picture of each proved nothing there.
- **A boot's end is a picture of the fight it started** (`out/end-N.png`), in `--view` (V pressed in the fight), each
  biome's by `--biome`: two renderers' or two builds' side by side show what one draws and the other doesn't (the
  WebGPU port's shaders were checked so). Its notes list the page's errors, uncaught and logged, each once with its
  count (`page errors`): three logs a shader that failed to compile and draws on without it. A boot that never
  reaches its lobby fails saying what the loading screen said last and the page's errors (`withErrorsTold`): it
  timed out bare, where the page had shown "Something went wrong while loading" with its cause. `--cast key@ms`
  holds a skill's key that long before the picture (`pictureEnd`), so its look is pictured on each renderer: the
  WebGPU port's Maelstrom drew nothing of its walls, seen so.
- **Its profiles are left behind from a sandboxed shell** (Chrome's files there can't be deleted: the tool says which), so its Chrome fetches nothing in the background: the models, Safe Browsing's lists and the components were 85 MB of a profile's 120. Delete `out/profiles/profile-*` from an unsandboxed shell now and then (each is a measure's own, never used again).
