# Skinning the procedural models (issue #85)

Question: could a GPU auto-rigging and skinning solver like Needle's [Mesh Baker](https://mesh-baker.needle.tools/) Deform mode give our heroes and creatures smooth, continuous bodies? Should we use it, borrow the technique, or skip it?

**Verdict:** don't use Mesh Baker. Borrow the idea in its simplest form: give the heroes skin weights computed from their own geometry when they are built, and only for the parts that cross a joint. Prototype that on the mage's neck and sleeve before committing. Leave the enemies and the IK code as they are.

## 1. Can we use Mesh Baker's solver?

No, as far as I could establish. All of this comes from Needle's public pages; I found nothing else.

- It is a web app. Its [docs](https://engine.needle.tools/docs/products/needle-mesh-baker.html) describe Deform under *Experiments → Show Deformation settings*: pins, IK handles, mirrored edits, pose slots, a Wiggle preview.
- It exports "a skinned GLB with standard joints, weights and pose clips". Other glTF readers can play the clips, but "live IK and secondary motion need Needle's rig runtime".
- It is marked experimental and "needs a compatible WebGPU device". Our renderer is WebGL.
- It is a one-time purchase. I found no npm package, source repository or licence terms for the solver or the rig runtime. Needle's public npm and GitHub presence is the Needle Engine and unrelated tools such as `gpu-animation`.
- The docs don't say how the weights are computed. The issue's guess (voxel binding) matches the "Smooth / Voxel" method choice and voxel resolution setting shown there, but that is an inference.

Even if it were a library, a baked GLB breaks the rule that everything is procedural, and a WebGPU-only solver is out for us. So the question becomes whether we can do the same job ourselves.

## 2. What we have today

- `Sculpt` (`entities/models/shapes.ts`) collects pieces per joint and merges them per (joint, material) into one rigid `Mesh` parented to that joint. Nothing is skinned; there is no `SkinnedMesh` anywhere in `src/`.
- The rig is plain `Object3D` groups (`buildHumanoid`, `rig.ts`). `groundFeet` and `reachArm` are hand-written IK on those joints. The cape colliders are capsules built from the same joints.
- So the joints already are a skeleton: a three.js `Bone` is an `Object3D`, and a `Skeleton` can be built over the existing joint groups without changing how anything animates.
- Everything that bends is a separate rigid piece. At the elbow, knee, shoulder and neck, pieces overlap or open a gap as the pose changes. Sleeves, robes and boots can't stretch across a joint.

## 3. The technique we'd use: analytic weights, no solver

Mesh Baker has to guess a skeleton and weights from an arbitrary mesh. We don't: every piece is generated by our own code, on a known joint, in that joint's local space, and each limb is a tube along a known axis. We can write the weights directly:

- **A piece that doesn't cross a joint** (armour plates, gems, the weapon, hair cards): weight 1 on its own joint, exactly as now.
- **A piece that crosses one** (the sleeve tube over the elbow, the neck over the shoulders and head, the robe over the hips and knees): for each vertex, blend the two joints by the vertex's position along the bone axis, with a smoothstep over a band about one limb radius each side of the joint. This is the classic "linear blend across the joint" weighting that a hand-painted arm would have.
- **Bind pose:** the rest pose from `resetPose`; the bone inverses are the joints' world matrices then.

This costs nothing at load beyond a loop over vertices (a few ms per hero), so there is no need for voxels, a worker or the GPU at build time. Voxel or geodesic binding only pays off when the mesh's shape is unknown, and for us it isn't.

## 4. Cost

Estimated from the code and three.js's behaviour; **nothing here has been measured yet**, which is the point of the prototype.

- **Load time:** negligible, as above. Heroes are built once per class and slot switch.
- **Draw calls:** should go down for a skinned hero. `Sculpt` makes one mesh per (joint, material); a `SkinnedMesh` is one per material for the whole body. Rigid pieces (weapons, pauldrons) stay on their joints, so only the body cloth and skin change.
- **Frame time:** a skinned vertex pays a bone-texture fetch ×4 in the vertex shader, in the colour pass and again in the shadow pass. A hero is a few tens of thousands of vertices and there are only a handful of them (co-op), so this should be small on a desktop GPU; a phone is the case to measure.
- **Shader programs:** each skinned material adds its own variant (`USE_SKINNING`) plus a skinned depth variant for shadows. That is the one real risk to the "0 late compiles" rule. `game/warmup.ts` already renders one of every hero model, so a skinned hero is warmed by the same path, but the count at boot (110 today) will rise and must be checked constant during play, including across class switch, shadow-quality change and first person (`viewModel.ts` moves and re-layers the hero's meshes).
- **Culling:** three culls a `SkinnedMesh` by its bind-pose bounding sphere, so a lunging or death-falling hero can be culled wrongly. Set `frustumCulled = false` on the hero, or bound it generously.
- **Enemies:** a crowd of rigid creatures is cheap today because `Sculpt` caches and shares geometry per kind. Skinned enemies would multiply vertex work by the crowd. Not worth it: creatures are seen small and from above, and their joints are hidden by fur, bark and armour.

## 5. What would change in the code

| Area | Change |
| --- | --- |
| `Sculpt` | An optional skinned mode: keep `skinIndex` / `skinWeight` attributes through `add()`'s attribute filter (it drops everything but position, normal and uv), merge per material across joints (`mergeGeometries` needs every piece to have the same attributes, so rigid pieces get weight 1), and build a `SkinnedMesh` bound to a `Skeleton` of the existing joint groups. The per-kind cache is for creatures and is untouched. |
| Piece helpers | A way to say "this piece spans joints A and B along this axis" when adding it (`limb()` and the robe's `lathe` are the main cases). |
| Hero models | Only `mage.ts` and `warrior.ts`: mark the sleeves, the neck and cowl, the robe skirt, leg armour and boots as spanning pieces. Pauldrons, plates and weapons stay rigid. |
| Cape colliders | No change: they read the same joints. |
| `viewModel.ts` | Needs checking: it hides parts of the hero on a layer the first-person camera doesn't draw, and one skinned body mesh can't be half hidden. The first-person arms would need their own skinned mesh or to stay rigid. |
| Warm-up, quality | Skinned materials warmed by the hero path; `heroDetail` already controls hero vertex counts. |

## 6. Would it replace `groundFeet` or `reachArm`?

No. Skinning only decides how a mesh follows the joints; where the joints go is still ours to solve. The floor contacts, joint limits and IK handles Mesh Baker offers live in its "rig runtime", which we can't get. `groundFeet` and `reachArm` stay. The only overlap: smooth skinning makes a slightly wrong IK pose look better, since the bend no longer opens a gap.

## 7. Recommendation

1. **Skip** Mesh Baker and any solver-based auto-rigging: unavailable at runtime, WebGPU only, and it solves a problem (unknown mesh) we don't have.
2. **Prototype** analytic skin weights on one place, the mage's neck and cowl plus one sleeve, behind a switch, and measure it against the rigid version (follow-up issue):
   - the seam at the elbow and neck across the walk, idle, cast and death poses, in third person and first person;
   - draw calls, frame time on Low and High, and programs (0 late compiles through class switch, quality change and view change);
   - culling during the death fall.
3. **Decide after that** whether to roll it out to the warrior and the rest of the body. Go if the seams visibly close and the program count stays flat; otherwise drop it and note why here.
4. **Not** the enemies.

Not done here: no prototype was built and nothing was measured. The cost section is reasoned from the code, not from a run.
