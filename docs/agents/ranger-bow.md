# The ranger's bow

Part of [`src/entities/models/AGENTS.md`](../../src/entities/models/AGENTS.md), kept here as only work on the bow needs it: the bow itself (`models/bow.ts`) and the ranger's shot as `models/ranger.ts` poses it, from the carry through the draw, the loose and the quiver, in third person and through the eyes, and how each part of it is measured. Read it before changing any of them, and add what you learn to it.

- **The ranger's bow** (`models/bow.ts`, posed in `models/ranger.ts`): a recurve whose limbs bend as its string is drawn, the string keeping its length (each frame the bend that matches it, by bisection: the tips come back and in, along the working part of each limb and not its stiff recurve); loosed, the limbs spring forward past rest and ring out.
- The shot is an archer's, every joint within a body's ranges (`armIK.ts`, the draw arm with its shoulder girdle): side-on to the target, the head turned back to it and the trunk bent at the waist for the shot's angle; the bow arm nearly straight along the arrow's line, which runs from an anchor at the side of the jaw, below and in front of the ear (under the corner of the mouth no arm could reach it as an archer's does: the only one within a body's ranges folded forward across the chest, and reaching it with the elbow behind put the hand through the head), so the draw length is what the arm reaches (0.90 m), the arrow just off the jaw's skin (on it, the arrow's last 9 cm, its fletching, went through the cheek and the beard: measure each arrow against the head's meshes by rays along it, with and without the cap), the string a finger's width off the face and its lower half in front of the coat over the bow shoulder, the bow canted a little to the left (`ANCHOR`, `CANT`: nearer the face and canted right as a traditional bow is, the lower half ran 6 to 8 cm through the coat at every full draw; further left, the upper half met the cheek), the fan anchored 4 cm out from the face (`FAN_OUT`: its string lies across the face, and nearer it went 5 cm into the beard); measure the string against every mesh of the body by rays along each half (the string probe: per frame, the length inside each part, and at full draw its clearance, the largest ring of rays round it that meets nothing, per half), over every scenario; the probe also casts rays along the arrow in the hand (from the quiver to the string and back) against the body but the quiver and that hand, and from the eyes, the nose and the mouth along the gaze against the draw arm (seen from his draw side the hand on the line lies over his face, which is right; before his own eyes it never is); the draw hand's fingers hooked on the string at their last joints, the wrist flat behind the hand at full draw (`HOOK_ROLL`, `DRAW_WRIST`: the hand rocks about the string, across its knuckles, as its wrist needs, eased; held as posed, the wrist bent 25 degrees in), the elbow in line behind the hand along the arrow, level with it (never above the nose: the forearm and upper arm folded flat behind the line, as an archer pulls; `ELBOW_UP`), the anchor low along the jaw (`ANCHOR`: at the jaw's corner the draw shoulder lay 22 cm under the arrow's line and the upper arm climbed 47 degrees to an elbow over the head, a pose with no pull in it) and the draw shoulder raised towards the line and drawn back as the string comes back (`DRAW_GIRDLE`, the solver's `girdleSet`; eased back after the loose, `GIRDLE_BACK`: let go at once, the elbow jumped 38 cm in a frame). Measure the full draw against an archer's photo from the side: the upper arm's rise, the elbow against the hand's height, and the forearm's line against the arrow's from above. The hand is rolled on the string as it comes back (`HOOK_TILT`, about the fingers' line: palm to the face and thumb up, the forearm's turn is at its end before the elbow comes in line, and its elbow stood 15 cm over the line and 40 degrees out behind it), and the shoulder is held where it is while an upright bow is drawn (the girdle drawing it back took the elbow round behind the neck; the fan keeps it, and a girdle no arm asks for goes back to rest: taken up again from where it was left, the elbow flipped across the chest at the loose).
- The draw goes as an archer's does: from the carry the bow comes in before the chest quicker than the body turns, the draw hand takes the string there (its way up from the hip bowing out in front of the chest: straight, it cut through the belly; riding its forearm until its elbow is up and out, then turning onto the string, `ArmGoal.ease`: turned onto it with the elbow low in front, the wrist met both its ends and the elbow jumped up), and only then does the bow come up and the string come back (drawn as the hand came for it, the string met the hand at the throat and the elbow went up over the head to come round), evenly over the draw (`drawnAt`: by the bow's own pull, half the string came back in a few frames), so quickly that a tap's bow is on the line by its least draw. The chest opens towards the target as the draw starts and closes as the string comes back (`OPEN`; loosed, it opens again for the next arrow from where it was, and between shots it stays as it was, its opening fading with the body's turn off the shot, never quicker than `CLOSE_V`: set closed at once, the trunk turned 0.2 rad in a frame at a tap's loose and 0.5 rad as a shot ended; measure each trunk joint's turn a frame); the draw elbow goes from out to the right at the shoulder's height round to in line behind the hand along the arrow (`SET_POLE`, the full draw's pole), as far as the string has come back, swinging round into the anchor at a capped speed (`ArmGoal.maxMove`, past it only as far as the ranges need, `REACH_SLACK`: held back, an elbow swinging up over the shoulder for the quiver took the shoulder 200 degrees past its range; capped likewise to the quiver and back, `FETCH_V`, `RELOAD_V`: free, it whipped round 23 cm in a frame there and flipped round its circle at a walking shot's follow-through; in line behind the hand all the way, it stood straight up over the head; sent forward while the string came back, it jumped round behind as the hand reached the anchor). The hand on the string is turned as on the line throughout (following the bow pitched down on its way up, the fingers pointed 40 degrees down and the wrist rose into the cheek).
- Loosed, the hand follows through a little back and out from the neck, level (the shoulder is under the anchor: back or down towards it, the elbow folded past its range), the bow tips forward in the loose fist only out on the line (tipped as it came in, its lower limb swung back into the body), and the hand goes up over the right shoulder to the quiver (slung from that shoulder to the left hip, its mouth over the shoulder: leaning in, its mouth was behind the head and the reach went 113 degrees past the range), draws the arrow up out of it beside the head and on up and forward to the arm's near full stretch over the shoulder (`RISE`, `PULL`: the arrows are as long as the draw, 0.96 m, most of a metre deep in the quiver; drawn out only to above the shoulder, it came forward through the shoulder and the neck) and brings it down in front of the chest to the string, turned over the top onto it (the head, turned to the target, is between the quiver and the bow: straight from one to the other the arrow went over the head and the arm through it; turned straight round, half a turn, it swept through the head), in one sweep timed by its path. Only what is out of the quiver shows (`arrowPieces`: the nock and vanes, a shaft scaled to the length that's out, the head once it's clear): drawn whole, pivoting at the quiver's mouth, its head swung out through the quiver's side into the hips. On its way up from the side the hand passes out to the side at the chest's height (`SIDE_UP`: straight up from the hip, the elbow folded shut and the forearm went through the chest), and the waypoint over the quiver stays in the shoulder's plane (behind it, the arm rose in a plane 57 degrees behind the body).
- The shot is an archer's pace, about a second to draw and to reload (`draw`, `castTime`: in 0.7 and 0.45 s it was a blur; the damage per arrow rose to keep the damage a second). The next arrow is nocked with the bow brought in before the chest, nearly upright, its arrow tipped a little down (`NOCK_AT`, `SET_TIP`: out along the shot, the nock was 37 cm across the body and the arm reached straight out across the face; tipped further down, the top limb leant back into the chest; canted 0.85 rad, side-on to the shot as the body is, its lower limb and its string swung back through the coat's left side, half the frames of a run of taps). Off the string the hand rides its forearm, its turn eased onto the string's on the forearm whichever the elbow (eased towards the hanging arm's turn in the world, half a turn off a raised one's, the forearm flipped from one end of its turn to the other; towards last frame's forearm, the wrist moved with it and the arm shook).
- Between shots the bow is carried in the left fist at the side with an arrow on the string, and stood a while without a shot he puts it back (`AnimState.nocked`; the bow comes up before the chest first, `STOW_UP`, and the hand takes the arrow off its string, lifts it over the shoulder and lets it down into the quiver from above its top: reached for at the side, the bow swung back with the walk and the hand went behind the back for it, 49 degrees past the shoulder's range); the next shot takes one out first, the bow brought in for it as for the next arrow (`AnimState`'s action `fetch`).
- The bow is carried in a neutral carry, as an archer walks with one: the arm hanging straight down, the bow level in the fist with its top limb forward (the grip square to the forearm, as a hanging fist holds it), and the nocked arrow pointing down at the ground beside the foot, a little out and forward (`CARRY_ARROW`, `CARRY_TOP`), the draw hand free; the bow keeps near level as the arm swings in a walk, following a third of its swing (`CARRY_FOLLOW`: all of it stood the bow upright at each step), and on its way to the shot it swings out past the thigh, turned about its length halfway (`CARRY_SWING_TURN`). The string lies inside the forearm, so the bow arm's elbow is on the side of the bow it's on at the shot: the fist holds the grip nearly square to the forearm, so the string can't pass round the arm's end, and an elbow that changes sides of the bow's plane takes the forearm through the string (carried upright beside the leg with the elbow bent, as it was, it went 12 cm into the bracer on three frames of every draw, and that carry would tire a real arm; turned straight from the level carry to the upright bow before the chest, the elbow crossed the bow's plane halfway and back). Measure the carry with rays along the string, the limbs and the nocked arrow against the body's meshes, skinned into the world each frame (the crossings of each, not only what lies between them: the coat's skirt is one sheet, the bracer an open tube), standing, walking, drawing from the carry and putting the arrow back, with an arrow on the string (shoot once first), and the forearm's least distance from the string. The coat's skirt is draped behind the string, the lower limb and the nocked arrow, and the bow arm is swung out at the shoulder just enough to keep the string, the nocked arrow and the lower limb off the thigh and the cloth over it (`stringOffThigh`, `armOffThigh` by points along each a few cm apart; by the string's alone, once each arm swung against its own leg the arrow went through the thigh and into the coat's hem once a stride, and the lower limb across the thigh setting off: the drape put the cloth in behind the string and the thigh put it out again, and the string ran through the coat's side by up to 15 cm on most frames of a walk; with its points 13 cm apart, each lay clear while the string between them didn't); `npm run lab -- carry --frames` lists every frame with a clip. The bow goes where the fist has it, turned as far as the wrist and forearm turned the hand (placed first and the hand fitted to it, it turned in a fist that couldn't follow).
- A fan (Briarflight) is shot with the bow laid flat, its top to the left and the arrows side by side in its window (to the right, the forearm turned palm up 29 degrees past its range), anchored a little out from the face (its string lies across, hooked palm down).
- Through the eyes (`FP_*`) the bow is posed in the view's own frame: at full draw its grip is held still below the crosshair and the arrow lies along the shot's own line through it (`toEyes` in `viewModel.ts`: the model is placed in view after the pose, so the line is turned into the eyes' frame and back), the bow's top tipped left, clear of the view's middle; a fan's flat bow just right of it, its arrows side by side along the line, the layout eased between the two (`fanK`, the string's held stretch with it). The line is low enough in the view that the nock, and the draw hand on it, stay below the frame from nocking to the loose, as a real archer never sees his draw hand: half in view at the nock, the hand popped in from below and out past the camera at every shot, up to 170 px a frame (anchored at fixed points of the view, the arrow ran 35 degrees left of the shot and the hand covered a third of the middle). A volley lofted over an area (Hailfletch) aims up through the eyes: the whole bow pose tipped up about them, as the bow arm raises it, eased in and out (`FP_LOB`; at its own 26 degrees under the view's limit, it read as a shot straight ahead, and with the grip moved up and left instead, the forearm turned half its range off the fist and the wrist bent 60 degrees back), and a shot cut off eases its angle back level (`PITCH_BACK`). Through the eyes the bow fist turns as posed, and its elbow is chosen to line the forearm up behind it (`FP_WRIST`, the solver's `wristW`: left to the ranges alone, the wrist bent 45 to 66 degrees back in every shot and the hand looked broken off; measure the bow wrist's bend frame by frame through the eyes). The arrow in view points only 0.08 to 0.4 rad above the view's axis (`FP_PITCH`): looking down at the floor or up at the sky the shot still goes level at chest height, and along it the bow tipped over. Between shots the bow is carried low on the left, nearly upright, the fist round its grip in view; the next arrow comes up from below (the quiver is behind the eyes), already along the line. Walking, the bow sways about its grip with the stride (`FP_SWAY`: across once a stride, up and down with each step, rolling a little; eased in and out with the pace, a third of it while drawn): held still in the view, it read as stuck to it. The bow arm's solve glides (`glide` in `armIK.ts`): its fist's turn about the grip and its slant on it were picked each frame from a few, and as the walk moved the elbow the carried bow and the fist jumped between two or three places, 6 to 13 px in a frame (watch the solver's choices per frame against the jumps on screen). Measure with a first-person film through the game's own camera on a fixed clock, every frame, over taps, held shots, fans, alternating shots, mouse look, walking and Hailfletch: each hand's, the grip's and the arrows' move on screen a frame while in view, its jerks (a sudden change of speed) and how often each comes into or goes out of view; where the arrow on the string points (its vanishing point) against the shot in flight; and the share of the view and of its middle the hero and the draw arm cover (the frame drawn again without them).
- The bow itself is built to be looked at from a foot away (`models/bow.ts`): limbs of a lacquered wood with a fine straight grain (the `bowWood` maps, neutral, tinted per vertex: pale sapwood on the back, heartwood on the belly; the limb needs its uvs, without which a map shows one texel), horn tips with the string's groove, a gilt vine inlaid down each limb's belly (the face the archer sees) and bent with it, a walnut riser with brass rings and a brass leaf on its face, sinew bindings, a leather grip in a spiral of thong below a shelf, and above the shelf a sight window cut past the riser's middle (`WINDOW`, `riserGeometry`: the riser no further right than the arrow's side from the shelf up, its rings level, so the shelf is flat; the arrow lies at x 0 on the shelf's leather pad beside a strip of leather on the window's face: through a round riser it ran 3 to 5 cm through the riser and the grip's brass collar at every shot, and with rings square to the riser's curve, which leans away above the grip, the shelf's edge stood 4 mm up into the arrow). The window is on the riser's right, as the bow is canted left in every view, so the arrow lies against the riser and the window faces up, to the eyes (on the left it faced down and away, and through the eyes the riser leaning over it hid the arrow, which seemed to go in behind it).
- A fan's five arrows lie side by side in the window, all on its face at one height (`FAN_PITCH` apart, the window tall enough for them), nocked as far apart along a stretch of string the fingers hold straight (`Bow.hold`, `fanHold`: the nocking point its middle, and the shot's line through the middle arrow), so they lie parallel and fan out only in flight (fanned out from one nock across the flat bow, each lay on what it crossed, a limb, the riser or the fist, 2 to 5 cm above or below the next, and the varying heights read as a mess). A fan takes what it lacks from the quiver (`Player.nocked` is a count: a fan after a shot fetches the other four), the hand setting off from where it was, as `drawHand` last placed it (on the string for a fan queued behind a shot: set off from the hanging arm, it jumped 137 px in a frame; its string point, not its wrist, when it was on the string: a fan queued behind a fan jumped 15 cm), and its arrows come clustered round the hand and spread into their row as they reach the string; carried, an arrow turns over the top swung out to the right (`TURN_OUT`: turned straight up, its back end swept through the side of the head), and stowed, it turns up as the hand rises and down into the quiver only as the hand comes back over it (pointed at the mouth from up and forward, it went through the raised arm). The hand's ways to and from the quiver are smooth through their waypoints (`through`, a Hermite curve with tangents in time: the elbow changed speed at each waypoint). The bow hand may slant the grip further one way than the other (`ArmGoal.slant` as `[lo, hi]`, `BOW_SLANT`: a held Hailfletch shot brought the fist into the arrow), and through the eyes it turns as posed whatever the wrist's range (`ArmGoal.free`: stopped at the wrist's end, the fist turned the bow with the elbow, 40 px at a fan's start). A string let back without a shot (a Hailfletch volley's draw lowered between angles) eases down over `LET_DOWN` (followed at once, the arrow jumped in the view), and the hand rolls on the string late in the draw (`TILT_POW`).
- Test in the lab first (`tools/lab`: scenarios run in the page on its fixed clock with nothing drawn and the view glide and intro skipped, measured each frame: clips by ray crossings, the carried arrows against the body, screen moves through the eyes, each frame's ranges, the hand and elbow in the chest's frame; planted faults prove each measure catches what it should), the films only for what it can't see: sixteen scenarios in under two minutes, where the films took half an hour. Measure with rays along each nocked arrow (`ARROWCLIP` in the bow film: points on its shaft near the riser, each tested inside each part of the bow and the bow hand by ray parity), every frame of every scenario through the eyes and not; a linen string with a dark serving, a brass nocking bead and wool silencers; its arrows have a leaf-shaped broadhead on a socket, shield-cut feathers and a horn nock, bound with thread (their material double-sided for the feathers).
- The pose's arrow follows the body's facing with the shot's small offset eased in (onto a new target it swung 31° in a frame), and is aimed for the arrow as it would leave (before the string moved its would-be speed was nothing and the pose aimed it 34° up). Keep each stance foot on its own side of the pelvis's middle as the leg IK sees it (that turn is the pose's, not the IK's: a spot across it was moved back out each time and the foot stepped again and again, the leg shaking).
- Measure with the bow probe (a fixed clock, scripted input, the mouse put over the target: the touch layer clears a scripted `input.aim` every frame; its holds long enough for the fetch and the full draw): at full draw the arrow against the shot, the draw length against the body's, both elbows, the draw forearm against the arrow from above and the side, the elbow's height over the arrow's line, both wrists, the nock against the mouth, the head and the shoulders' line against the arrow, the string's length and its clearance of the bow arm, the arrow's head past the riser's back, how much each ankle and knee moves a frame; over every scenario (standing, walking, strafing, running shots, taps, held shots, the fan) the bow's limbs inside the body's capsules by part (and on which frames), the hands' moves a frame against the body, and each arrow's launch against the nocked arrow. Measure the arms with the range probe, and trace an arm frame by frame through a shot (its shoulder's raise, plane and turn, the elbow, the forearm's turn, the wrist, where the hand and elbow are in the chest's frame, its depth in the body): the elbow jumping round its circle between frames shows there and nowhere else.
- What is still off: for a few frames as the string comes back to the face the hand passes in front of the eyes, the wrist at its ends, and the shoulders' line lies about 12 degrees off the arrow.
