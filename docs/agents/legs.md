# The legs

Part of the gotchas in [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), kept here as only work on the legs needs it: the leg IK (`models/ik.ts`) that places every walker's feet, a walk and a run as a person's, going round, stops, steps by the dais's edges, the body and arms swinging with the legs (`walkCycle` in `models/rig.ts`), the stance a pose asks for, and the head's look-at. Read it before changing any of them, measure with `npm run lab -- gait`, `stops`, `feet` and `form` against `main`, and add what you learn to it.

- **Leg IK and head look-at** (`models/ik.ts`; `?ik=0` turns both off and restores the walk cycle's own legs and gait for A/B): `LegIK` runs at the end of a model's `animate` (heroes and every walking enemy, not the floating witch) and takes the legs over. A planted foot is held at a world spot while the body moves over it; a swinging foot arcs to where it will land (predicted from the body's speed), timed by the walk cycle's phase, which `gaitRate` keeps in step with the speed and the leg length (`Player.animateBody`, `Enemy.gait`: a step per half cycle, so the stride matches the ground covered), each foot swinging through as the cycle's own thigh on its side comes forward (`legU`: the left at phase 0), so the arms and the hips' and chest's turn that `walkCycle` swings by the same phase go against the legs, each arm forward with the other leg, as a person's (with the feet half a cycle the other way every walker paced, an arm swinging forward with the leg on its side; check each hand's fore-aft position in the hips' frame against each ankle's over a walk: about -1 with its own side); a standing character re-steps when its feet fall too far from under it; the pelvis (`body.position.y`) drops when a foot would be out of reach, a striding foot counted no further ahead of its hip than it lands nor behind it (`airReach`: its leg eases into its stretch as it trails off the ground and as it reaches out before pawing back to land; sunk for those, the hips came down 7 to 9 cm in each flight of a run and every foot landed under them with its knee bent 45 degrees), and a runner's stride not at all while it is in the air (it reaches out all but straight and the hips come down after it lands: sunk for its reach as it came down, they fell 5 cm in the flight's last 3 frames and the knee landed bent 36 degrees, a runner's 20); a foot lies level on `groundHeight` (dais steps), on the highest level under its heel, its ankle or its ball, the rest of it out over a lower level as on a stair, and on the level it was put down on until it steps again (`under`: by the level under the ankle alone, a foot by the dais's edge stood on the step below with its toes or heel up to 25 cm into the riser, and re-read every frame, a foot put down with its ball on the edge dropped a level and sank into the dais); standing, a foot's spot by an edge moves to the nearest place where its ball is well on the level the body stands on and nothing of it is over a higher one, else to the nearest where it rests with its toes clear of a riser, each place put on its own side of the pelvis's middle as a step lands it (`onBodyLevel`: a person by a ledge keeps a foot on it rather than reaching down the step; slid along itself, its spot jumped 20 cm as its ball crossed the edge, or lay out of reach down the step, and drawn straight in, a step landed it back out on its side, and each time the foot stepped for its spot again and again), and a standing foot out of reach steps only to somewhere else (`NEAR_STEP`: stepping to where it was, it landed out of reach again as the pelvis had risen while it was up, and beat up and down five times a second on the dais's step at full draw). Measure feet in the lab (`feet`: its slides are the slip of the sole's point on the ground that moved least, frame by frame, as rolling over the heel or toes and turning on the ball keep one point still: by the ankle's move, a turn on the ball read as 7 cm of slide), standing at full draw round the dais's edges at many spots and bearings and walking across them: per frame each sole's length in a riser (over a higher level than the foot stands on), how far it dips under its own level, and each foot's moves (a foot that never stays still for four frames is one long step to a step counter); each leg is two-bone IK (`reachArm` with `hinge = -1`), blended over the pose by a smoothed weight.
- `rig.groundFeet` is leg IK run after the pose: a crouch bends the knees instead of sinking the feet, and a planted foot lies flat, on the ground under it, level in the world and heading the way it points (`layFlat`: levelled by cancelling the knee's pitch alone, a foot turned against its shin kept part of the shin's roll as a pitch, its toe 1 to 3.3 cm into the floor) (`groundHeight`: measured from the root, a foot planted on the dais's step was in the air); where the shin leans further over a foot on the floor than an ankle bends, the heel lifts, the foot pivoting on its toes, which stay where they lay.
- **Slowed, the body walks**: a hero drawing a shot moves at 0.45 of his run, 2.8 to 3.2 m/s, and jogging there, more than a third of the time in the air with his legs dangling under a body that glided on, he read as hung from a string; so up to `WALK_TOP` (3.4 m/s for a man's legs, in proportion for others) the gait is a walk and from `RUN_FROM` a run (`runShare`): a walk's share of the cycle on the ground is above a half (`dutyAt`), its step at most a leg long (`WALK_STEP`), its stance may take the body over more ground than a run's (`WALK_STANCE`), it lands about half its stance ahead (`WALK_AHEAD`), and the pelvis sinks for the stance it plans before a foot is out of reach (`plannedDrop`: a stalking walk's bent knees). At a walk a foot is always down: one waits for the other to land, on its toes if it must (`WALK_SUPPORT`), unless it is dragged (the leg falls short of it, measured once the leg is solved) or its leg is solved past a range (`pastInStance`: the hip's extension, its crossing in, the ankle's bend), when it must go, early if it must (`leftBehind`). A stride goes on with the cycle and at least at the pace planned as it left (`SWING_MOST`: following the cycle alone, a swing begun before a reversal hung in the air a second as the cycle stalled), an early one takes a whole swing's time and lands early (stretched to land at its window's end it took up to 1.8 swings, landed late and went early again), no foot leaves while the other has only just left (`HOP_GAP`), nor, unless it is dragged, within a quarter of a cycle of it (`GALLOP_GAP`, `justLeft`, the out-of-reach re-step too: a turn or a set-off left a foot behind a few frames after the other went, and the legs swung together), a planted foot moving re-steps on its own only further behind than its stride would start early (`farBehind`: stepped first, at a run a foot pushing off left as a quick step, out of turn), the first from standing is quick (`FIRST_STRIDE`) but only the first (a run's second, leaving with the first in the air, takes its whole swing: as quick, both landed 6 frames apart and left again so), the cycle sets off from a foot's mid-swing (`LegIK.setOff`, which `Player` and `Enemy` call before moving the phase on: from wherever a stop had left it, a first foot at its window's start landed early in it and went again, or the second foot's window came round 6 frames after the first, and both legs swung together through the first strides, a gallop at every set-off), the foot behind is held to a run's push as the body sets off for one (`runPush` by the speed it is getting up to), and the cycle's length is read from how fast its phase moves either way (`rate`: the signed rate passed through nothing as a walk turned from forwards to backwards). Nothing turns or sinks faster than a body does: the body onto the way it goes (`TURN_MOST` in `player.ts`: half round in four frames at a reversal), the pelvis in the world (`PELVIS_TURN_MOST`, the trunk taking the rest), the hips for the legs' reach (`DROP_RATE`). Measure with `npm run lab -- gait` against `main`: each foot's time on the ground against the plan, both and neither, hops, and the film.
- **Going round in circles** (WASD rolled round, a lap in 1.6 s: drawn, a circle 0.7 m across its radius) the feet danced, both up a third of the time drawn and over half running, for several reasons. The walk cycle turned round (backpedalling) whenever the body went more than about 100 degrees off its aim, twice a lap, and each turn brought a foot just down due again at once: it now turns round only at a foot's mid-swing, a whole number of half cycles (`cycleOn` in `player.ts`), where the swinging foot's window goes back the way it came and the other's is half a cycle off either way. A landing goes along the curve the body is on (`curve`: how much the way it goes turns a metre, smoothed, none through a reversal's flip or mid-reversal, `CURVE_*`), the pelvis turns towards where the way it goes will be (`CURVE_LEAD`: after it, it lagged 40 to 60 degrees and the feet landed out to the side), and late in a swing the landing settles where it is (`LAND_SETTLE`: following the body's way to the last frame, the foot landed moving sideways and stopped dead). A stride is aimed afresh only when the body's way turns quicker than the stride turns after it, and not in its last 15% (`REAIM_UNTIL`: against the way it went as it began, going round re-aimed every swing near its end). Wheeling round on its feet counts the facing's turn off the curve (running round, the facing turns with it, and the feet stepped out of turn). A foot past its window goes late (`LATE_SHARE`: counted against the window to come alone, the feet fell a quarter of a window behind the cycle and never caught up, every stride early or a re-step landing under the hip), but only one down since before its window began (`downAll`: one that landed in its window near its end went again six frames later, and its stride, squeezed into what was left, broke the rhythm). A walk lifts a foot only just clear of the ground (`WALK_LIFT`: lifted as a jog's, 15 cm, its knee folded up behind and it skipped), its step fits its stance (`WALK_STEP` 0.9: a leg long, the stance was more ground than the legs reach), a waiting foot takes a whole swing (squeezed into what was left of its window, it landed short), a planted foot behind its hip is reached with its heel up before the hips sink (`reachedAt`: sunk for it flat, they fell 12 cm at each stance's end), and a planted hip opened past its range lets the foot go (`pastInStance`). Check with `gait --scenarios circle,circleDrawn --trace`.
- **Stopping**: the game tells the legs where the body is going (`LegIK.intend`: the velocity it eases towards and how quickly, from `Player.handleInput`), so a stop is known from the frame the keys are let go, as a person who decides to stop shortens the stride under way (read from the body's own slowing, which a co-op copy still does, it was known only once the stride was all but down). A stopping body's landing is aimed where the body will be as it slows (`slowingOver`, `easedOver`), never past where the foot will stand once it rests (`landWhereItRests`, `spotAtRest`: its stance's spot round the rest point, on the level the body rests on; aimed by the speed alone, the last foot landed 40 to 60 cm past where the body stopped and stepped back under it a quarter second later, and by the dais's edge it landed down the step and stepped up a second later), and a little short of it is drawn onto it (`REST_PULL`); the stride lands on that landing as far from the hip as it is now, its stroke as slow as the body will be (`landOff`, `strokeK`: aimed at where the hip would be, with the hips coming back over the body as its lean came off, it landed 15 to 20 cm past), turned out as it will stand (`landingYaw`). A planted foot already where it will stand takes no stride, even out of reach for a moment (`standsWhereItRests`); one too far from it brings itself up as the body brakes (`closesStop`, at a first stride's pace: left till the body stood, its step was still in the air half a second later); a foot the run had just put down a little ahead or behind is kept there (`keepSplit`, `SPLIT`: a person stops with a foot forward), turned as it lies (`splitTurn`: turned to its stance, it stepped in place a moment after the body stood), till the pose moves its spot; braking, a foot down stays down, on its toes if it must, until the other lands (`braking` in `walkFoot`, a run's push held while it brakes: let go as at a run, the foot behind pushed off into the air with the other still coming down, and both landed together, a hop at nearly every stop); a stride the body stops under finishes in the time its swing had left, and one in the air as the body is brought to a stop lands while there is speed to brake (`stopLandIn`, `STOP_LAND`: a run's by the time the body is down to a fifth of its speed, a walk's as it stands; at a runner's swing, half a second, the last foot came down after the body was still, and with the other pushing off as the keys were let go both were up 12 frames as the body braked from 5 m/s), and with both feet in the air the later lands a braking step after the other (`BRAKE_STEP`: each hurried alone, a stop let go in a run's flight came down on both feet in the same frame). The trunk leans back against the braking, as far as the stop is from a run's speed and fading as the momentum goes (`PUSH_LEAN`, `BRAKE_SPEED`: by its speed alone it stayed leaning forward 10 degrees until it had stopped, over feet braking it ahead of it), and is upright again quickly once still. Standing, a foot steps only once the other is down, even one out of reach (stepped while the other was still in the air, both were up together for 13 frames as he stopped); by an edge a standing foot may come within `EDGE_GAP` of the pelvis's middle to keep to the body's level (kept `GAP` out, no place on the dais fitted the outer foot until the body stood 12 cm inside its edge); wheeling round on its feet, a standing body steps quicker and a step in the air comes down at once (`WHEEL_STEP`); moving, a step taken on the move comes down quickly too while the planted leg is solved past a range (`STUCK_HURRY`: at its own pace, a re-step on a sharp turn while tapping shots held the ranger's other hip crossed in 12 degrees past its range for 8 frames; not a standing step the body sets off under, which brought down a frame sooner put his feet a third of a cycle apart through a whole run, a gallop's rhythm that the strides' timing never pulls back: the feet are timed off the cycle's windows, and a foot a third of a cycle late leaves at its window's end, lands early in the next and waits out the other's gallop gap). Measure with `npm run lab -- stops` against `main` (every hero), then `gait`, `carry` (a draw begun half a second after a stop wheels round to shoot behind) and `range`.
- **The body swings with its legs**, not the walk cycle's clock: each arm swings against its own thigh as the leg IK last placed it, by the thighs' swing apart (`thighForward`; crouched, both point forward), at most as far as the cycle swings it, and the hips' turn and sway and the rise and fall follow where the feet are in their strides (`bodyPhase`, read by `walkCycle` through `root.userData.legs`, every walker's). By the clock, a foot taken off the cycle (held for the other, early for its reach, two strides in one window as a set-off's quickening cycle lands one early) left the arms swinging with the leg on their own side as often as not and the hips swaying out over the foot in the air; making the feet keep to the cycle instead (the cycle following the feet, one stride a window, early strides stretched back onto it) helped one scenario and broke another each time. Measure with `gait`'s hands against knees by half second (a person's -1) and its rhythm, and the carry after: the bow arm swings with it.
- The gait follows a real runner's (`stepLength`, `dutyAt`: a run's steps a second and each foot's time on the ground by speed, at a man's size, from a runner's, `RUN_CADENCE` and `RUN_CONTACT` after Dorn, Schache and Pandy 2012, about 3 steps a second at 6 m/s, each foot down a sixth of a second and a step nearly 2 m: by a formula capped at 1.65 leg lengths a step, a hero at 6 m/s took 3.9 steps a second and his legs churned; a walk keeps its own step, by the runner's cadence a walk at 1.4 m/s took 2.65 steps a second). At a run a foot behind its hip stays down on its toes until its heel is as high as a run's push off lifts it (`pushingOff`, `pushReach`, `runPush`: taken up as soon as its heel lifted, it left two thirds of the way through its stance with the knee bent under the hips, no push off at all), the foot rolling up of its own only part of the way (`stanceRoll`: rolled up to the whole push, the knee was bent 50 degrees as it left), so the leg is near straight behind as it goes; a walk's goes as soon as it is out of reach (held to a run's push, its hip went 18 degrees past its extension). A run's foot in the air is pitched from its shin as a runner's (`RUN_TOE_*`, `shinBack`: pointed down behind it as it leaves, square to it by mid-swing, then handed back to its pitch in the world to land), set before the swing clears the ground, from last frame's shin, so the clearance knows where the toes are, and corrected by this frame's once the leg is solved (`shinShare`): pitched in the world, under a knee folded up behind, it was bent up against the shin as far as an ankle goes in the air all through the swing, and turned on the shin after the clearance, its toes went into the floor and the ankle twisted 14 degrees past its range. A run's swing reaches back less as it leaves (`RUN_STROKE_OUT`) and lifts the foot less (`runLift`): with the toes pointed, the thigh was driven up to 76 degrees mid-swing. Going the way the pelvis faces, it follows a runner's thigh and knee through the swing at the body's own lengths (`RUN_SWING_*`, `runnersSwing`: the ankle where those angles put it in the hip's frame, its ends moved onto where the foot left and lands), the heel coming up behind the hips, the knee folded past 110, before the thigh drives through (lifted a fifth of a metre and swung by the stance's stroke alone, the foot trailed low behind the hips and the knee folded to 97 where a runner's goes to 114); backing up, strafing or braking it keeps the stroke's (a heel kicked up behind would be ahead or out to the side). Moving, no step moves a foot's target faster than a running swing (`FOOT_V`, `FOOT_VK`): when the plan jumps (the phase or the way the body goes turning round as an attack turns the body onto its aim while it strafes or backs up) the foot catches up over a few frames instead of teleporting, a swing whose window was already under way when the foot could leave runs its whole arc in the time left (`tw0`), and a landing is never predicted more than 0.6s ahead. Don't fix such jumps by turning swings into timed steps: the feet shuffle. Check with per-frame ankle jumps in a real run (attacking while strafing) and the gait's state shares against main's. Keep the swing's landing offset (`half`, capped at 0.4 of the leg, `SLIP` is 0: a planted foot never creeps) and the duty within the legs' reach: at speed the contact shortens so the body passes over at most `STANCE` leg lengths of ground, as a runner's does. A planted foot rolls instead of sliding (`roll`: the heel stays put as it lands, the toes as the heel peels up, the ankle moving only round them), turns about the ball of the foot (`BALL`; about its heel when the ball is out over a lower level: turned about a ball in the air past a step's edge, the rest of the sole skidded 7 cm over the step), and when the body goes out of its reach it keeps its spot and steps at once (`over`), never dragged. A swing starts and lands where the ankle actually is, carrying its pitch on, its first frame already under way, and a foot in the air eases into the leg's full stretch rather than meeting it. Measure leg quality with the deterministic probe (`legdet`: a fixed 1/60s clock and scripted input; contact-point slide, ankle and knee acceleration, crossed legs) after restarting Vite, against main and the duty within the legs' reach, or the pelvis dips at each step.
- **A foot in the air clears what is under it and ahead of it** (`clearStep`, once its speed is capped: each point of its sole as the foot is tipped, hanging from its shin, a margin over the highest level under it and rising at a slope towards a higher one ahead, eased down once past and down onto the level it lands on as it lands; swung between the levels it left and lands on alone, it went through the dais step's riser up to 17 cm deep), and a moving foot's landing is moved along its stride until its toes are over no higher level than the rest of its sole (`offRiser`, the same way for the whole stride; late in a swing tested where the foot is, which the leg's reach and the pelvis's side keep off the gait's prediction).
- **A swing is drawn in the hip's frame, not the world's** (a cubic from where the foot left to `half` ahead, its tangents the stance's backward stroke: `STROKE_OUT` / `STROKE_IN`): a foot eased along in the world trails behind the hip, then rushes ahead of it and dangles under a squatting pelvis. Judge a gait by its joint-angle curves over the cycle against a runner's (hip and knee flexion against the phase from touchdown, from joint recordings), not by the pelvis height: an upright torso over legs that are flexed all the way round reads as a squat at a pelvis height that looks fine.
- A body that travels sideways to the way it faces (a hero casting or attacking, where the body faces the aim) turns its pelvis and legs towards the way it goes and turns its chest back (`twist`, `TWIST`), or its feet cross. Moving in any direction while attacking, the legs keep one coherent left, right rhythm (count it: the same foot leaving twice running is a break), crossing a little as a person's do: moving, no extra corrective step is ever taken (it would be a double step); a planted foot the body is about to leave out of reach takes its own stride early instead, stretched to land on time (`EARLY`), even with the other foot in the air, from as soon as its last window has ended (`toNextWindow`: counted against the window behind it until the phase wrapped round, a foot planted just after its window, as at a reversal, stood out of reach on its toes for half its stance, 9 to 13 frames strafing drawn up and down the dais's steps, the pelvis sinking 10 to 17 cm onto it, then let go: a foot caught on a nail), and a foot the body has left out of reach in its window goes at once, not once it has settled (the first step from standing lands behind a body already on its way); a stride's progress only goes on, by however far the cycle moves either way (a reversal turns the phase back: following it the foot swung back the way it came), and when the way the body goes turns round under it, the same stride is aimed afresh from where the foot is, turned round in a quick swing's time at least (`reaimStride`: in what its old swing had left, 6 frames at a run's reversal, it came down under the hip and was left behind 6 frames later, with the other only just up); a planted foot out of reach rises onto its toes (`peel`) rather than sliding; only a body wheeling round on its feet (`yawRate`, smoothed so an attack's snap onto its aim doesn't count) steps out of turn. Between one attack and the next the body stays on its aim (`AIM_HOLD` in `player.ts`): wheeling round to the way it goes and back at every blow made the legs dance.
- The pelvis turns as far as the trunk lets it (`trunkTurns`: the pose's own twist of the chest on the hips and the IK's together within `TRUNK`; with `?rom=0`, at most `TWIST`, 0.8 rad): with a pose that doesn't twist the chest that is ±0.82 rad, and an archer's side-on shot lets his hips turn round to his side, as an archer walks along his side-on line (held to 0.8 rad of the aim, strafing drawn, his legs went 42 degrees off the pelvis's line, each stride cut short and pushed out wide at its middle); the legs walk along its line, forwards or backwards: going back and to the side it turns the other way and backpedals (never steps sideways backwards across itself), each way costed by how far the legs go off its line plus how far it turns (`TURN_COST`, `HYST`), within the range the pose's own twist of the chest leaves it followed slowly (`POSE_TWIST_FOLLOW`: by each frame's, an attack's blows twisting the chest one way and the other turned the legs from forwards to backwards and back along a strafe every few blows, the pelvis swinging 130 degrees each time and the feet galloping across it), but first chosen as the body gets going with the pose as it is then (followed slowly from standing too, an archer's side-on draw begun with a strafe walked it forwards and turned round to backpedal half a second on, mid-stride: the foot behind was dragged and taken up with the other in the air, five gallops in one walk down the dais's step). The legs step up to `SLACK` off that line first (a diagonal is walked with the hips half turned, so forwards to backwards along it turns them less), and the hips turn no faster than `TWIST_RATE`. A stride is drawn along its own direction, turning towards the way the body goes rather than snapping to it (the foot whipped across), and a stride re-aimed late keeps a third of a swing's time at least. Check with the probe's leg-to-pelvis angle and pelvis turn rate (`d:w/s+atk`, `sd+atk`).
- A reversal (left, right, left again while attacking) doesn't swing the pelvis round through the aim: it keeps its line and the legs go backwards along it (`backing`), turning round only after `BACK_HOLD` of going the new way, and it holds its turn while the smoothed velocity collapses mid-reversal. No foot lands or swings across the pelvis's middle (`GAP`), and a planted foot a turn leaves across it re-steps (`CROSS`). Going the way it faces, none lands further out than a track (`WALK_TRACK`, `RUN_TRACK`, `drawToTrack`: a runner's feet land 5 to 10 cm apart, nearly in a line, a walker's 10 to 15; landed under their hips, a hero's were 23 cm apart running straight on and he ran with his legs apart), drawn in only as far as the body faces the way it goes (`TRACK_FACING`: strafing keeps its stance, and drawn in, its strides went across the pelvis), and a foot in the air passes the other a little further out (`SWING_GAP`). The gait's `--frames` gives each step's width. Test with the zigzag probe (attacking while switching left/right, forward/back and diagonals every 0.25-0.7s; count crossed-leg frames in the gait's pelvis frame, ankle jumps, both feet up) against main.
- The hero models bracket `legs.update` with `captureArms()` / `holdArms()`: each wrist keeps its hand's world turn through the lean and the pelvis's dip, so a weapon or shield keeps its angle (the hands' positions follow the body: pinning them too folded the elbows into the chest); a pose that needs a hand turned does it before `captureArms`.
- The body moves with it (`walkCycle`): at a run it is highest in flight and lowest as a foot takes the weight, rising and falling 6 cm a step as a runner's hips do (`RUN_BOB`, smoothly at both ends: by the walk's `|cos|` its top came to a point in the air) (a walk is the other way round), and it shifts sideways over the foot it stands on; the trunk leans 13 degrees at a run (`LEAN`: at 0.045 a radian per m/s it leant 19, a runner's 10), the head held up against the models' own lean of the spine. At a run the arms pump as a runner's (`RUN_ARM`, `RUN_ELBOW` in `rig.ts`, after Hinrichs 1987: swung from the shoulder about a middle a little back, the elbow bent near a right angle and more as it comes forward, the thighs' swing apart swinging them all the way; hanging nearly straight and swinging a walk's, a hero at 6 m/s read as stiff-armed), each as far as what its hand holds lets it (`WalkOpts.pump`, `pumpL`; `walkCycle` returns each forearm's swing, `ArmSwings`, for a model holding a load steady against it): a free arm all the way; the warrior's weapon arm half, its forearm kept level so the blade stays up (`RUN_PUMP`, `RUN_LEVEL`, `RUN_BEND`), a shield arm bent and swinging little (`RUN_SHIELD_*`), a two-hander carried at high port, the blade up past the right shoulder in both hands (`holdInChest`, `RUN_TWO_*`); the mage's staff arm not at all, the staff carried higher and swinging less (pumped, it swung wildly), its elbow held through the stride (`WalkOpts.elbowSwing`: by the walk's elbow swing the staff bobbed up and down each step), the free arm all the way; the ranger's bow arm keeps its carry (`pumpL: 0`), the draw arm pumping while its hand is free (`drawPump` in `ranger.ts`: eased off while the hand draws, fetches or stows, and a stow waits for it: begun from a pumping arm, the reach for the quiver came up as a zombie's arm). A pumping arm is loose: its forearm follows the upper arm a little late and a free hand flops on its wrist after it (`RUN_FOREARM_LAG`, `RUN_HAND_LAG`, `RUN_HAND_LOOSE`; `WalkOpts.looseL` / `looseR` off for a hand holding something: bent in step with the shoulder, the arms pumped as a machine's). Measure a change against a runner's numbers (cadence, contact and flight time, knee and hip angles, lean, bounce) with joint recordings drawn as stick figures, not by the pelvis's height alone: `npm run lab -- form`, then `gait`, `stops`, `range` and the ranger's `feet` and `carry` against `main` (a run's change reaches the walks and the stops).
- What is still off a runner's: the knee lands bent 27 degrees (a runner's 20) and bends less at mid-stance (27, a runner's 43), the ankle leaves the ground at -2 to +4 (a runner's -24: a rigid foot can't fold at its toes as a runner's does), the mid-stance's ankle reads 28 to 33 (the reference's own thigh, knee and ankle there disagree), at a run's reversal a planted hip goes up to 10 degrees past its extension for a frame or two (held out of reach by the gallop's wait, its leg snaps straight the frame it is dragged: let go then, the hip reads as it does in an ordinary set-off, where letting go sooner brings the gallop back), and at a walk's reversal while attacking the warrior's up to 13 for 3 frames, a foot past its window and more than a whole window before its next, neither late nor early, held till the body has left it far enough for a step (let go whatever its window, a check's gait had 10 more gallops over its 81 runs, 72 against 62), and the mage's staff tips forward at the front of each stride as it did before.
- Keep the matrix work to the hip chain (a crowd pays for it every frame: updating the whole skeleton here cost 8x more). It does nothing while the body is off the ground (an enemy rising at spawn) or dying, and restarts under the hips after a teleport (`reset()`). Everything is local and visual: a co-op copy computes it from the motion it replays, so there is no protocol change.
- **Where a standing body puts its feet is the pose's to say** (`legs.stance(i, x, z, turn)`, in the root's own frame; the heroes' `*_FEET`): a foot further than a few cm from its spot steps there, one at a time, its knee bending over the way it points, so an idle's stance (side-on, the rear foot turned out) and an attack's step in are real steps; moving, the gait ignores it. The pose then moves the pelvis freely (`body.position`, the hips' turn and roll: a weight shift, a lunge, a crouch) and the planted feet hold. A pose that moves the body faster than feet can step (a dash's lunge or charge) or spins faster than they could follow (Steel Tempest) owns the legs: it passes a lower `weight` to `legs.update` (the warrior's `legOwn`), the lean, twist and pelvis fade with it, and the feet restart where the pose has them when it gives them back; one leg alone goes through `legW` (`ownL` / `ownR`), the other staying planted.
