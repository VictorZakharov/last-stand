# `src/ui/skillArt`

The painted skill icons, a WebP a skill named by its `impl` (`ui/skillArt.ts` loads them), made and checked with `npm run icons` (`tools/icons/README.md`). They are one of the few binary assets allowed.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../../AGENTS.md).

## Gotchas

- **Skill icons** (`ui/skillArt.ts`): a skill's painted icon is `src/ui/skillArt/<impl>.webp`, found by its `impl` name, so one picture serves every class with that skill (the healing draught). A skill without one shows its glyph (`SkillDef.icon`).
  - **Size:** each is a 384 px square WebP made from the 1254 px original (WebP quality 88, 12 to 46 KB each). The largest slot is a tablet's touch attack button at 125 css px, and a phone's is 93 at three to three and a half device pixels each.
  - **Drawing:** the picture fills the slot (`.slot.art`), and the slot's frame and states (active, out of energy) are drawn again over it from its own `box-shadow` (`::after`, `inherit`), under the cooldown and the key. In the key diagram (How to play, the pause menu) a skill's picture fills its whole key, and the key, the skill's name and "hold" stand over it in a dark glow (at a keycap's icon size it was too small to read).
  - **Loading:** every picture is fetched at boot, and the service worker precaches them with the build.
  - **A new icon** matches the set, which is simple and bold.
    - **Simple:** one subject, or two or three of the same object at most; clean silhouettes with smooth shading; pure black to the edges, with nothing scattered round it (no sparks, leaves, rings or ground).
    - **Bold:** big, filling about 80% of the square; chunky proportions (thick shafts, big heads); a strong saturated glow in the skill's `icon.color` lighting it.
    - **Crafted:** an object is a real one, finely made, as the warrior's are: polished steel with bevels and crisp highlights on its edges, wood with its grain, leather and gold fittings, lit by the skill's glow, in a dynamic diagonal composition with the energy as arcs and ribbons behind it. An arrow is a whole arrow seen from its side, larger than life where it counts (a big steel head, a straight thick wooden shaft, three big feathers, a nock), its glow trailing behind it: the ranger's second set coiled glows round thin shafts, and players saw "unknown long objects". A figure keeps what makes it one clear at slot size (Veilstep's hood, shoulders and crystal staff: a hooded shroud of light read as a ghost). A symmetric emblem is laid out exactly, as with a compass and a ruler: its pieces identical and evenly placed, its rings true circles, its steel smooth (Quarry Mark's first reticle, four arrowheads of different sizes with hooked barbs on a lumpy, smoky ring, looked messy), and a target keeps its middle black, with bold bright rings round it and a bright point in it (its middle filled with orange glow, a steel hoop read as a furnace with a crosshair at slot size).
    - **Padded:** the touch buttons are round, so 99.5% of an icon's light must lie inside the circle they draw, and its content at least 4% clear of the square's edges. The ranger's arrows ran corner to corner and the circle cut up to 16% of their light. On a black background a picture is padded exactly by shrinking it onto a larger black square, which `npm run icons -- add <impl> <original>` does as it writes the WebP (`tools/icons/README.md`); `check` measures every icon (most of the warrior's and mage's, made before this rule, reach past the circle, Twin Fangs and Raise Shield by 10% of their light).
    - **Coloured apart:** each skill of a class in a colour of its own, as the warrior's and mage's are, with `icon.color` (the tooltip's name, the key diagram's glow) set to match. Veilstep's is violet though its light in the game is green: Void Lance's icon is green.
    - **Judged at slot size:** compare it with the others shrunk to 60 px (`npm run icons -- sheet --try <impl>=<picture>`: each class's icons side by side, large and at 60 px square and round, the candidate in its place). The ranger's first set was crowded (vines, sparks, a stag behind the reticle), and the next came out as thin, pale arrows that faded beside the others.
    - **Tooling:** the ranger's and Veilstep's were made with Codex CLI's image generation, attaching icons of the set as style references (`codex exec -i <icon> ...`): the warrior's for crafted objects, the class's own for its look, and the accepted ones of a set being made. Ask for two designs of a new one: the first idea isn't often the one.
