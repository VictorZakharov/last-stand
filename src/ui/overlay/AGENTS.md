# `src/ui/overlay`

The world's overlays, drawn by the renderer over the finished frame: `layer.ts` (the labels and their one instanced draw), `atlas.ts` (the texture they're painted into), `paint.ts` (painting them with the canvas as the page's CSS drew them), `bars.ts` (the health bars and gauges' art), `outline.ts` (an outline round one foe).

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../../AGENTS.md).

## Gotchas

- **What follows the world is drawn in WebGL, not on the page** (`ui/overlay`): damage numbers and floating words, the foes' bars and names, the props' gauges, the dummies' meters, the floor's item names, the partners' tags and a downed partner's marker. Each is a `Label`, painted with the canvas into one atlas when what it shows changes (`Label.show`, its art from `overlay/bars.ts` or `textArt`) and placed every frame (`anchorLabel` / `floatText` in `ui/floaters.ts`, or a module's own `onLayout` hook), and all of them are one instanced draw over the finished frame (`overFrame` in `core/renderer.ts`; drawn as a transparent quad when none shows, so its program compiles at load). They're placed by the camera's matrices before the frame is drawn, so `updateCamera` refreshes them as it ends (only the frame's drawing did: placed with the camera's turn of the frame before, the dummies' meters were up to 1442 px off them as a view switch glided round; `lab ui --states views`). Floating texts are capped at `MAX_FLOATS`, the oldest going. A label's art is in px of the UI's reference layout, drawn at the UI's scale (`Label.sizedBy`: the HUD's, or the lobby's for the floor's names), so it is the same share of any screen, and its pixels go with the square of the canvas's pixels per px of its art (the canvas's ratio times that scale), so the atlas grows with it (`SIDE_PER_RATIO` in `overlay/atlas.ts`: 2048 px square at 1, 3072 at 2, 4608 at 3), a slot given back is taken by the next label of its height it holds (kept for its own width alone, a fight's numbers of every width left it full of slots nothing new fitted: at 2, a heavy fight's 250 numbers left 56 with no slot, which aren't drawn), and a change of ratio empties it for every label to be painted again (`restartAtlas` in `overlay/layer.ts`); a full one with a quarter of it given back is packed again the same way (a repaint of every label shown, 20 to 80 ms at 3: it shouldn't happen). The perf report says how much of it is used. Measure with floating numbers a frame at each ratio (`--screen`): the labels with no slot, the use, the repacks. A label the pointer picks (`Label.pickable`, `labelAt`: the floor's names) is picked where it was last drawn, and only with the canvas itself under the pointer. As page elements moved every frame, they repainted what lay round them, and in Chrome on Windows (154 at least: ANGLE on Direct3D 11) a repaint that needs a Skia program not yet built holds up Chrome's GPU process, the game's canvas with it, for 1 to 4 s: the dummies' meters moving as the camera followed the hero froze the lobby every few seconds while the hero walked (a 20 s walk built 5 Skia programs in 4.9 s; on layers of their own, `will-change: transform`, 1 in 4 ms; in WebGL, none). Anything else on the page that must move every frame goes on a layer of its own. The perf report's `compiled after load` counts three.js's programs alone, not Chrome's for the page: measure with a Chrome trace of the walk (Skia's `driver_link_program`, inside `RasterDecoderImpl` on the GPU process's main thread), in Chrome itself (the lab's Chromium 149 never showed it): `npm run stalls` plays the game in the installed Chrome on a fresh profile and lists each stall with the programs built in it, the longest tasks through it and what the page changed just before (`--url` for production or a preview, `--headed`, `--links`). Look at the machine first: 1.4 to 1.9 s stalls in the owner's Chrome went away with a reinstall of the GPU driver, and the measures above were taken on that machine, perhaps already in that state; another agent's browser on the same GPU is in no trace of this one.
- **Digits on their line** (`paint.ts`): the body face's own digits are old-style, rising and dropping along a row of numbers, and a canvas takes no `font-variant-numeric`; so the labels paint the body face in a twin of it with its lining digits on (`FontFace.featureSettings`), made from the body face's own web font stylesheet, read with CORS from an address of its own (the page's is asked for without, and the worker players have can't answer a CORS request for it: `scripts/AGENTS.md`), and named first in its stack (until it has loaded, the face's own). The display face's are lining already. `npm run lab -- ui` holds each label font's digits to a line in every state.
- **An outline round a foe** (`ui/overlay/outline.ts`, Quarry Mark's preview): the foe's solid meshes drawn white into a mask of their own (each with a mask material made for its own, its alpha test kept: a leaf card drawn whole outlined as a box; glows and particles hidden), and the mask's edge drawn over the finished frame within the foe's box on the screen, under the world's labels and in front of everything else. It's a render of the foe alone, never of the scene with a layer: that would see none of the scene's lights, and every lit material would look for its program again the next frame. Its programs are compiled at load on one of every foe (`warmOutline` in `game/warmup.ts`). A creature's own meshes are on the crowd's layer (`models/crowd.ts`: its crowd draws it), which the camera has on while the mask is drawn.
