# `src/ui`

The page's UI over the game.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md); each directory under this one has an AGENTS.md of its own.

- **The lobby and menus:** `menus.ts` (the sanctuary with equipment and stash, the summary, pause and help), `loadoutEditor.ts`, `attributes.ts`, `stashFilter.ts`, `tooltip.ts`, `skillDamage.ts` (a skill tooltip's damage), `itemIcons.ts` (an SVG icon per item base), `saves.ts` (the save slots screen), `heroSelect.ts` (the hero selection screen over the hall of heroes), `coop.ts` (the room dialog, the partners' frames, the downed and the revive), `scoreboard.ts`.
- **The run:** `hud.ts` (bars, hotbar, score, target frame, minimap, spoils, the choice after a wave), `floaters.ts` (damage numbers and labels over the world, drawn by `overlay/`), `damageMeter.ts` (the dummies' readout), `touch.ts` (the touch controls).
- **Around it:** `scale.ts` (the UI scaled as a share of the screen), `focus.ts` (a clicked control gives its focus back), `loading.ts` and `logoWind.ts` (the loading screen and its exit), `pwa.ts` (the install link and the reload offered on a new deploy), `perfHud.ts`, `netHud.ts` and `battleLog.ts` (the overlays down the left, each switched in the pause menu), `clipboard.ts` (their reports copied), `skillArt.ts` (the skill icons), `altCursor.ts` (the cursor drawn over mouse look while Alt is held).

The PWA's worker and caches are `scripts/AGENTS.md`; the skill icons' pictures `skillArt/AGENTS.md`; the world's overlays `overlay/AGENTS.md`; the stylesheets `styles/AGENTS.md`.

## Gotchas

- **The UI must fit any screen size, desktop and mobile.** Anything that overflows or overlaps at some viewport size is a bug.
  - Scale: `--ui` for the lobby and modals, `--hud` for the in-run HUD, `--touch` for the touch controls (all set in `src/ui/scale.ts`, which `uiScales` exports for code).
  - **The UI is a share of the screen, whatever the OS's scaling**: a 4K screen shows it the same at 100% as at 300% (the fit has no ceiling: capped at 1.35, the UI at 100% was half its size at 300%). Only small screens (phones, small windows) keep a floor in css px, so they stay readable, and the touch controls are sized for a finger in css px. Everything drawn over the screen follows it: the tooltips' cards (`zoom`), the world's labels (drawn at `--hud`, the floor's names at `--ui`: their art is in px of the reference layout, `Label.sizedBy`), and the loading screen, styled before any script runs (its `--u` in `index.html`, the same fit in CSS). An element scaled with `scale:` keeps its offsets from the screen's edges unscaled, so give them as `hud()` too; a `zoom`ed one's are zoomed with it. Check with `npm run lab -- ui --screens 3840x2160@1,1280x720@3`: the same lines, and pictures apart only by text's antialiasing (the lobby's text drifts up to 1% of the screen down a panel: Blink rounds each line of text to whole css px).
  - Layouts: the lobby is the village (`game/village.ts`), its panels shown only while one is open (`openPanels` in `ui/menus.ts`: the inventory by I or the touch Bag button, the chest's with the stash beside it), against the screen's right edge, the village beside them. Short screens (phones held sideways, height ≤ 520) and upright tablets show one panel at a time, picked from `#lobby-rail`, and the camera view shifts so the character stays beside the panel (`lobbyViewShift`). Phones held upright get a "turn your device" overlay.
  - The village's own UI: the card an object opens (`villageCard.ts`: a portal's starting wave and Enter, the chronicle's save slots), the map (`villageMap.ts`, M or the touch Map button: drawn once from `world/villagePlan.ts` at a size, unfolding panel by panel by transforms alone), the training's tracker (`training.ts`, top-left; its keys said with a keyboard alone) and the crest blown away as sand after 8 s (`crestBlow.ts`, a fade with reduced motion, Low quality or the wind off). On touch the Use, Bag and Map buttons stand in the top-right corner (`#touch-village`), the tracker in the top-left.
  - Nothing moves as the class changes: text that differs by class is laid over the other classes' in one grid cell, only the current one shown, so its box is the largest's (the class card's name and tagline: the warrior's name pushed the class buttons onto a line of their own and the ranger's tagline took two lines, the panel growing by up to 40 px).
  - Check desktop at 1024×700 and 1920×1080, a phone at 844×390 and a tablet at 1180×820 and 820×1180 (Playwright with `isMobile` + `hasTouch`).
- **Touch** (`src/ui/touch.ts`): touch mode (`body.touch`, `input.touchMode`) follows the last pointer used, and the browser's emulated mouse events are ignored in it.
  - Controls: a move stick on the left half and the skill buttons on the right. The stick's travel is its size as drawn (`--touch`), its knob under the finger, full speed from 0.6 of the travel and however far past its edge the finger goes (held to the edge and divided by the finger's whole distance, half a screen out walked the hero at a seventh of his speed; check with `npm run lab -- touch`, on a phone's `--screen` too). The basic attack, channels and drawn bows act while held. A skill aimed by holding (`SkillDef.aimed`) is held as its key is, its own preview showing where it would land, and cast as the finger lifts; the rest cast on release, a plain ring showing a drag's spot. A drag aims by hand, with the right thumb while the left moves; a tap aims at the nearest foe, but a movement skill's (Veilstep) goes as far as it reaches the way the stick points, or the way the hero faces with it let go (at the nearest foe, a tapped blink landed the mage among the foes). The buttons have no tooltip: a tap's click opened the skill's card pinned over them, and the next tap there landed on the card. Check with `npm run lab -- tap --class mage` (and `ranger`).
  - A control clicked or tapped gives its focus back (`ui/focus.ts`): left focused, a class button showed the browser's focus ring at the game's next key, clipped by its group's frame to a second border down its side, and Space or Enter pressed it again. Check with `npm run lab -- ui --states clicked`.
  - The touch HUD's top corners sit at the screen's edges, with no safe-area inset (`$touch-edge`, `$touch-beside` in `styles/_theme.scss`): a phone held sideways has its camera cutout in the middle of its short side, and the inset (34 px on the owner's S25 Edge) and a margin stood the pause button, the scores button and the bars 44 px in, where the minimap's corner never took one. What reaches the middle of a side (the skill buttons' arc) keeps it. In touch mode the choice after a wave stays clear of the bars, between them while that leaves it 460 px of the reference layout and under them otherwise (an upright tablet's), its buttons 32 css px tall at least (`_touch.scss`). On a short screen the choice after a wave stays in the top quarter, its spoils and risk on one line and its buttons as tall as anywhere (narrowed in touch mode alone: with the mouse's keys beside them, its labels wrapped), and the minimap has a thin line round it in place of its bezel (`_responsive.scss`). Check with `npm run lab -- ui --states hudTouch,decisionTouch --screen 832x384@2.8125 --insets 34,0,0,0`.
  - Everything hover-only needs a touch path. Tooltips open on tap (`bindTooltip`); items open an action sheet (`openItemSheet`); skills are bound by tapping a key and then the skill. Drag and drop and Shift are desktop extras.
  - **An item's sheet keeps its buttons in view** (`#item-sheet`, `_modals.scss`): its cards scroll inside it and the buttons stand under them, the box sized in the window's units over `--ui` (it's zoomed: its `86vh` was 86% of the window times the UI's scale, and the box scrolled the buttons below a phone's screen with two shields' long block notes, though there was room round them). On a short screen its cards take the room across it, in smaller text. Check with `npm run lab -- ui --states sheetTouch --screen 832x384@3.75` (and the screens above).
  - **A long press on an item lights its slot's items until the next tap** (`menus.ts` `pressAt`, `liftFrom`): on touch the light follows the finger's press, not hover, and stays as it lifts; a tap on a lit item keeps it (its sheet too), a tap anywhere else puts it out, and so does equipping. A tap shorter than the focus's delay lights nothing, and opens the item's sheet; a long press lights alone (`longPressClick`: the browser clicks after a long press too, and a stash item's sheet opened with the light; with the sheet skipped, the tap's tooltip stayed pinned in its place, so it's put away too). It went out as the finger lifted (the pointer's leave), and on Android a long press is also a drag's start (which put it out) and a context menu (which unequipped or equipped the item): neither does anything on touch. Check with `npm run lab -- ui --states litTouch` on a phone's screen.
- **The stash's slot focus waits for the pointer to rest** (`menus.ts` `focusSlot`, `FOCUS_DELAY_MS`: 200 ms): the item under the pointer is lit at once in full (its colours, its glow fading in, the glow's loops: `.focal`), the others that fit its slot light up (and the rest dim) once it has rested there, and they all go out the moment it leaves. Hovering fast held the owner's 144 Hz at 95 frames a second with every change of focus at once (`npm run stalls -- --scenario sweep --item-ms 100`), and the whole focus waiting (the item under the pointer too) looked laggy to the owner. A change of focus paints nothing (`_items.scss`): the others are lit by their glow (a layer painted once) and their size, the rest dimmed by their opacity, each at once. Lit and dimmed by their filters and colours too, every change painted the 60 items again, and fading in, the 30 to 60 fades were the page's to step every frame: resting on each item in turn (`--item-ms 300`) held 80 to 90 frames a second, 125 to 137 without. Read a change's cost in the stalls report's changes of the page's look (each kind's work over the quiet phase's).
- **The tooltip follows the pointer by its transform alone** (`tooltip.ts` `place`, on a layer of its own): its size is measured when it's shown and when the window's size changes. Measured on every move, the comparison's stacked cards were taken apart and stacked again each time, two layouts of them, and placed by `left` and `top` it was laid out and painted again with what lay under it (10 to 16 ms of the browser's work after each move, in the stalls report).
- **The lobby's practice keeps the hotbar it built** (`hud.ts` `buildHotbar`): it's built again only for another hero or other skills in its slots. Space stows the lobby into practice and back, and built on every press, each slot was laid out and its icon painted afresh: 34 to 41 ms of the browser's work after each (`npm run stalls -- --scenario stow`).
- **The overlays down the left move by the compositor** (`#diag`'s `translate`, `menus.ts` `clearOverlays`), kept clear of the lobby's panels every frame while one is shown. Placed by `left`, every move laid them out and painted them again, and the fold tab's box read every frame forced a layout as the panels slid (Space pressed rapidly kept them sliding). Their clearance is read from the page until the panels rest in a way they're laid out (`restKey`: the window, a short screen's panel, the hero panel folded), then remembered, and forgotten when a panel's size changes.
- **Digits are lining** (`font-variant-numeric: lining-nums` on the page, `_base.scss`): the body face's own are old-style, rising and dropping along a row of numbers as lower-case letters do ("digits are all jumping up and down in the same line"). The property replaces, not adds: a rule that sets it keeps `lining-nums` (`lining-nums tabular-nums`), and the controls, whose browser style sets their font afresh, inherit it. The world's labels paint in the body face's lining twin (`overlay/AGENTS.md`). `npm run lab -- ui` checks every state: each look of the page's text showing digits, set out alone and pictured, and each font of the labels, with their digits' tops and bottoms on a line.
- **A skill's tooltip states the damage the hero deals with it now** (`skillDamage.ts`, from the skill's `hits` in `combat/skills`): each kind of hit's range (the roll's ±10%, `DAMAGE_ROLL`), a second's for a channel's ticks and how often they come, what the figure is made of (the base, the percentages its tags take, `damageScale` as `rollPlayerDamage` takes it) and the crit; a skill that heals or wards the hero, what it gives him now. It's built each time it opens, from the hero's skill as his weapons have it (`Player.known`) and his stats, so it follows gear and attributes; another save's skills show their base figures (`'base'`), and the touch buttons have none. Check with `npm run lab -- tooltips` (every hit a skill lands within what its tooltip states, and the figures following a weapon's damage) and `npm run lab -- ui --states skillTips,skillTipsTouch` at each screen above (every skill's tooltip within the window, opened by the mouse over the hotbar and by a tap in the lobby).
- **The cursor is the game's own**, from the loading screen on: a gilded arrow, lit with an ember's glow over what takes a click. Its art is two SVGs inline in `index.html`'s styles (`--cursor-art`, `--cursor-hand-art`, at most 32 css px: Chrome swaps a larger one for the system's near the window's edge), set on the page there (`--cursor`), so it shows before the bundle loads. Give a control `cursor: $cursor-hand` (and a disabled one `$cursor`, `styles/AGENTS.md`), never a keyword (`pointer`, `grab`, `default`, `help`): those show the system's; a text field keeps its caret. Check with `npm run lab -- ui`: every state's `cursors` line, the system's over any element is a problem.
- **The cursor over mouse look** (`altCursor.ts`): the close views hold the pointer locked, so the browser shows no cursor and sends every click to the canvas, and the HUD's buttons (the battle log's Pause, Copy, Clear) couldn't be pressed. Held Alt stills the view, the lock kept (letting it go and taking it again needs a fresh click or key every time, and Chrome shows its banner each time), and the mouse's movement moves the cursor drawn instead (the same art, on a layer of its own, lit as the page's would be). What it does goes to what lies under it as the browser's own would: hover (the hotbar's tooltips), a press (on the pointer's events: their down cancelled holds back the mouse's, so the canvas never hears an attack) and the wheel (a list scrolled, the view's zoom kept); over the world it does nothing. Alt's own keys are cancelled (let go alone, it takes the keys to the browser's menu). Check with `npm run lab -- cursor` (the lab's browser plays the lock).
- **The fight's loading bar** (`readying.ts`, `#readying` in `index.html`, `styles/_readying.scss`): the lobby shows before the fight's and the other heroes' programs are warm (`game/warm.ts`). Pressed early, Start, Change hero, a save slot, biome or co-op pick dims the lobby, shows the loading screen's bar in a small panel with the warm-up's progress, and goes ahead once it's done (on a first visit, about 1 s on the owner's machine; a return's fight is warmed behind the lobby once the loading screen has left, about 3.5 s after the lobby shows). It says what the latest press waiting readies (`READYING`, each press's `whenWarm` label: "Readying the heroes", "Readying slot 2"): the page's own "Readying the fight" showed for a class pick too. Check with `npm run lab -- ui --states readyingHeroes` (Change hero clicked with the heroes kept cold, and every label on one line on the screen).
- **The hero selection screen** (`heroSelect.ts`, `#heroes` in `index.html`, `styles/_heroes.scss`): every class's hero in 3D in the hall of heroes on the village's terrace (`game/hall.ts`), the camera coming to the one in focus and him showing off a skill of his (`HallDef.showcase`), his card in a panel (role, tagline, traits, skills, his records in the save slot) and the choice ("Play the X", or "Stay the X" for the lobby's own) on him: placed where the camera comes to rest on him (`placeChoice`, as the focus or the layout changes, never a frame: the camera's drift moves him a few px), seen through, shown as the mouse comes over him (`overHero`), on touch always; a drag begun on it pans the scene as the backdrop's does (in the panel's corner, the owner wanted it "on the hero... on hover"). A first visit opens on it (`hasSavedClass` in `loot/profile.ts`: no class cookie and no profile in the slot), the loading screen's logo gathering into its logo (`loading.ts` `blowFor`); the lobby's Change hero opens it again (through `whenWarm(['heroes'])`), and Back or Esc closes it but on a first visit. Arrows, the heroes' names, a tap or click on a hero, the scene dragged and the keys (←/→ or A/D, Enter or Space to choose) move the focus. A drag pans the camera along the row with the finger or the mouse (a hero's spacing on the screen a hero along, `dragHall`), and let go it lands on the hero nearest, carried on by a flick (`FLICK_MS`); the backdrop takes its own touches (`touch-action: none`: the browser's pan took them, and the page never heard the move). The cards' parts lie on rows they share (a subgrid), so nothing in the panel moves as the hero changes: in a column each, the ranger's tagline on two lines put his traits and skills 30 px lower. A card comes in by a fade alone: it slid up 10 px as it came, which read as the panel shifting "like its adjusting font". The heroes' names are held to the window's width too, clear of Back. The panel has the lobby's right pane's tabs too ("pro users will probably keep at inventory"): the hero's card, his Equipment (his gear round his class's doll and his stash, `heroGear.ts`) and his Attributes (`renderAttributes` given the hall's copy and his profile), the one last chosen kept (`last-stand.hero-page` in local storage) and its pages in one cell, so the panel's size never follows a page. The gear is to look at, not to change ("equipment tab is locked in hero selection screen, but hover on items should still work"): he isn't the lobby's hero until he's chosen, so his items show their cards on hover (a tap on touch) and nothing clicks, drags or throws. The save slot under the title opens the save slots over the screen (Esc closes them first, `main.ts` gives them the keys before it), and a slot played there opens the hall again at once on its heroes (`heroSelectSlotSwitched`, from `switchSaveSlot`). The panel stands at the right, or along the foot of an upright screen; the hero is framed in the room it leaves (`layOut`: the view shifted by `heroSelectShift`, the camera further back and looking at his middle on an upright screen, `frameHall`). Check at every screen above with `npm run lab -- ui --states heroes` (the hero framed head to feet in the room the panel leaves, the parts none over another, the choice's words within it: the buttons' own 24 px ran past it on a phone; the choice on the hero, shown on hover; the panel's parts in place for every hero and still as each card comes in; the tabs' pages, the gear read-only with its cards; the save slot clicked and slot 2 played from it; a finger's drag followed and landed where it carried the row).
- **Loading screen:** `#loading` (markup, SVG logo and styles) is inline in `index.html` so it paints before the bundle. The game renders behind it but only advances once it fades (`revealed` in `main.ts`), so the lobby intro is seen. Tips are in `data/tips.ts`. The lobby's panels are laid out under it from the arena's end (`layOutMenuAhead` in `menus.ts`, their content rendered with the hero): their first raster builds Skia's programs on the GPU process's main thread one after another (a first visit's 86, 1.2 s), and painted with the hero the lobby's warm-up waited behind them; now they're built while he is (`npm run stalls -- --scenario boot --links` counts each step's by where they were built). Anything the boot reads back from the GPU's process (a canvas's pixels, a program's locations) waits behind that raster too: read it early, or on the CPU (`readCtx2d`, `core/AGENTS.md`); the logo's program is read as soon as it links. Its exit (`ui/logoWind.ts`) snapshots the SVG letters (the font is inlined, since an SVG image can't load web fonts) onto the device pixel grid, turns every pixel into a WebGL point, blows them away and gathers them into the lobby logo (`#menu .logo-art`, kept hidden until then). The motion is closed-form in the vertex shader. Each point is kept as its place in the letters, and the letters, the lobby logo and the canvas are measured again on every frame of the exit (the lobby logo snapshotted again once it has moved or changed size, its old snapshot read where it is till then): made as the lobby was laid out and placed then, a window resized on the loading screen (full screen and back) blew the letters from where they had been and gathered them where the lobby's logo had been, 106 px and 22 px off. Check with `npm run stalls -- --scenario boot --resize 1920x1080` (five trips, the first held till the exit is made ready; `--stale-wind` is its canary). No copy of a logo is ever shown at rest beside the other, so the two never visibly swap: a point is drawn only once the wind lifts it (the real letters are clipped away just behind the front), and landing points take the colours of a snapshot of the lobby logo and land into it: the field draws the snapshot under them on the canvas's own pixels, each column shown as its points land (evenly over `LAND_SPREAD`), a point gone as it lands, so their last picture is the snapshot exactly. The lobby logo is then shown as that snapshot (`showPicture`: a canvas on the same pixels, in its letters' place) in the frame the field goes, till the logo moves or changes size or the screen its pixel ratio, when its SVG shows again. (The logo uncovered under landed points that faded jumped as it took over, a pixel down and a quarter aside at 1536x864: the browser paints the SVG a little otherwise than its snapshot, the grain and the edges, and points landing on one pixel added up to a darker rim and shadow. Its places were all exact.) Check with `npm run lab -- wind` (`--resize 1920x1080@1.25,1536x864@1.25 --screen 1536x864@1.25`: full screen and back at 125%): the particles' picture as each column rests against the logo after, at the screen's pixels; and look at it with `npm run stalls -- --scenario boot --film-logo`. It scales to the device: touch and low-end machines get a ~120k point budget (a coarser snapshot and a 2× canvas) instead of 1.5M. A watchdog cuts it short when the median of its first 10 frames is over 50 ms (under ~20 fps), and counts the launches in a row it did (`last-stand.logo-wind-slow`; one that keeps up clears the count). It falls back to a plain fade with reduced motion, Low graphics quality, or after two launches in a row cut short. Judged by the frames' mean and switched off for good on one launch (`last-stand.logo-wind-off`, cleared now), a single stall in those frames (a first visit's compiles, the GPU busy elsewhere) turned the blow off on a 4070 Ti: check with `npm run stalls -- --scenario boot --stall-blow 600` (the page held up once in the blow's first frames: the logo blown away on both visits, where production's is cut short and then a plain fade). The plain fade ends on `#loading`'s own `transitionend` (`loadingDone`): its parts' transitions bubble up to it, and the first to end (the bar's fill, the tip's fade) cut its 0.9 s off at 0.15 to 0.6 s, the loading screen gone in a frame. Nothing over the page shows or redraws under it: the overlays down the left (`#diag`, `booting` till `loadingLeft`) stay off until it's gone, as on the owner's phone the performance overlay's redraws there (4 a second) flickered the loading screen as it left; closed, it didn't. Film the exit with `npm run stalls -- --scenario boot --film` (a phone's: `--perf --window 799x384 --scale 3.75 --auto low@518400`): each frame, the flickers found, and when the loading screen was gone.
- **The battle log** (`battleLog.ts`, "Show battle log" in the pause menu): every hit the local hero deals, under the skill it came from (`HitOpts.skill`: a dash's step, a timer or a channel's tick can't be told from what is being cast), and every blow he takes, under the foe and its attack (`hurtPlayer`'s attacker), with what his armor, shield and ward stopped. They come through combat/damage's log sink, null while it's off or paused (its panel's Pause: nothing listed until it goes on, so what it shows stays to be read), so off it costs nothing. A line names the hero alone on his side and counts the foes on theirs (it once counted him: "Mossback strike ▸ You ×3"), and says what stopped each part of a blow ("stopped by armor", "resisted", "blocked by shield", "absorbed by ward": a bare "17 armor" wasn't clear). A burst's hits (a channel's ticks, a held attack's blows) join one line while they keep coming. It's drawn from the main loop at most every 0.1 s, on a layer of its own. The overlays down the left (`#diag`: it, the performance and network stats) keep clear of the lobby's hero panel, beside its fold tab or the one panel a short screen shows (`clearOverlays` in `menus.ts`, once a frame while one is shown, as drawn, so they follow a panel as it slides: they lay over it). On touch they sit under the touch HUD's corner (the pause and scores buttons, the bars, the buffs), and the log's lines let a touch through to the move stick's pad. Check with `npm run lab -- log` (every hit listed as it landed, the panel showing every line) and `npm run lab -- ui --states logLobby,log,logTouch` with a phone's `--screen`. (It came from a Bull Rush that seemed to deal nothing: its number showed a moment after a charged Power Strike's, and was missed.)
- **The perf overlay's clock** (`perfHud.ts`): the durations it reports (the update by stage, the render) are timed by `perfClock.now`, which a harness swaps for the browser's own clock (`npm run lab -- perf`: the lab's page clock stands still within a frame), while its window and graph run on the frames' timestamps. `lastPerfFrame()` and `takeGpuFrames()` give a harness each frame as the overlay summed it; the overlay's draw count is the GL draws (`renderer.info`), so a mesh three sets up and never sends to the GPU (an instanced mesh of none) isn't in it. Its report says, a 0.5 s bucket at a time beside the frames a second, our frame's time, how late it started and the GPU's time for our draws, and lists the browser's long frames (50 ms+, Chrome's Long Animation Frames API) with what the main thread did in each (the tasks before its render, the frame callbacks, style and layout, the longest script by what ran it): a fall with ours and the GPU's steady and the frames late, and no long frame, was held up outside the page (its GPU's process, the machine). `perfReport()` gives a harness the report (`npm run lab -- perf` checks it).
- **What the village shows first is laid out under the loading screen** (`layOutMenuAhead`, the tracker's `initTrainingTracker`, the practice's bar in `main.ts`'s boot): their first raster builds Chrome's Skia programs, and shown first mid-walk (the practice's bar as the hero walked into the yard, the tracker at the reveal) a first visit's held Chrome's GPU process 0.25 to 0.4 s. Laid out ahead, the lobby's walk linked 2 Skia programs where it had 18 to 25. Check with `npm run stalls -- --scenario lobby` (a first visit: its stalls and the Skia programs linked).
