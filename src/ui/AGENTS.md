# `src/ui`

The page's UI over the game.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md); each directory under this one has an AGENTS.md of its own.

- **The lobby and menus:** `menus.ts` (the sanctuary with equipment and stash, the summary, pause and help), `loadoutEditor.ts`, `skillStrip.ts`, `attributes.ts`, `stashFilter.ts`, `tooltip.ts`, `itemIcons.ts` (an SVG icon per item base), `saves.ts` (the save slots screen), `coop.ts` (the room dialog, the partners' frames, the downed and the revive), `scoreboard.ts`.
- **The run:** `hud.ts` (bars, hotbar, score, target frame, minimap, spoils, the choice after a wave), `floaters.ts` (damage numbers and labels over the world, drawn by `overlay/`), `damageMeter.ts` (the dummies' readout), `touch.ts` (the touch controls).
- **Around it:** `scale.ts` (the UI scaled as a share of the screen), `focus.ts` (a clicked control gives its focus back), `loading.ts` and `logoWind.ts` (the loading screen and its exit), `pwa.ts` (the install link and the reload offered on a new deploy), `perfHud.ts`, `netHud.ts` and `battleLog.ts` (the overlays down the left, each switched in the pause menu), `clipboard.ts` (their reports copied), `skillArt.ts` (the skill icons).

The PWA's worker and caches are `scripts/AGENTS.md`; the skill icons' pictures `skillArt/AGENTS.md`; the world's overlays `overlay/AGENTS.md`; the stylesheets `styles/AGENTS.md`.

## Gotchas

- **The UI must fit any screen size, desktop and mobile.** Anything that overflows or overlaps at some viewport size is a bug.
  - Scale: `--ui` for the lobby and modals, `--hud` for the in-run HUD, `--touch` for the touch controls (all set in `src/ui/scale.ts`, which `uiScales` exports for code).
  - **The UI is a share of the screen, whatever the OS's scaling**: a 4K screen shows it the same at 100% as at 300% (the fit has no ceiling: capped at 1.35, the UI at 100% was half its size at 300%). Only small screens (phones, small windows) keep a floor in css px, so they stay readable, and the touch controls are sized for a finger in css px. Everything drawn over the screen follows it: the tooltips' cards (`zoom`), the world's labels (drawn at `--hud`, the floor's names at `--ui`: their art is in px of the reference layout, `Label.sizedBy`), and the loading screen, styled before any script runs (its `--u` in `index.html`, the same fit in CSS). An element scaled with `scale:` keeps its offsets from the screen's edges unscaled, so give them as `hud()` too; a `zoom`ed one's are zoomed with it. Check with `npm run lab -- ui --screens 3840x2160@1,1280x720@3`: the same lines, and pictures apart only by text's antialiasing (the lobby's text drifts up to 1% of the screen down a panel: Blink rounds each line of text to whole css px).
  - Layouts: on desktop the lobby is a CSS grid `[left | center loadout | right]` with the panels against the screen's edges; each folds away to its edge on its own, leaving a labelled tab (`FOLDS` in `ui/menus.ts`, kept in `localStorage`). A fold tab stands outside its panel's frame, so a panel that scrolls does it inside (the left one's `.ml-body`), never on the panel itself. Short screens (phones held sideways, height ≤ 520) and upright tablets show one panel at a time, picked from `#lobby-rail`, and the camera view shifts so the character stays beside the panel (`lobbyViewShift`); they never fold a panel. Phones held upright get a "turn your device" overlay.
  - Nothing moves as the class changes: text that differs by class is laid over the other classes' in one grid cell, only the current one shown, so its box is the largest's (the class card's name and tagline: the warrior's name pushed the class buttons onto a line of their own and the ranger's tagline took two lines, the panel growing by up to 40 px).
  - Check desktop at 1024×700 and 1920×1080, a phone at 844×390 and a tablet at 1180×820 and 820×1180 (Playwright with `isMobile` + `hasTouch`).
- **Touch** (`src/ui/touch.ts`): touch mode (`body.touch`, `input.touchMode`) follows the last pointer used, and the browser's emulated mouse events are ignored in it.
  - Controls: a move stick on the left half and the skill buttons on the right. The stick's travel is its size as drawn (`--touch`), its knob under the finger, full speed from 0.6 of the travel and however far past its edge the finger goes (held to the edge and divided by the finger's whole distance, half a screen out walked the hero at a seventh of his speed; check with `npm run lab -- touch`, on a phone's `--screen` too). The basic attack, channels and drawn bows act while held. A skill aimed by holding (`SkillDef.aimed`) is held as its key is, its own preview showing where it would land, and cast as the finger lifts; the rest cast on release, a plain ring showing a drag's spot. A drag aims by hand, with the right thumb while the left moves; a tap aims at the nearest foe, but a movement skill's (Veilstep) goes as far as it reaches the way the stick points, or the way the hero faces with it let go (at the nearest foe, a tapped blink landed the mage among the foes). The buttons have no tooltip: a tap's click opened the skill's card pinned over them, and the next tap there landed on the card. Check with `npm run lab -- tap --class mage` (and `ranger`).
  - A control clicked or tapped gives its focus back (`ui/focus.ts`): left focused, a class button showed the browser's focus ring at the game's next key, clipped by its group's frame to a second border down its side, and Space or Enter pressed it again. Check with `npm run lab -- ui --states clicked`.
  - Everything hover-only needs a touch path. Tooltips open on tap (`bindTooltip`); items open an action sheet (`openItemSheet`); skills are bound by tapping a key and then the skill. Drag and drop and Shift are desktop extras.
- **Loading screen:** `#loading` (markup, SVG logo and styles) is inline in `index.html` so it paints before the bundle. The game renders behind it but only advances once it fades (`revealed` in `main.ts`), so the lobby intro is seen. Tips are in `data/tips.ts`. Its exit (`ui/logoWind.ts`) snapshots the SVG letters (the font is inlined, since an SVG image can't load web fonts) onto the device pixel grid, turns every pixel into a WebGL point, blows them away and gathers them into the lobby logo (`#menu .logo-art`, kept hidden until then). The motion is closed-form in the vertex shader. No copy of a logo is ever shown at rest, so the two never visibly swap: a point is drawn only once the wind lifts it (the real letters are clipped away just behind the front), and landing points take the colours of a snapshot of the lobby logo, which is then uncovered left to right under them. It scales to the device: touch and low-end machines get a ~120k point budget (a coarser snapshot and a 2× canvas) instead of 1.5M. A watchdog cuts it short when the first frames run under ~20 fps, and remembers that (`last-stand.logo-wind-off`). It falls back to a plain fade with reduced motion, Low graphics quality, or after the watchdog tripped.
- **The battle log** (`battleLog.ts`, "Show battle log" in the pause menu): every hit the local hero deals, under the skill it came from (`HitOpts.skill`: a dash's step, a timer or a channel's tick can't be told from what is being cast), and every blow he takes, under the foe and its attack (`hurtPlayer`'s attacker), with what his armor, shield and ward stopped. They come through combat/damage's log sink, null while it's off or paused (its panel's Pause: nothing listed until it goes on, so what it shows stays to be read), so off it costs nothing. A line names the hero alone on his side and counts the foes on theirs (it once counted him: "Mossback strike ▸ You ×3"), and says what stopped each part of a blow ("stopped by armor", "resisted", "blocked by shield", "absorbed by ward": a bare "17 armor" wasn't clear). A burst's hits (a channel's ticks, a held attack's blows) join one line while they keep coming. It's drawn from the main loop at most every 0.1 s, on a layer of its own. The overlays down the left (`#diag`: it, the performance and network stats) keep clear of the lobby's hero panel, beside its fold tab or the one panel a short screen shows (`clearOverlays` in `menus.ts`, once a frame while one is shown, as drawn, so they follow a panel as it slides: they lay over it). On touch they sit under the touch HUD's corner (the pause and scores buttons, the bars, the buffs), and the log's lines let a touch through to the move stick's pad. Check with `npm run lab -- log` (every hit listed as it landed, the panel showing every line) and `npm run lab -- ui --states logLobby,log,logTouch` with a phone's `--screen`. (It came from a Bull Rush that seemed to deal nothing: its number showed a moment after a charged Power Strike's, and was missed.)
- **The perf overlay's clock** (`perfHud.ts`): the durations it reports (the update by stage, the render) are timed by `perfClock.now`, which a harness swaps for the browser's own clock (`npm run lab -- perf`: the lab's page clock stands still within a frame), while its window and graph run on the frames' timestamps. `lastPerfFrame()` and `takeGpuFrames()` give a harness each frame as the overlay summed it; the overlay's draw count is the GL draws (`renderer.info`), so a mesh three sets up and never sends to the GPU (an instanced mesh of none) isn't in it.
