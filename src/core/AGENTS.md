# `src/core`

The engine under the game.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- `renderer.ts`: the renderer, its post chain (the scene in an MSAA target of its own; sanitize, bloom and the grade on single-sample targets), the canvas's fit, the final grade and the camera rig in each view.
- **Lights on the arms' layer and the warm-ups':** through the eyes the warrior's arms are drawn by a camera of their own, on their own layer, and a warm-up behind the lobby draws its part alone on a layer of its own (`WARM_LAYER`, `warmUp`'s `drawn`); a light a pass's camera can't see changes that pass's light count, which every material it draws is compiled for. So every light is on both layers as well from the moment it joins the scene (`lightThePasses` in `renderer.ts`, on the scene's `childadded`): put on it once as the arms were first drawn, a hero built afresh by a class switch brought his own light in after, and 11 programs compiled at the next run's start (#268). A light added under a group already in the scene isn't seen there: add the group's lights before the group, as the biomes do, or enable the layer itself. `npm run lab -- compiles` counts each pass's lights.
- `layers.ts`: the scene's layers, every use of one in one list, so no two take the same bit. The crowds' bodies' own meshes once took the arms' layer, so through the eyes the arms' own camera drew every foe within 6 m over the frame, huge at its narrower view. That was 656 draws a frame more with 38 foes before the hero. `lab draws --view first` checks what that camera draws.
- `hdr.ts`: HDR output on a display in HDR mode. `lowLife.ts` and `bloodEdges.ts`: the low-Health look.
- `input.ts`: the keyboard and mouse (touch feeds the same state, `ui/touch.ts`), pointer lock, the aim.
- `seeThrough.ts`: the props and walls between the camera and the hero dissolved.
- `materials.ts`: the material kit every entity builds through (shared FX uniforms: dissolve, frozen, hit flash; `nearGlow`). `shaders.ts`: every compiled program pinned. `quality.ts`: the quality setting and Auto.
- `pbr.ts`, `pbr.worker.ts`, `textures.ts`, `kept.ts`: the procedural maps and textures, made in workers while loading (four at most: more made a first visit's no sooner, vying with the GPU's compiles), the steel and the shown hero's face first, the other faces last (`inTheLobbysOrder`). The workers share the maps out among themselves, by orders in their names and Web Locks (`pbr.worker.ts`): a worker's messages are the page's main thread's to pass on, so handed out by the page its first map waited 0.8 s for the arena's long task and each next one for whatever task ran; each keeps what it made only once every worker has finished, as a read of the store waits for every write asked before it (a first visit spent 13.8 s of its 22 s of maps waiting there). Each is painted on its canvases as it comes (a map asked for before is handed out blank, and a warm-up waits for the maps its materials draw with, `mapsArrived`); a production build keeps them across visits in IndexedDB by the worker script's name (`kept.ts`), so a return reads them back in a few ms. A canvas whose pixels are read back takes `readCtx2d` (drawn and read on the CPU: on the GPU each read is a round trip to the GPU's process, waiting behind its work, and a first visit's hero build waited 0.8 s behind the lobby's panels' raster for the engraving's); one only drawn and made a texture keeps `ctx2d` (on the GPU: from the CPU its upload copies every pixel, the lobby's maps' 0.58 s against 4 ms). The engraving reads the steel's pixels as its map came (`albedoPixels`), not from its canvas. Every map a surface repeats must tile, its last column meeting its first and its last row its first: `npm run lab -- maps` fails on a seam (main's steel, slabs and fur had them: noise read at 0.05 or 0.08 of its period, a slab's shade taken round a count one longer than the course). So every lattice in a recipe is a whole number of cells across the map, each octave twice the last. `patterns.ts`: the stone, wood and steel's patterns after sdf-harness's materials (its `patterns.ts` at beed9c8, MIT; the owner's): its hash, value and fractal noise, cells, ashlar spans and joints, each lattice wrapped round the map and made once as a table of its hashes (hashed at each pixel, and with `Math.hypot`, the bark took four times main's: a table and `Math.sqrt`, twice); and its solid noise as it has it (`valueNoise3`, which needn't tile), the shape of the forest's rocks (`boulderGeo` in `world/props.ts`). The recipes after it (`ashlar`, `flagstone`, `marble`, `slate`, `steel`, `wood`, `bark`, `rock`, `moss` in `pbr.ts`) take its patterns' shapes and its presets' numbers in the tile's own unit (a block, a slab, a furrow), their colours darkened to the arena's: a new look keeps its map's means near the one it replaces (`lab maps` lists them), as each biome's light was set for them. A recipe may take its colours from a photo (`photos/AGENTS.md`), decoded where it runs before it runs (`photosFor`), its brightness the height or a height of its own: `bark` is the owner's oak photo, `forestFloor` and `cobblestone` Poly Haven's scans (CC0, colour and height, `photoGround`: 72 and 91 ms, where the procedural floors took 1325 and 556), each recipe's procedural map kept as the map made where its photo fails. How big a map's tile comes out on a surface is the surface's UVs' and the map's repeat's, which `pbrMaterialMaps` takes each way (`Repeat`; another repeat of the same maps copies the first's textures, which share their one upload): size a repeat by `npm run lab -- tiles`, which reads each surface's tile in metres (the forest's bark ran a tile round a whole trunk, 2.2 m, and 9 m along a root). A material may layer what sdf-harness calls a substrate (after UE5's) over its maps, as three's `MeshPhysicalMaterial` has them: the forest's moss takes a sheen, its fronds pale at grazing angles; a physical material is a program of its own, so keep a layer to small surfaces and check `lab compiles`. `parallax.ts`: the floors' relief. `engraving.ts`: the heroes' engraved steel and embroidery.
- `audio.ts`: the sound effects, synthesized. `music.ts`: the music's player.
- `timers.ts`: the game-time scheduler (it respects pause, unlike `setTimeout`). `cookies.ts`: small preferences.
- `tuning.ts`: values a dev build changes while it runs (`tunable(name, values)`; the lab's `--tune name.field=...` sets them before a command): a pose or a camera compared across values without editing and booting again. Read a tunable's fields where they're used, each frame; production registers nothing.

## Gotchas

- **Bright effects:** light adds up, so the final grade (`core/renderer.ts`) compresses it the way the eye does, and effects and their lights stay at full strength: don't dim single effects to fix a bright scene.
  - **Adaptation:** after bloom, the average light over a 16x9 and a 4x3 grid of the frame (`AreaPass`) is held under per-view targets (`ADAPT`, just above each view's ordinary scenes, which it leaves untouched) by compressing only the light above `ADAPT_BASE`, on a power curve. Never scale a whole pixel by its area's brightness (the dim ground round an effect darkens into a shadow around it), and don't adapt before bloom (effects drop under bloom's threshold and turn into flat discs with no glow). Above `HI_KNEE` the brightest channel eases towards `HI_CAP`, and in a large glare tone mapping keeps a bright pixel's colour (`HUE_*`, `GLARE_*`), so a big bright area stays green or blue instead of turning white.
  - **Distance:** effect materials dim with their distance from the camera (`nearGlow` in `core/materials.ts`: full strength from the top-down camera's distance, down to a quarter up close). `additive()`, the glow particles, projectile cores (a hostile bolt's is an orb of added light, with a pooled light when one is free; the spore throwers' are `fx/sporeOrb.ts`, the Rift Witch's and the Colossus's `fx/riftBolts.ts`: blended orbs, with added-light tails and halos), entity glows (`kit.glow`, the staff orb included; a hero's gear glows opt out) and the hit flash on bodies have it; wrap a new effect material in `nearGlow()`. The world's own glows don't.
  - **First person:** particles fade within 3.5m of the camera, a bolt flying at the camera shrinks away over its last metres (never pops out: nothing near the camera may switch off at once), and a cast leaves from `Player.castPoint` / `palmPoint`, which in first person are the weapon and hand in view. Keep lights off the camera: above the head, not at 2m. An effect round the body (a heal, a war cry) is seen from inside it: `Player.eyes` tells a skill it is, so it can put its particles where they're seen (a wider ring) and its light ahead of the view (`Player.lightAhead`; straight above, the weapons' steel mirrors it back into the view). The highlights on the hero's own materials are eased there from half of `SHINE.eyes` towards it (`core/materials.ts`, `kit.u.uShine`, set each frame by his player, 0 in the other views): a hand's breadth from the eyes, the sun on a blade's flat blew it out white and bloomed over 7.7% of the frame, its rougher grime left as dark blots across it. `lab haze` measures it.
  - Nothing large may flash or pulse faster than about 1.5 times a second: 3+ flashes a second is a photosensitivity hazard. A hit's flash on a body is full at most every 0.7s (`hitFlash` in `util.ts`). A pattern moving across the view flashes too: Maelstrom's wide ribbons passing a point 4 times a second, and its lashes of lightning at every damage tick, each flashed a sixteenth of the screen 3 to 4 times a second.
  - **Measure an effect** with `npm run lab -- cast <skill>` (`--class`, against main: commit, swap the skill's files in from `origin/main`, run, swap back): every frame of it drawn and read back in each biome and view, with the biome's foes coming at the hero. Its light is held to the budget from the owner's screenshots (it raises the frame's mean by 50 at most, its brightest sixteenth 150, 5% of the frame bright, as stored), its flashes are counted as WCAG counts them (a sixteenth of the screen each), and the shaders compiled after loading must be none. Read its timeline before changing anything: a mean that leapt as Maelstrom's first tick landed in first person was a dark foe in front of the camera dying, and a bright rock behind it showing.
- **The low-Health look** (`core/lowLife.ts`, the grade in `core/renderer.ts`): under half of his most Health the local hero's frame drains towards grey and blood seeps in from the screen's edges, both growing as the Health falls (the drain to 90% at none), with a heartbeat of 0.9 to 1.4 a second (none with reduced motion). It's all uniforms of the final grade (`setLowLife`), no shader change. The blood is vector shapes painted once on a canvas for the screen's shape (`core/bloodEdges.ts`: a seep with a wavy front in from every edge, deepest in the corners, drips from the top, droplets and clots, from one seed), into a mask whose green is how near to nothing the Health must be for each pixel to show, so the grade shows what the Health reaches; nothing reaches further in than 0.29 of the screen's height (`MOST_IN`). Painted again only once a new size has held for 250 ms. Page elements over the canvas would repaint every frame (see the overlays' gotcha). Measure with `npm run lab -- health` (`--canary`): at none, 65 to 83% of the colour drained and blood on 19 to 26% of the screen, none in its middle.
- **Canvas size:** `fitViewport` in `core/renderer.ts` fits the canvas, render targets and camera on resize *and* every frame, and ignores zero sizes. Moving the window between monitors can report a zero height mid-move (zero-sized render targets: a black screen until the next resize) or change the pixel ratio with no resize event. Read the window size through it, not `innerWidth`/`innerHeight` directly, in anything that feeds the camera. The canvas is the screen's own pixels, and the scene and its post chain render the quality's share of them (`sceneRatio`, `sceneSize`), the last pass scaling it up: anything sized in the scene's pixels (the aim laser's width) reads those, not the canvas's. Check a screen with the lab's `--screen` (`1280x720@3`: a 4K screen at 300%), which puts the page on it as a move between monitors does, and two with `--screens`.
- **HDR output** (`core/hdr.ts`, the pause menu's HDR: Auto or Off): on a display in HDR mode (`dynamic-range: high`) the frame's highlights go past the screen's white, and everything under them shows as on an SDR screen.
  - **The way out:** a WebGL canvas can't be shown in extended range (Chrome 149 has no tone mapping mode for it), so its drawing buffer is made float16 (`drawingBufferStorage`, with `EXT_color_buffer_float` on, or it stays 8-bit) and each frame is copied onto a WebGPU canvas configured `toneMapping: { mode: 'extended' }` (`copyExternalImageToTexture`, on the GPU, at the end of `render()`). The copy takes the colour as it is (`premultipliedAlpha: true`: the frame's alpha is 0, the canvas drawn as light added over the page, and by default the copy divided the colour by it). The WebGL canvas stays under the HDR one, invisible, and still takes the pointer. Both canvases hold extended sRGB, encoded (0.5 shows as 127, past 1 brighter than white).
  - **The grade** does the rest in uniforms, never a shader change: under `HDR_KNEE` (half of white) the frame is the SDR one as a gamma 2.2 screen shows it (Windows shows SDR content by the sRGB curve, which lifts its shadows), above it the highlights grow by their brightest channel towards the peak (hue kept), all of it at the paper white, and the highlights' cap grows with the room the peak gives over the paper white (`uCap`), or every effect's core would stay at SDR's.
  - **The levels** are shares of the OS's white for SDR content, as no page can read a screen's nits: Brightness (the paper white: lower it if the daylit forest is too bright) and Highlights (the peak), shown only while HDR is on and kept in `last-stand-hdr`. `?hdr=1` puts it on with any display (on an SDR one, past white is clipped), `?hdr=0` never.
  - **Measure** with `npm run lab -- light --hdr` (every pixel under the knee against the SDR frame at the paper white, nothing past the peak, the canvas shown read back against the frame drawn, with `--canary`) and the SDR frames with `light --ab`, which agree with main line for line. What it costs (a float16 drawing buffer and a copy of the frame a frame) hasn't been measured on a phone, where HDR displays are common.
- **Quality presets** (`data/quality.ts`, pause menu, Auto by default) change the scene's pixel ratio, MSAA, shadow map size, which small character parts cast shadows and the floors' relief (its steps a uniform), all live and without shader changes. Keep new settings in that category, or they cause compile hitches when switched. The scene's pixel ratio is often less than the canvas's (Low on a phone: a 3120x1440 canvas, the scene 1048x483), so anything sized in pixels sizes itself by the scene's target (`pointScale()` for points), not the canvas: the particles were 3 times too wide there.
- **Compiling is a first visit's cost, a lit program's most** (`npm run lab -- programs --cost`: each of the boot's programs compiled again alone, salted so no cache answers, its canary the cache): ANGLE's D3D compiler took about 0.56 s over a lit program written out for 16 point lights, 0.05 s over an unlit one. three writes the point lights' loop out once a light (`#pragma unroll_loop_start`, for a light's shadow map); no point light here casts one, so they're lit in a loop (`loopPointLights` in `renderer.ts`; the written-out block kept for a point light with a shadow): the programs' 40 s of compiling fell to 22 to 27 s, the pictures and the frame's GPU time the same. Keep a material's variants few (a uniform, not a define: the kit's near glow and rim in `materials.ts`, 157 programs to 146), and see what one costs before adding it. A page can't keep a program itself (WebGL has no program binaries): the GPU's process keeps them by their source, so a return reads them back in a few ms, and a deploy that leaves a shader's source as it was keeps its cache. A warm-up asks whether a program is linked only after a fence placed behind its compile has passed (`commandsDone` in `shaders.ts`): each question is a round trip to the GPU's process, and asked while it was reading programs back it held the lobby up 250 ms at a time.
- **Behind the lobby, a warm-up is a step a frame and draws only its part** (`warmUp`'s `step`, `drawn`, `giveUpMs`): each root's compile, a 2 ms slice of uploads, each program's uniforms read and the draw in a frame of its own (each cached program is read back on the GPU process's main thread, which draws every frame of the browser, and a whole part's in one frame dropped several), its part alone on the warm-ups' layer into a pixel of the target (the scene drawn again for each part held the lobby up), and what it adds kept out of the lobby's frames till its programs are linked: three links a program as it first draws it and waits there, so a foe seated in its crowd (which draws it whatever the camera sees) or an object unculled before its link had the lobby's frame wait for it (a return's lobby drew 18 to 36 frames a second). A program not linked 150 ms after its commands are done is cold: the part is given up, nothing drawn (`cold`). What a draw itself makes (a shadow's depth programs, the outline's masks) still links inside it. `npm run stalls -- --scenario boot` counts the lobby's frames a second as the perf overlay does (`--cold-return` for a return whose fight was never compiled).
- **Shaders ANGLE compiles at a draw** (D3D11; `npm run stalls -- --scenario boot --angle`: `D3DCompile` on the GPU process's main thread): a vertex attribute of integers read as floats is a format D3D11 converts, and a `flat` varying under GL's last-vertex convention needs a geometry shader, and for each ANGLE compiled a shader of its own at the program's first draw, on the main thread, holding every frame up (a first visit's 760 ms). So the bones' indices are floats (`models/shapes.ts`, `handShape.ts`) and a `flat` varying is the first vertex's (`firstVertexProvokes` in `renderer.ts`, WEBGL_provoking_vertex: the crowds' rows, the same at a triangle's every vertex). A return reads such shaders back with the program; keep integer data out of float attributes.
- **Shadows' programs don't hang on the casters' order** (`steadyShadowPrograms` in `shaders.ts`, at the renderer's set-up): three draws every caster that tests no alpha with one shared depth material, copies the caster's `map` onto it, and builds it a new program only when the instancing, instance colours or skinning change between draws, so which variant (with a map, or without) a caster got hung on what was drawn before it, and a new order mid-fight compiled one more. The map, which does nothing without an alpha test, is let go before each such draw. A program compiled mid-fight shows in the perf report by its kind (`depth`: a shadow); `npm run lab -- compiles` breaks every kind of prop and fights a first wave in each biome (`--boot low@518400` as a phone boots) and names the draw that first used it.
- **Shadows' programs are compiled with the rest** (`compileShadows` in `shaders.ts`, from `compileAhead` and `warmUp`): three's compile makes none (its shadow pass makes each as it first draws with it, linked there, the page held up: a first visit's lobby warm-up made 10 inside its draw), so each caster is compiled with a stand-in for the depth material the pass would draw it with (r185's `getDepthMaterial`: its `customDepthMaterial`, else three's own, its side, alpha test and map as the pass sets them, the map let go as `steadyShadowPrograms` lets it go), without the scene's fog and environment (the pass draws with neither), and the warm-up waits for their links. The pass's own materials then find them made. What the shadow map alone draws (`inShadowsOnly` in `world/culling.ts`) is left out of the colour compile (`withoutUndrawn`, with the crowds' bodies' own meshes). `npm run lab -- programs` lists each program a draw made: the boot's are 0 (#275's: 32).
  - **The resolution is counted in the screen's pixels, never by the OS's scaling** (as a 4K video plays at 4K at any scaling): each level renders at most `pixels` of them (High all of them, Medium 2560x1440's worth, Low 1920x1080's), on any device: what the hardware holds is measured, not guessed from what it is (a phone, a desktop, a card's name). In px per css px, a 4K screen at 300% (1280x720 css px) rendered 1920x1080 stretched to 4K, where at 100% the same screen rendered 4K, on an RTX 4080 that drew the frame in 3.3 ms. A share of the screen's pixels is rounded to a power of two's fraction (`SHARE_STEP`), so the same screen at any scaling renders the same.
  - **Auto steps High's resolution down first**: with the frame rate under `minFps`, the scene's pixels halve a step down to `highFloor` (a 1080p picture), then the level goes down keeping them, then Low's halve down to `fewest` (960x540's worth). What it settles on is kept as a budget of pixels (`low@1036800` in its cookie, `qualityBudget`), which holds on a screen of any size. It never steps back up on its own, so a frame that isn't the device's measure is left out: Auto holds (`holdQuality`) while a warm-up runs behind the lobby or under the fight's loading bar (`game/warm.ts` `isWarming`; compiled cold behind the lobby, a return drew 0 to 6 frames a second for 3 s, enough to halve the pixels for good). The probe of it fed Auto slow frames and read each step's canvas, scene and cookie (`--screen` at 1x, 2x and 3x; one 4K screen at 100% and 300% gave the same lines).
- **The floors' relief** (`core/parallax.ts`, parallax occlusion): a floor's height, kept in its roughness map's red channel (`buildPBR` in `core/pbr.ts`: no material reads it, roughness is green), is walked down along the view ray and its maps read where the ray meets it, so the crypt's grout and the forest's soil lie sunk below the cobbles' and pebbles' tops. Only the hollows sink: the tops stay at the floor's own level, where everything on the floor stands. It samples with the floor's own uv gradients (`textureGrad`: in a loop the GPU's are undefined, and the moved uv's jump at each stone's edge), takes up to `relief` steps (the quality preset's: 16, 8, none on Low; half of them looking straight down) and fades out by `fade` m (past the top-down view's 30 to 51). Its shift is a pixel or two from the top-down view and shows most over the shoulder and through the eyes. Apply it with `floorRelief(shader, ...)` after a floor's own edits in its `onBeforeCompile`. A floor has to give it something to lift: the forest's (`forestFloor` in `core/pbr.ts`, 5 m a tile, heights in cm) is a litter of fallen leaves in three layers (each turned its own way, cupped, with a raised midrib and veins, the lower ones older and darker), twigs and pebbles over clumpy soil, whatever lies highest showing at each point; as soil with flat round leaves 1 cm a texel, it showed no relief at all.
- **Views** (V, in runs and the lobby, not touch): `core/renderer.ts` has top-down, third and first person, gliding between them. The close views lock the pointer for mouse look through the run (the bank-or-continue choice too: B / C pick), and in the lobby while its panels are stowed (V into a close view stows them, back to top-down shows them; Space or the Esc that lets go of the pointer brings them back, the pointer free for them; its view shift beside a panel is the top-down view's alone) (`wantPointerLock` in `core/input.ts`, taken while the last key press or click still counts as a user gesture, else on a click) and aim at the crosshair (`input.centerAim`): the foe under it, else the floor, else a point `aimRange` ahead. Losing the lock mid-wave pauses. Held Alt keeps the lock and stills the view, the mouse moving a cursor drawn for the HUD instead (`ui/altCursor.ts`, which hears the mouse before `input.ts` does). In first person the body faces where the camera looks (`lookFacing`), so melee arcs and blocking follow the view, and only its arms, hands and weapons show (`entities/viewModel.ts`): the posed model (at its own scale) is moved so the camera sits just above its neck (the head rides the spine, so a lean never brings the shoulders under the camera), turned and tilted with the view and tipped back a little so the hands sit in frame, and the rest (pauldrons included) goes on a layer the camera doesn't draw. Every attack animates as in the other views, and **the middle of the view stays clear**: weapons, hands and spell effects are seen but kept off it, so what's ahead (the foes) is. `Model.firstPerson` switches a model's first-person poses, tuned by measuring where the weapon and hands land on screen (`FP_*` in `models/warrior.ts` and `models/mage.ts`): the warrior holds its weapons low in the corners, damps the stride's arm swing, swings lower and narrower with the blade up so the swing crosses the view broadside, places Power Strike, Twin Fangs and Bull Rush in the view itself (`holdInView` with `fromEyes` in `viewModel.ts`: a point of the camera's view in the posed model's world, each arm reaching by IK and the wrist turning the weapon onto a direction; `FP_CHOP`, `FP_CUT`, `FP_TUSK`): Power Strike crosses the blades over the top of the view as the charge builds and drives them down through it, Twin Fangs cocks each blade across the upper view and cuts it down to the opposite corner, Bull Rush draws the blades back, drives them ahead through the rush and flings them out to both sides as it lands (in every view: the charge's pose follows through for a moment after the rush, its weapons sweeping out with slash trails, and through the eyes the view widens with the speed, `kickFov`, as streaks of air rush at it); it raises its shield to just under the crosshair and hides its elbow fur; a two-hander (whose lower grip, the left hand's, would sit below the frame otherwise) is always placed in the view: at rest in both hands low on the right, the right elbow kept down so the forearm rises from the frame's edge, its swing sweeping the blade across the view like a wiper, Power Strike a raised guard on the right and the blow ahead; the mage holds the staff out to the right, narrows its bell sleeves and plays the free hand's gestures (cast, Void Lance) in the left of the view, shifting the hand in the chest's space and reaching it with arm IK (`reachArm`). The third-person camera never pulls in towards the character: props and walls between it and the player dissolve instead (`core/seeThrough.ts`; from above too, in the lobby and the top-down view, where a pillar hid the hero behind it, with a hole about his own size there: at the shoulder view's, a stump beside him came apart; not through the eyes), so give a biome's prop and wall materials `seeThrough(m, 'prop')` (not the floor's; low foliage too: the canopies' wide hole took the ferns round him wherever he stood), and a raw `ShaderMaterial` on a prop (the gates' membranes, the beacons' flames) `seeThroughFade`, its fragment shader fading by `seeFade()`: the chunk patch can't reach it, and the membrane hid the hero standing in a gate.
- **An attack goes the way the body faces.** The cursor aims at the floor (or a prop) as it is seen (`floorHit` in `core/input.ts`: the dais's top, its step, then the floor; met on the floor's plane, the cursor over the dais pointed 35 cm past what it showed and the hero turned away from it near the feet), the body turns from its own position to that point, and a shot leaving a weapon's tip heads the way the body aims, closing on the point only from a few metres out (`Player.shotHeading`: aimed from the staff's tip, out beside the body, a point near the feet sent the bolt sideways or back). Measure with the aim probe: the cursor at screen offsets round the feet, on the dais and off it, zoomed in and out, the shot's heading and the body's facing against the point under the cursor.
- **The aim meets a body as it's drawn** (`aimAlong` in `core/input.ts`): the first thing the eye's ray meets, of a foe its rigid meshes and its skinned limbs alike (`castSolid` in `fx/stuckArrows.ts`, the skinned through `entities/models/hulls.ts`), looked for as far as its body reaches as built (`Enemy.reach`: a boss's crown stood 1.8 times the height its hits count). Met on the rigid meshes alone within its height, the aim over a boss's limbs and crown went on to the floor behind it, and Quarry Mark marked a small foe there. The foe met is kept (`input.foe`, none for touch): a skill that picks a foe takes it, not the foe nearest the point. Measure with `npm run lab -- held --class ranger` (every point over each biome's boss and its summons, in each view) and its cost with `--profile=aimAlong`.
- **Music** (`core/music.ts`, `data/music.ts`): each biome has a track for its lobby and one for its waves' fights (`BiomeDef.music`): the Thornwood's lobby plays Quiet Beginnings and its fights Sunlit Forest Battle, the crypt's Shadowed Forest Dusk and Crypt Motif. The lobby's track plays in the lobby and in a run wherever there's no fight: the countdowns, the choice after a wave, the summary. The battle track comes in as each wave's fight starts (`G.run.phase === 'fighting'`) and fades out once the wave is won, the lobby's coming back as it goes (`FADES`: the lobby's in over 3 s and out over 1.2, the battle's in over 1 and out over 2.5). Every track comes back where it was left (the lobby's between wave 1 and 2 where wave 1's fight left it, the battle's on from wave to wave), from its top only once it was let go: a new run's first wave, a biome switched to in the lobby, which crossfades the two lobbies'. Each track is a loop of its piece made by `npm run music -- make` (`tools/music/README.md`, which says how each was cut: Crypt Motif has no repeats, and loops whole): the piece up to where it comes back, a crossfade into the loop's start and a second more of it, looped from just past the crossfade to as far past the loop's end (`TRACKS`' `loop`), so both sides of the seam are the same samples whatever delay a decoder leaves at the file's start (an audio element's loop left a gap of the MP3's padding). MP3s at 44.1 kHz, LAME's VBR quality 5, about 110 kbps (Opus would be smaller, but iOS Safari plays it unreliably), 4.9 MB for the four. Each is fetched whole and decoded when it's first wanted (a run's battle track as the run starts, so its first wave doesn't wait; through a run both its tracks stay decoded), looped by a buffer source and faded by a gain node of its own (iOS ignores an element's own volume) through the effects' WebAudio context, and its decoded samples (20 MB a minute) are let go once it's silent and wanted nowhere. The music is asked to start as the lobby shows (a browser lets it start before the page's first click or key only where it allows autoplay, as Chrome does on a site visited often or an installed app; elsewhere that first gesture starts it), and stops in a hidden tab, playing on from there when it comes back. Measure with `npm run lab -- music` (`--canary`): each biome's lobby, a run's countdowns, fights and wins, a hidden tab and the bank back, judged by what reaches WebAudio and what each track's output carries.
