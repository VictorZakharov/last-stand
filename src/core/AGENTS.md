# `src/core`

The engine under the game.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- `renderer.ts`: the renderer, its post chain (the scene in an MSAA target of its own; bloom, the area's light and the grade on single-sample targets, each reading the scene's target itself, its NaN and Inf pixels cleaned as it reads it, `SCENE_READ`, and bloom added by the grade and the area rather than blended over a copy of the frame: two passes over every pixel fewer, at every level), the canvas's fit, the final grade and the camera rig in each view.
- **Lights on the arms' layer and the warm-ups':** through the eyes the warrior's arms are drawn by a camera of their own, on their own layer, and a warm-up behind the lobby draws its part alone on a layer of its own (`WARM_LAYER`, `warmUp`'s `drawn`); a light a pass's camera can't see changes that pass's light count, which every material it draws is compiled for. So every light is on both layers as well from the moment it joins the scene (`lightThePasses` in `renderer.ts`, on the scene's `childadded`): put on it once as the arms were first drawn, a hero built afresh by a class switch brought his own light in after, and 11 programs compiled at the next run's start (#268). A light added under a group already in the scene isn't seen there: add the group's lights before the group, as the biomes do, or enable the layer itself. `npm run lab -- compiles` counts each pass's lights.
- `layers.ts`: the scene's layers, every use of one in one list, so no two take the same bit. The crowds' bodies' own meshes once took the arms' layer, so through the eyes the arms' own camera drew every foe within 6 m over the frame, huge at its narrower view. That was 656 draws a frame more with 38 foes before the hero. `lab draws --view first` checks what that camera draws.
- `hdr.ts`: HDR output on a display in HDR mode. `lowLife.ts` and `bloodEdges.ts`: the low-Health look.
- `input.ts`: the keyboard and mouse (touch feeds the same state, `ui/touch.ts`), pointer lock, the aim.
- `seeThrough.ts`: the props and walls between the camera and the hero dissolved.
- `webgpu.ts`, `rendererChoice.ts`, `shaderPorts.ts`, `meshlets.ts` (the GPU-driven drawing's clusters made in workers, `meshlets.worker.ts`): the WebGPU renderer, a trial behind `?renderer=webgpu`, which the pause menu opens (without `&meshlets=1`,
which only the address asks for: on a phone the hero stood stuck mid pose under them) with nothing kept: the plain address always loads WebGL, and players default to it (`docs/research/webgpu.md`); a GLSL `ShaderMaterial` is drawn there by its port (`portShader`: its shader in WGSL, its uniforms read off the material drawn), so a new one gets a port, or a boot logs it "Not ported to WebGPU". A ported or look-given material carries a node of its own (`refreshedEachDraw`): three refreshes a still mesh's uniforms only when the material it was given holds a node, and without one the outline's strength stayed at the warm-up's 0. A kit material (`materials.ts`) is refreshed as a plain one is, and also when its looks change (`stillWhereStill`, `looksChanged`: kept on each render object, as three's observer is one for every render object of a node build, and kept on it the two dummies' looks took turns and refreshed every draw): refreshed at every draw, the lobby's two training dummies were 47 full refreshes a frame, standing still. `?builds`' `__webgpuFull` names each full refresh's cause (a node, animated, its looks, moved, changed). A still material (`stillNodes`: see-through, kit) still carries its node, made still at every draw (three marks a build as holding a node again as each of its render objects is first drawn): the meshlets' pipeline draws a copy of each material, which takes the node with it, and a copy without one drew its batches as they first drew, so once the clusters' pool grew a few seconds in, the forest's standing stones and boulders were drawn nowhere (3455227 to 1c2cfbe; check `&meshlets=1` with `npm run stalls -- --scenario lobby`, its end pictured, as every other check runs without it). That copy takes every own property, a `customProgramCacheKey` too (`seeThrough` sets one, for WebGL), and three keyed the copy's node build by it alone, so batches of one see-through material shared the first one's build and its offsets into the culling's buffers: the crypt's obelisks and beacons drew another batch's range, nothing at all, whenever their batch built second (a race with the warm-ups, so a picture or two proved nothing). Each batch's draws are now built apart (`batchesBuiltApart`); the stones likely went the same way. Found with stalls' `--before` (a change made just before the picture) and `--read` of the batch's uniforms against its range. Built apart, each batch is a full node build (about 6.5 ms), so a batch made at a draw holds that frame up: three dropped every batch not drawn in the projection that resized its render list's culling (each warm-up's new biome, the lobby's frames shrinking it to their own) and any left undrawn for 60 projections, so the lobby's frames, the warm-ups' draws and the fight's first frames made theirs again, 1477 builds at a draw in the lobby's first seconds, frames held up 200 to 740 ms. `batchesKept` keeps them (a resized culling sized for the undrawn too, which are moved into it) and the scene pass is sized before the warm-ups compile into it (its culling is made for the size drawn at: compiled at 1 px, the lobby's first frame made all 35 again). A lobby that stays (`--warm-behind`) no longer holds up past about 100 ms on a return, and Start once it's warm makes no batch, only about 40 to 80 ms of the crowds' shadows' builds. The scene's matrix walk is no lever: all of it is 0.22 ms a frame (804 objects), hidden biomes or not. A batch's draws are refreshed only as the batch moves in its culling's buffers or any buffer is freed (`batchMoved`): those were 38 full refreshes a frame changing nothing, but refreshed on a relocate alone, whole passes were refused every frame (`Buffer used in submit while destroyed`, 500 times a boot; a boot's `page errors` say so). Of three's per-object time about half is the full refresh (bindings 2.1 ms, nodes 1.3, under the profiler); what was left of it in the lobby was mostly the hero's 68 scene draws, each moved part refreshed whole for its matrix, now drawn as one (`models/drawAsOne.ts`). A render bundle still asks each of its objects whether to refresh (its draws cost nothing, its `needsRefresh` does), so a moving object in a bundle saves little: it needs its matrices elsewhere first. three executes a pass's bundles as it ends, after what's blended, so opaque meshes in one would draw over the glows before them: `bundlesBeforeTransparents` executes them before the first blended draw. A stand-in's refresh looks at its base's values alone (`baseChanged`): three's observer compared 85 properties a draw, through accessors. A texture read only by still meshes isn't sent by their refresh: what changes it sends it (`uploadTexture`). `?builds`' `window.__meshletBatches` says of each batch whether a compile or a draw made it, at what size, in which render list and culling, and what became of the one it replaced. A pipeline first needed at a draw is made beside the frames, its object left out till then (`pipelinesBeside`), so anything drawn only once into a texture (the still shadows, the environment maps) draws inside `pipelinesAtOnce`, or it would miss what wasn't made yet.
- **Each WebGPU frame begins a node frame of its own** (`newNodeFrame` in `renderWebGPU`): three's passes draw their scene once a node frame, and only three's own animation loop began one, at the browser's frames. A frame drawn without a new one drew the post chain and the overlays over the scene's last picture: a phone's game went on with the scene stuck (from play: "everything stuck, but not frozen"), and main's lobby here, three's loop held, changed 9% of the screen's middle as the hero walked (the labels), 95% now (`lab profile --renderer meshlets`, which holds it).
- **What WebGL's GLSL patches do has a WebGPU side of its own:** the scene's MSAA, chosen once as the game starts (`setSceneSamples`, `?msaa=N`: a pipeline is made for one count), and its share of the screen (`setSceneShare`); bloom's levels weighed as `BLOOM_LEVELS` by its tints; the environment shadowed (`shadeEnvironmentNodes`: the sun's shadow in a variable, read by the direct light and by the terms the lights leave). A uniform node set once a render goes in `renderGroup`: in the object's group, the default, a still material's shared refresh never sends it, and see-through props stayed at the warm-up's, solid before the hero. The GPU-driven drawing makes its draws' materials from a material's own enumerable properties and never converts it, so what `takePorts` sets on a converted one misses it: a still see-through material holds its mask itself (`maskOwn`). A kit's `colorNode` keeps its map's alpha: taken from the opacity alone, the mage's ink hid his forearm. Check a look on `&meshlets=1` and without it.
- **WebGPU's frame is about its CPU work** (the forest lobby at 1518x768: GPU 2.47 ms, CPU 8.05, the perf overlay's). What's cut: a crowd's materials are still (`stillNodes`, their rows sent by `writeRows`, once a frame: a shadow's render of the scene wrote them again; a texture made anew refreshes its slots in full for a frame, `refreshedInFull`, or their bindings would hold the old one); so is the grass (its clock in `renderGroup`); culled instances stay static in usage (three refreshes dynamic ones in full at every draw, and a new version already says they changed); the sun's shadow draws every other frame from about 65 frames a second by the frames' mean (`sunShadowInTurns`, `?shadowEvery=1`: by each frame's own time, one that missed the screen's refresh had the next draw the shadow too, and the game stuck at half the screen's rate); and one of the adaptation's three grids a frame. Left, under the profiler: the meshlets' `project` and `finishRenderList` about 1.2 ms, the hero's pose 1.4 (WebGL's too), the post chain's 15 renders about 0.8.
- **three's unpacking state is set as the renderer starts** (`unpackStateKnown` in `renderer.ts`): three reads back the unpacking values it hasn't set itself before a copy into part of a texture (`copyTextureToTexture`), a round trip to the GPU's process that waits behind its work: the first label's copy into the overlays' atlas held a first visit's lobby frame 41 ms while the hall's programs compiled.
- `materials.ts`: the material kit every entity builds through (shared FX uniforms: dissolve, frozen, hit flash; `nearGlow`; a worn item's glow in its rarity's colour, `ITEM_GLOW`, its colour a copy's own `kitLooks.aura` made by `kitCopy`, its tendrils (`AURA_GLSL`, WGSL's `auraTendrils` in `shaderPorts.ts`) on one looping clock moved once a frame by `tickItemGlow`: `src/entities/models/AGENTS.md`). `shaders.ts`: every compiled program pinned. `quality.ts`: the quality setting and Auto.
- `pbr.ts`, `pbr.worker.ts`, `textures.ts`, `kept.ts`: the procedural maps and textures, made in workers while loading (four at most: more made a first visit's no sooner, vying with the GPU's compiles), the steel and the shown hero's face first, the other faces last (`inTheLobbysOrder`). The workers share the maps out among themselves, by orders in their names and Web Locks (`pbr.worker.ts`): a worker's messages are the page's main thread's to pass on, so handed out by the page its first map waited 0.8 s for the arena's long task and each next one for whatever task ran; each keeps what it made only once every worker has finished, as a read of the store waits for every write asked before it (a first visit spent 13.8 s of its 22 s of maps waiting there). Each is painted on its canvases as it comes (a map asked for before is handed out blank, and a warm-up waits for the maps its materials draw with, `mapsArrived`); a production build keeps them across visits in IndexedDB by the worker script's name (`kept.ts`), so a return reads them back in a few ms. A canvas whose pixels are read back takes `readCtx2d` (drawn and read on the CPU: on the GPU each read is a round trip to the GPU's process, waiting behind its work, and a first visit's hero build waited 0.8 s behind the lobby's panels' raster for the engraving's); one only drawn and made a texture keeps `ctx2d` (on the GPU: from the CPU its upload copies every pixel, the lobby's maps' 0.58 s against 4 ms). The engraving reads the steel's pixels as its map came (`albedoPixels`), not from its canvas. Every map a surface repeats must tile, its last column meeting its first and its last row its first: `npm run lab -- maps` fails on a seam (main's steel, slabs and fur had them: noise read at 0.05 or 0.08 of its period, a slab's shade taken round a count one longer than the course). So every lattice in a recipe is a whole number of cells across the map, each octave twice the last. `patterns.ts`: the stone, wood and steel's patterns after sdf-harness's materials (its `patterns.ts` at beed9c8, MIT; the owner's): its hash, value and fractal noise, cells, ashlar spans and joints, each lattice wrapped round the map and made once as a table of its hashes (hashed at each pixel, and with `Math.hypot`, the bark took four times main's: a table and `Math.sqrt`, twice); and its solid noise as it has it (`valueNoise3`, which needn't tile), the shape of the forest's rocks (`boulderGeo` in `world/props.ts`). The recipes after it (`ashlar`, `flagstone`, `marble`, `slate`, `steel`, `wood`, `bark`, `rock`, `granite`, `limestone`, `moss`, `soil` in `pbr.ts`; `soil` is the procedural forest floor's soil without its leaves and twigs, grit poking out of it) take its patterns' shapes and its presets' numbers in the tile's own unit (a block, a slab, a furrow), their colours darkened to the arena's: a new look keeps its map's means near the one it replaces (`lab maps` lists them), as each biome's light was set for them. A recipe may take its colours from a photo (`photos/AGENTS.md`), decoded where it runs before it runs (`photosFor`), its brightness the height or a height of its own: `bark` is the owner's oak photo, `forestFloor` and `cobblestone` Poly Haven's scans (CC0, colour and height, `photoGround`: 72 and 91 ms, where the procedural floors took 1325 and 556), each recipe's procedural map kept as the map made where its photo fails. How big a map's tile comes out on a surface is the surface's UVs' and the map's repeat's, which `pbrMaterialMaps` takes each way (`Repeat`; another repeat of the same maps copies the first's textures, which share their one upload): size a repeat by `npm run lab -- tiles`, which reads each surface's tile in metres (the forest's bark ran a tile round a whole trunk, 2.2 m, and 9 m along a root). A material may layer what sdf-harness calls a substrate (after UE5's) over its maps, as three's `MeshPhysicalMaterial` has them: the forest's moss takes a sheen, its fronds pale at grazing angles; a physical material is a program of its own, so keep a layer to small surfaces and check `lab compiles`. `parallax.ts`: the floors' relief. `engraving.ts`: the heroes' engraved steel and embroidery.
- `audio.ts`: the sound effects, synthesized, and the volumes (#408): everything goes through one output, the master volume, the effects through their own level (`LEVEL` at full) and the music through its own (`musicOutput`). Each is a slider's share squared (its middle a quarter, -12 dB, half as loud as heard: a linear gain did nearly nothing over its top half), eased to with a 30 ms time constant as a slider moves (no zipper noise), saved in the cookie `last-stand-volume`. The lab's offline renders (`playInto`) play at full, whatever the settings. `music.ts`: the music's player.
- **A sound's power is in its mids, not its level** (`audio.ts`): the master's compressor holds every effect under about 0.06 RMS, so a louder layer is only squeezed, and a laptop's speakers play almost nothing under 100 Hz (Void Lance's first drone, a 41 Hz sub, was silence there: from play, "not powerful enough"). Weight comes from what's above 150 Hz: driven saws (`drive`, a soft clipper), noise blasts and flanged roars (`flange`), and the shared hall (`space`). **A spell plays no notes over the music:** Void Lance's chimes, chord, choir and glittering bells played as a second tune in another key (from play, "the background music gets duplicated", "like coins falling"; no second track had started), and so was #413's rumble of saws on A under the beam, climbing an octave as the portal opened (a glissando, its 215 Hz overtone 9 dB over its noise). So Void Lance is noise alone, as raw arcana: a flanged roar, a driven pressure of dark noise, and the arcana crackling (`crackles`: sparse clicks, an electric discharge's spit, which the beam's sparks and Splintering Bolt's fizz are made of), each layer drifting at its own slow rate so a long hold doesn't tire (`lanceInhale` the portal, its spark's crackle going round the ears once a trace; `lanceBurst` the beam bursting out; `lanceDrone` the beam): held, 88% of it is above 150 Hz and no peak stands over 3 dB over its noise (`lab sound lance`). Render a sound offline to hear and measure it with `npm run lab -- sound` (WAV files in `tools/lab/out/sound`, its level, its share above 150 Hz, its most tonal peak, the nodes it made: `playInto` plays the effects into its `OfflineAudioContext`, and the throttles go by the context's own clock for it); and in the game, log `AudioBufferSourceNode.prototype.start` to see what a hold starts (a track is the one over 10 s long). The mage's other spells are built the same way, each the thing it shows: Splintering Bolt a small seeking orb thrown fast at every cast (`boltCast`: a snap, a push of dark noise, a zip falling as it leaves and its fizz trailing off, a little apart in colour and pan each time, as a held button casts one every 0.34 s; #413's thump, a sine falling from 260 Hz, was a kick drum three times a second over the music), and its burst on a foe (`boltBurst`: a pop, a spit of crackle, sparks and the two splits zipping off to either side; a split's own burst smaller, with neither; the shared `boltHit` was a tick the ranger's arrows make too). Held, its cast is about 9 dB over a bow's shot and its bursts some 10 dB over its cast; a cast and its three bursts make about 75 nodes (#413's 22, its bursts a tick each); Starfall's crystals falling and striking (`starfallFalling`, `starfallStrike`), Glacial Nova's crack, icy wind and ice forming as grains of noise (`novaBurst`: its three bell tones read as "a coin"), Maelstrom's burst and its spinning wind and fire for the storm's life (`maelstromBurst`, `maelstromSpin`, stopped as the storm's effect is disposed), and Veilstep's vanishing and forming where he lands (`veilstepPass`, noise alone: its first pop, a sine falling from 320 Hz, read as "a beep"; the dodge keeps `blink`, as `zap` stays shared with the other classes). A held sound winds down, never cuts (from play, "not just have the sound abruptly end ... like a tune down"), but goes with what it's the sound of: Void Lance's beam is gone the frame it's let go, so its sound closes to dark in a fraction of a second (18 dB down in 0.2 s) with a falling whoosh, the last of its crackle and a soft whump (`lanceOut`), the hall ringing on after it (#413's sank two octaves over 1.5 s after the beam had gone), eased out by `setTargetAtTime`, as an exponential ramp to silence is 20 dB down a quarter of the way in and reads as a cut. A one-shot's `decay` (`env`) is the time to -80 dB, so a blow heard as a blow needs 0.3 s or more (the orb's first thump, at 0.16, was a click). A layer that starts late is silent until it does (`blowAt` sets its gain to nothing at the voice's start): a gain node is 1 until its first scheduled value, so Glacial Nova's 22 grains of ice, each meant for its moment over half a second, all played at full from the cast, and Veilstep's forming before he'd gone (the nova measured 5 times louder for it). An offline render's left and right levels over time catch it: a layer at full where it should be silent shows as a level, or a side, that the sound's own layers can't explain. The loading screen's exit has a sound of its own (`logoGust`, from `ui/loading.ts` as the wind starts, the plain fade none): the gust's body, rush and blown snow swept from the right ear to the left over the logo's letters, the swirl rising into the lobby's logo up on the left, a low sigh of air as it lands, timed to `ui/logoWind.ts`'s `SWEEP` and gather. It plays by itself, with nothing to press (from the owner's review of #422: "I dont like two new buttons ... it just needs to autoplay, and be less hammering on the brain"; #422's title menu waited for a click to be heard), wherever the browser lets sound play by then (`whenRunning`: the context running, or resumed within 0.4 s, as Chrome allows on a site visited often or the installed app); before a first visit's first click or key it's suspended and the gust isn't played, as a sound asked for then is heard at that click, in the lobby. It's soft, as the first sound a visit hears: rising over 0.7 s, its air opening no higher than 1.3 kHz, gently flanged, the swirl rising to 1.5 kHz and easing away, no pulses (#422's first take rushed to 3.2 kHz with a fizz of snow over 5 kHz flickering 11 times a second, and its swirl was cut off in 0.15 s: `lab sound logoGust` read its loudest half second -22 dB, its centroid 5.6 kHz with 74% of it above 2 kHz; now -34 dB, 1.5 kHz and 22%).
- `frameLoop.ts`: the game's frames, each as the browser asks for one (vsync, the default) or uncapped (the pause menu's Vsync: Off, the cookie `last-stand-vsync`, `?vsync=0` for a visit), each drawn as soon as the last is done, through a `MessageChannel` (a nested timeout is held to 4 ms); a hidden tab always waits for the browser. Uncapped is for measuring: the screen shows its own rate either way, but synced to a 60 Hz screen the GPU's time a frame read 14 ms where it was 7, waiting for the screen to take the frame, and a faster build looked like a slower one once both reached the screen's rate. The owner's lobby (stalls, `this,this?vsync=0`, the overlay read): synced 137 to 142 fps, the GPU 5.57 ms; uncapped 178 to 182, the GPU 3.51 ms, the CPU's 5.5 to 6 ms holding it. The perf report and the traces say `vsync off`, and its frames never set the display's rate (`refreshOf`). Uncapped, a frame waits for the GPU to be done with all but the last frame sent (`AHEAD`, a fence in each frame's commands: `glFence`, `gpuFence`, set by `renderer.ts`): begun as soon as the last frame's commands were sent, a GPU-bound game queued 4 or 5 frames ahead of the GPU and a press waited behind them all (from play: "Vsync off raises FPS but makes the game feel laggy"): 152 ms from the key to the GPU done with the frame that read it, against 34 synced; now 22 (`npm run stalls -- --scenario latency --url "this?vsync=1,this?vsync=0" --window 2560x1440 --scale 2`). On WebGPU 29 ms without the fence, 16 with it (`onSubmittedWorkDone`; WebGPURenderer's WebGL 2 backend, `?renderer=webgpu-gl`, takes the GL fence). Where the CPU is the slower side (`--cpu-slowdown 4`) no frame waits and nothing is lost: none of some 600 frames a round waited, the lag and frames a second a fence-less loop's (`?ahead=99`) within the noise. Compare such settings on one build in turns (`?ahead=N`, `?frameWaits` counting the frames that waited): two builds compared so read the fence 20 ms and 10% of the frames worse, which the waits, none, showed was noise between the builds. `holdFrames(true)` holds the game's steps while the loop keeps asking for frames: the profiler (`src/perf/profiler.ts`) draws its own frames meanwhile. `renderer.ts` `turnBy` turns the camera round what it follows in any view (watching a co-op run).
- `timers.ts`: the game-time scheduler (it respects pause, unlike `setTimeout`). `cookies.ts`: small preferences.
- `tuning.ts`: values a dev build changes while it runs (`tunable(name, values)`; the lab's `--tune name.field=...` sets them before a command): a pose or a camera compared across values without editing and booting again. ## Gotchas

- **Bright effects:** light adds up, so the final grade (`core/renderer.ts`) compresses it the way the eye does, and effects and their lights stay at full strength: don't dim single effects to fix a bright scene.
  - **Adaptation:** after bloom, the average light over a 16x9 and a 4x3 grid of the frame (`AreaPass`) is held under per-view targets (`ADAPT`, just above each view's ordinary scenes, which it leaves untouched) by compressing only the light above `ADAPT_BASE`, on a power curve. Never scale a whole pixel by its area's brightness (the dim ground round an effect darkens into a shadow around it), and don't adapt before bloom (effects drop under bloom's threshold and turn into flat discs with no glow). Above `HI_KNEE` the brightest channel eases towards `HI_CAP`, and in a large glare tone mapping keeps a bright pixel's colour (`HUE_*`, `GLARE_*`), so a big bright area stays green or blue instead of turning white.
  - **Distance:** effect materials dim with their distance from the camera (`nearGlow` in `core/materials.ts`: full strength from the top-down camera's distance, down to a quarter up close). `additive()`, the glow particles, projectile cores (a hostile bolt's is an orb of added light, with a pooled light when one is free; the spore throwers' are `fx/sporeOrb.ts`, the Rift Witch's and the Colossus's `fx/riftBolts.ts`: blended orbs, with added-light tails and halos), entity glows (`kit.glow`, the staff orb included; a hero's gear glows opt out) and the hit flash on bodies have it; wrap a new effect material in `nearGlow()`. The world's own glows don't.
  - **First person:** particles fade within 3.5m of the camera, a bolt flying at the camera shrinks away over its last metres (never pops out: nothing near the camera may switch off at once), and a cast leaves from `Player.castPoint` / `palmPoint`, which in first person are the weapon and hand in view. Keep lights off the camera: above the head, not at 2m. An effect round the body (a heal, a war cry) is seen from inside it: `Player.eyes` tells a skill it is, so it can put its particles where they're seen (a wider ring) and its light ahead of the view (`Player.lightAhead`; straight above, the weapons' steel mirrors it back into the view). The highlights on the hero's own materials are eased there from half of `SHINE.eyes` towards it (`core/materials.ts`, `kit.u.uShine`, set each frame by his player, 0 in the other views): a hand's breadth from the eyes, the sun on a blade's flat blew it out white and bloomed over 7.7% of the frame, its rougher grime left as dark blots across it. `lab haze` measures it.
  - Nothing large may flash or pulse faster than about 1.5 times a second: 3+ flashes a second is a photosensitivity hazard. A hit's flash on a body is full at most every 0.7s (`hitFlash` in `util.ts`). A pattern moving across the view flashes too: Maelstrom's wide ribbons passing a point 4 times a second, and its lashes of lightning at every damage tick, each flashed a sixteenth of the screen 3 to 4 times a second.
  - **Measure an effect** with `npm run lab -- cast <skill>` (`--class`, against main: commit, swap the skill's files in from `origin/main`, run, swap back): every frame of it drawn and read back in each biome and view, with the biome's foes coming at the hero. Its light is held to the budget from the owner's screenshots (it raises the frame's mean by 50 at most, its brightest sixteenth 150, 5% of the frame bright, as stored), its flashes are counted as WCAG counts them (a sixteenth of the screen each), and the shaders compiled after loading must be none. Read its timeline before changing anything: a mean that leapt as Maelstrom's first tick landed in first person was a dark foe in front of the camera dying, and a bright rock behind it showing.
- **The low-Health look** (`core/lowLife.ts`, the grade in `core/renderer.ts`): under half of his most Health the local hero's frame drains towards grey and blood seeps in from the screen's edges, both growing as the Health falls (the drain to 90% at none), with a heartbeat of 0.9 to 1.4 a second (none with reduced motion). It's all uniforms of the final grade (`setLowLife`), no shader change. The blood is vector shapes painted once on a canvas for the screen's shape (`core/bloodEdges.ts`: a seep with a wavy front in from every edge, deepest in the corners, drips from the top, droplets and clots, from one seed), into a mask whose green is how near to nothing the Health must be for each pixel to show, so the grade shows what the Health reaches; nothing reaches further in than 0.29 of the screen's height (`MOST_IN`). Painted again only once a new size has held for 250 ms. Page elements over the canvas would repaint every frame (see the overlays' gotcha). Measure with `npm run lab -- health` (`--canary`): at none, 65 to 83% of the colour drained and blood on 19 to 26% of the screen, none in its middle.
- **Canvas size:** `fitViewport` in `core/renderer.ts` fits the canvas, render targets and camera on resize *and* every frame, and ignores zero sizes. Moving the window between monitors can report a zero height mid-move (zero-sized render targets: a black screen until the next resize) or change the pixel ratio with no resize event. Read the window size through it, not `innerWidth`/`innerHeight` directly, in anything that feeds the camera. The canvas is the screen's own pixels, and the scene and its post chain render the quality's share of them (`sceneRatio`, `sceneSize`), the last pass scaling it up: anything sized in the scene's pixels (the aim laser's width) reads those, not the canvas's. Check a screen with the lab's `--screen` (`1280x720@3`: a 4K screen at 300%), which puts the page on it as a move between monitors does, and two with `--screens`.
- **HDR output** (`core/hdr.ts`, the pause menu's HDR: Auto or Off): on a display in HDR mode (`dynamic-range: high`) the frame's highlights go past the screen's white, and everything under them shows as on an SDR screen.
  - **The way out:** a WebGL canvas can't be shown in extended range (Chrome 149 has no tone mapping mode for it), so its drawing buffer is made float16 (`drawingBufferStorage`, with `EXT_color_buffer_float` on, or it stays 8-bit) and each frame is copied onto a WebGPU canvas configured `toneMapping: { mode: 'extended' }` (`copyExternalImageToTexture`, on the GPU, at the end of `render()`). The copy takes the colour as it is (`premultipliedAlpha: true`: the frame's alpha is 0, the canvas drawn as light added over the page, and by default the copy divided the colour by it). The WebGL canvas stays under the HDR one, invisible, and still takes the pointer. Both canvases hold extended sRGB, encoded (0.5 shows as 127, past 1 brighter than white). On WebGPU (`webgpu.ts`) the renderer's own canvas is made in extended range as it starts (`initHdrForWebGPU`), and the grade's last node does what the uniforms below do; three configures its canvas once, so a switch shows after a reload there.
  - **The grade** does the rest in uniforms, never a shader change: under `HDR_KNEE` (half of white) the frame is the SDR one as a gamma 2.2 screen shows it (Windows shows SDR content by the sRGB curve, which lifts its shadows), above it the highlights grow by their brightest channel towards the peak (hue kept), all of it at the paper white, and the highlights' cap grows with the room the peak gives over the paper white (`uCap`), or every effect's core would stay at SDR's.
  - **The levels** are shares of the OS's white for SDR content, as no page can read a screen's nits: Brightness (the paper white: lower it if the daylit forest is too bright) and Highlights (the peak), shown only while HDR is on and kept in `last-stand-hdr`. `?hdr=1` puts it on with any display (on an SDR one, past white is clipped), `?hdr=0` never.
  - **Measure** with `npm run lab -- light --hdr` (every pixel under the knee against the SDR frame at the paper white, nothing past the peak, the canvas shown read back against the frame drawn, with `--canary`) and the SDR frames with `light --ab`, which agree with main line for line. What it costs (a float16 drawing buffer and a copy of the frame a frame) hasn't been measured on a phone, where HDR displays are common.
- **The environment's reflection is shadowed** (`shadeEnvironment` in `renderer.ts`, three's lit shaders' chunks patched as the renderer starts): three lights a surface's reflection of the environment whatever stands between it and the sky, and each biome's environment has a bright panel where its sun or moon stands, so the crypt's glossy marble walks shone as bright in a column's shadow as out of it. The sun's shadow on a point now leaves a quarter of that reflection (`ENV_IN_SHADOW`) and half of the ambient light (`AMBIENT_IN_SHADOW`: the marble is lit mostly by its ambient, and with the reflection alone shadowed a box's shadow on a walk still hardly showed). The shadows' edges are soft by a fixed width on the ground (`PENUMBRA` in `quality.ts`: the shadow map's radius scaled by its size, so each quality level blurs as wide). Through the eyes a tree's shadow across a walk now shows (`lab light --ab`: only shaded pixels change; the frames' means fall by 0.010 at most, under the forest's canopy, and by 0.001 in the crypt). A three that changes the chunks logs an error and leaves the reflection unshadowed.

What's left, largest first: the hero 55 to 70k (his two hands 12.5k each, meshed on a 3.2 mm grid whatever the level), the forest's thicket 28k, its four stumps 26k, the trees' bark and leaves, the crypt's small dead trees and roots 27k and its three columns 25k. **A level cuts the same on both renderers** (from play: "cut same effects on same quality level, on webgl and webgpu, in a similar way, so they look roughly similar"): a cut is a count, an `autoUpdate` or a uniform both draw by. - **MSAA follows the screen's density** (`samplesOn` in `renderer.ts`): a level's samples divided by the screen's pixels per css px, to 4, 2 or none (High's 4 is 2 at a ratio of 2 and none at 3; Medium's 2 none from 2), as a dense screen's pixels are already as fine as the samples would make an edge, and its samples cost the most (a 4K laptop at 300% drew 6.1 Mpx with 4 samples a pixel, its frame 119 ms, trace 11CW-PY3R). - **A point light lights only where it reaches** (`POINT_LIGHTS_LOOPED` in `renderer.ts`): three works out every point light's lighting at every lit pixel, its colour 0 or not, so each pixel paid for all 20 in view (the biome's 10, the pool's 8 sitting at intensity 0, the hero's own two), each past its `distance` too. The lighting (and a casting light's shadow lookup) is skipped where its colour came out 0 (three's own `directLight.visible`), which changes no pixel (`lab light --ab`: all 18 pictures the same): in the forest at 4.4 Mpx on Low's settings the point lights fell from 2.29 ms of a 3.9 ms scene pass to 0.96 (`lab scenecost --ab`, the owner's 4070 Ti). A laptop's integrated GPU spent 86% of its frame on that pass. On WebGPU the same test passes over them in three's `DynamicLighting` loop (`loopPassesUnreached` in `webgpu.ts`, three's `PointLightDataNode` setup with it): it worked out the physical model's whole direct term for every light at every pixel, and at a phone's pixels the forest's point lights took 13.8 ms of the scene's 18.2, 5.0 after (`lab passes --features --device phone --level verylow`; `--ab=HEAD`'s pictures the same); on the owner's phone the lit shading was 61.5 ms of a 78.7 ms frame (trace V08X-13SR). Give every point light a `distance`: one with none (0) lights every pixel. What's left of their cost is the loop over them, not their lighting: at a 4K laptop's 8.3 Mpx on Low (trace D5RK-S3DD: 16 fps, the GPU 57 ms a frame) the forest's point lights read 1.4 to 1.8 ms of a 4.3 ms scene pass, and their lighting where they reach 0.08 (`lab scenecost`'s "where they reach": the same programs, the lights put out of reach). So an idle light, or a point past a light's reach, is passed over in the unshadowed loop on a test of the distance's square, before three works out its direction, distance and attenuation (`toLight`; the same pixels: `lab light --ab` and `--boot low`, all 18 the same): the scene pass about 15% shorter in the forest and 9% in the crypt (`lab scenecost --ab --fresh`, 4.25 to 3.6 ms and 2.15 to 1.95, on a GPU another session was using). A light the loop passes over is tested on one packed value, read before the light itself (`lightBounds.ts`: each light's place as the camera sees it and its reach's square, 0 for one that lights nothing, in a list every lit material shares, packed anew at each render for its camera; the loop's bound stays a constant): 13% of the scene pass with the GPU loaded, the crypt's 6.49 ms to 5.64 and the forest's 13.78 to 11.98 at 20.7 Mpx on a 4080 laptop (`lab scenecost --screen 2880x1800@2`, ±0.03), the pixels the same (`lab light --ab`). Timed at a phone's 4.5 Mpx, where that GPU idles, it read as nothing (±0.25): time a change of this kind loaded. What was tried and is out: a loop over a list of the lit lights alone, its bound a uniform and each light read through the list (the forest's pass 3.8 ms to 6.5: a loop the compiler can't unroll, over a struct array indexed by a value it can't know, costs more a light than the constant loop it replaced, though it visited half as many), and the opaque draws sorted by program before material (within the noise), and a grid of the lights reaching each cell of the floor, a bit a light in a texture read once a pixel, the loop passing a light over on its bit (7% under the same shader with every bit set, but nothing under main: the read and the bits cost what they saved over the packed test, the crypt 5.84 ms against main's 5.68). **A read that takes the screen's gradients can't be left out by a branch**: a plain `texture()` under an `if` is worked out at every pixel and then picked (D3D's compiler flattens the branch, as the gradients must exist on both sides), so the two casting lights' shadows, five taps each (three's soft edges), were read at every lit pixel, in the light's reach or not: 1.3 ms of the crypt's 5.7 ms scene pass and 2.6 of the forest's 12.6. Their taps are read at the map's one level (`textureGrad` with no gradients, `pointShadowInReach` in `renderer.ts`; a shadow map has no other level, so the pixels are the same: `lab light --ab` at High and at Low, all 18), and the branch then leaves them out: the scene pass 14% shorter in the crypt and 17% in the forest, at every level. An `if`/`else` round a plain `texture()` saved nothing at all, and an early `return` before it made the five taps 0.25 ms slower: check a branch round a texture read by its row in `lab scenecost`, never by reading the shader. **The still shadows keep their five soft taps at every level**: read once on Low and Very low (no radius: 6 to 7% of the scene pass more) the fires were blinding in some frames (from play, on #490's preview; `lab fire` and `lab light` read nothing of it, 0.02 to 0.16% of a frame's pixels changed). A fire's light sways with each puff (`LIGHT_SWAY` in `world/fire.ts`) while its shadow map stays as drawn, so by the bowl and the logs, where the light is strongest, a single hard read flips between lit and dark as it sways, which the five taps' blur hides (the likely cause: not reproduced here). WebGPU's taps are its nodes'. A shorter reach on Low saved 0.02 to 0.05 ms more, and the still shadows left unread 0.04 to 0.19, with the brazier lighting its own plinth through its bowl: neither was kept.
- **Bloom on Low draws at half the scene's pixels each way** (`bloomShare`, `setBloomShare` in `renderer.ts`: its targets sized again and its blurs' steps, uniforms): its bright pass, five levels' blurs and composite draw a quarter of the pixels, each level as wide on the screen as the next one up was (from play, an S25 Edge's bloom 6 ms of an 80 ms frame at full res; by night it adds under half a thousandth of white to the frame's mean either way). - **Compiling is a first visit's cost, a lit program's most** (`npm run lab -- programs --cost`: each of the boot's programs compiled again alone, salted so no cache answers, its canary the cache): ANGLE's D3D compiler took about 0.56 s over a lit program written out for 16 point lights, 0.05 s over an unlit one. three writes the point lights' loop out once a light (`#pragma unroll_loop_start`, for a light's shadow map); so only the two point lights that cast (each biome's warmest, `staticShadow` in `world/props.ts`; three puts them first) are written out and the rest are lit in a loop (`loopPointLights` in `renderer.ts`: with one casting, three wrote every point light out again): the programs' 40 s of compiling fell to 22 to 27 s, the pictures and the frame's GPU time the same. - **Behind the lobby, a warm-up is a step a frame and draws only its part** (`warmUp`'s `step`, `drawn`, `giveUpMs`): each root's compile in slices of about 8 ms, a few meshes at a time (`COMPILE_SLICE_BEHIND_MS`, `onlyThese`: the rest's materials set aside for the call, as three's compile passes over a mesh with none; a part's root compiled in one task held an S25 Edge's village 160 to 215 ms a part, its perf traces, and its longest step is the warm-up's `compileStepMs`, which `stalls` lists), a 2 ms slice of uploads, each program's uniforms read and the draw in a frame of its own (each cached program is read back on the GPU process's main thread, which draws every frame of the browser, and a whole part's in one frame dropped several), its part alone on the warm-ups' layer into a pixel of the target (the scene drawn again for each part held the lobby up), and what it adds kept out of the lobby's frames till its programs are linked: three links a program as it first draws it and waits there, so a foe seated in its crowd (which draws it whatever the camera sees) or an object unculled before its link had the lobby's frame wait for it (a return's lobby drew 18 to 36 frames a second). What a draw itself makes (a shadow's depth programs, the outline's masks) still links inside it. `npm run stalls -- --scenario boot` counts the lobby's frames a second as the perf overlay does (`--cold-return` for a return whose fight was never compiled).
- **Shaders ANGLE compiles at a draw** (D3D11; `npm run stalls -- --scenario boot --angle`: `D3DCompile` on the GPU process's main thread): a vertex attribute of integers read as floats is a format D3D11 converts, and a `flat` varying under GL's last-vertex convention needs a geometry shader, and for each ANGLE compiled a shader of its own at the program's first draw, on the main thread, holding every frame up (a first visit's 760 ms). So the bones' indices are floats (`models/shapes.ts`, `handShape.ts`) and a `flat` varying is the first vertex's (`firstVertexProvokes` in `renderer.ts`, WEBGL_provoking_vertex: the crowds' rows, the same at a triangle's every vertex). - **Shadows' programs don't hang on the casters' order** (`steadyShadowPrograms` in `shaders.ts`, at the renderer's set-up): three draws every caster that tests no alpha with one shared depth material, copies the caster's `map` onto it, and builds it a new program only when the instancing, instance colours or skinning change between draws, so which variant (with a map, or without) a caster got hung on what was drawn before it, and a new order mid-fight compiled one more. The map, which does nothing without an alpha test, is let go before each such draw. The pass's own materials then find them made. What the shadow map alone draws (`inShadowsOnly` in `world/culling.ts`) is left out of the colour compile (`withoutUndrawn`, with the crowds' bodies' own meshes). `npm run lab -- programs` lists each program a draw made: the boot's are 0 (#275's: 32).

In px per css px, a 4K screen at 300% (1280x720 css px) rendered 1920x1080 stretched to 4K, where at 100% the same screen rendered 4K, on an RTX 4080 that drew the frame in 3.3 ms. - **The floors' relief** (`core/parallax.ts`, parallax occlusion): a floor's height, kept in its roughness map's red channel (`buildPBR` in `core/pbr.ts`: no material reads it, roughness is green), is walked down along the view ray and its maps read where the ray meets it, so the crypt's grout and the forest's soil lie sunk below the cobbles' and pebbles' tops. - **Music** (`core/music.ts`, `data/music.ts`): each biome has a track for its lobby and one for its waves' fights (`BiomeDef.music`): the Thornwood's lobby plays Quiet Beginnings and its fights Sunlit Forest Battle, the crypt's Shadowed Forest Dusk and Crypt Motif. The lobby's track plays in the lobby and in a run wherever there's no fight: the countdowns, the choice after a wave, the summary. The battle track comes in as each wave's fight starts (`G.run.phase === 'fighting'`) and fades out once the wave is won, the lobby's coming back as it goes (`FADES`: the lobby's in over 3 s and out over 1.2, the battle's in over 1 and out over 2.5). Every track comes back where it was left (the lobby's between wave 1 and 2 where wave 1's fight left it, the battle's on from wave to wave), from its top only once it was let go: a new run's first wave, a biome switched to in the lobby, which crossfades the two lobbies'. Each track is a loop of its piece made by `npm run music -- make` (`tools/music/README.md`, which says how each was cut: Crypt Motif has no repeats, and loops whole): the piece up to where it comes back, a crossfade into the loop's start and a second more of it, looped from just past the crossfade to as far past the loop's end (`TRACKS`' `loop`), so both sides of the seam are the same samples whatever delay a decoder leaves at the file's start (an audio element's loop left a gap of the MP3's padding). MP3s at 44.1 kHz, LAME's VBR quality 5, about 110 kbps (Opus would be smaller, but iOS Safari plays it unreliably), 4.9 MB for the four. Each is fetched whole and decoded when it's first wanted (a run's battle track as the run starts, so its first wave doesn't wait; through a run both its tracks stay decoded), looped by a buffer source and faded by a gain node of its own (iOS ignores an element's own volume) through the effects' WebAudio context, and its decoded samples (20 MB a minute) are let go once it's silent and wanted nowhere. The music is asked to start as the lobby shows (a browser lets it start before the page's first click or key only where it allows autoplay, as Chrome does on a site visited often or an installed app; elsewhere that first gesture starts it), and stops in a hidden tab, playing on from there when it comes back. Measure with `npm run lab -- music` (`--canary`): each biome's lobby, a run's countdowns, fights and wins, a hidden tab and the bank back, judged by what reaches WebAudio and what each track's output carries.
