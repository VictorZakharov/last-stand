# `src/core`

- `inputDelivery.ts` aggregates key arrival/handler/editing counts, pointer presses, pointer movements and maximum DOM delivery delays once a second during manual recording. Movement without clicks must be counted: windowed trace 65SQ-60GS showed steady frames while the player reported a roughly 5 FPS cursor for ten seconds, without clicking or testing keys. Capture is installed before UI key interceptors. A zero-arrival second with live frames differs from captured keys swallowed before the game's handler. No key codes, coordinates, text or event objects are retained. DOM timestamps cannot measure time spent in a native queue before the browser creates the event.

- `keyDelivery.ts` measures DOM dispatch delay from a valid monotonic timestamp, not the key's value. Ignore missing, future, epoch and non-finite stamps. `main.ts` records at most one delayed-delivery mark a second, plus focus tags; no key codes or typed text enter traces. It cannot alone detect a native queue that delays the event before that timestamp exists; compare manual arrival counts as well.

- `frameLoop.ts`: the game's frames, each as the browser asks for one (vsync, the default) or uncapped (the pause menu's Vsync: Off, the cookie `last-stand-vsync`, `?vsync=0` for a visit), each drawn as soon as the last is done, through a `MessageChannel` (a nested timeout is held to 4 ms); a hidden tab always waits for the browser. Uncapped is for measuring: the screen shows its own rate either way, but synced to a 60 Hz screen the GPU's time a frame read 14 ms where it was 7, waiting for the screen to take the frame, and a faster build looked like a slower one once both reached the screen's rate. The owner's lobby (stalls, `this,this?vsync=0`, the overlay read): synced 137 to 142 fps, the GPU 5.57 ms; uncapped 178 to 182, the GPU 3.51 ms, the CPU's 5.5 to 6 ms holding it. The perf report and the traces say `vsync off`, and its frames never set the display's rate (`refreshOf`). Uncapped, a frame waits for the GPU to be done with all but the last frame sent (`AHEAD`, a fence in each frame's commands: `glFence`, `gpuFence`, set by `renderer.ts`): begun as soon as the last frame's commands were sent, a GPU-bound game queued 4 or 5 frames ahead of the GPU and a press waited behind them all (from play: "Vsync off raises FPS but makes the game feel laggy"): 152 ms from the key to the GPU done with the frame that read it, against 34 synced; now 22 (`npm run stalls -- --scenario latency --url "this?vsync=1,this?vsync=0" --window 2560x1440 --scale 2`). On WebGPU 29 ms without the fence, 16 with it (`onSubmittedWorkDone`; WebGPURenderer's WebGL 2 backend, `?renderer=webgpu-gl`, takes the GL fence). Where the CPU is the slower side (`--cpu-slowdown 4`) no frame waits and nothing is lost: none of some 600 frames a round waited, the lag and frames a second a fence-less loop's (`?ahead=99`) within the noise. - `timers.ts`: the game-time scheduler (it respects pause, unlike `setTimeout`). `cookies.ts`: small preferences.
- `tuning.ts`: values a dev build changes while it runs (`tunable(name, values)`; the lab's `--tune name.field=...` sets them before a command): a pose or a camera compared across values without editing and booting again. ## Gotchas

- **Bright effects:** light adds up, so the final grade (`core/renderer.ts`) compresses it the way the eye does, and effects and their lights stay at full strength: don't dim single effects to fix a bright scene.
  - **Adaptation:** after bloom, the average light over a 16x9 and a 4x3 grid of the frame (`AreaPass`) is held under per-view targets (`ADAPT`, just above each view's ordinary scenes, which it leaves untouched) by compressing only the light above `ADAPT_BASE`, on a power curve. Never scale a whole pixel by its area's brightness (the dim ground round an effect darkens into a shadow around it), and don't adapt before bloom (effects drop under bloom's threshold and turn into flat discs with no glow). Above `HI_KNEE` the brightest channel eases towards `HI_CAP`, and in a large glare tone mapping keeps a bright pixel's colour (`HUE_*`, `GLARE_*`), so a big bright area stays green or blue instead of turning white.
  - **Distance:** effect materials dim with their distance from the camera (`nearGlow` in `core/materials.ts`: full strength from the top-down camera's distance, down to a quarter up close). `additive()`, the glow particles, projectile cores (a hostile bolt's is an orb of added light, with a pooled light when one is free; the spore throwers' are `fx/sporeOrb.ts`, the Rift Witch's and the Colossus's `fx/riftBolts.ts`: blended orbs, with added-light tails and halos), entity glows (`kit.glow`, the staff orb included; a hero's gear glows opt out) and the hit flash on bodies have it; wrap a new effect material in `nearGlow()`. The world's own glows don't.
  - **First person:** particles fade within 3.5m of the camera, a bolt flying at the camera shrinks away over its last metres (never pops out: nothing near the camera may switch off at once), and a cast leaves from `Player.castPoint` / `palmPoint`, which in first person are the weapon and hand in view. Keep lights off the camera: above the head, not at 2m. An effect round the body (a heal, a war cry) is seen from inside it: `Player.eyes` tells a skill it is, so it can put its particles where they're seen (a wider ring) and its light ahead of the view (`Player.lightAhead`; straight above, the weapons' steel mirrors it back into the view). The highlights on the hero's own materials are eased there from half of `SHINE.eyes` towards it (`core/materials.ts`, `kit.u.uShine`, set each frame by his player, 0 in the other views): a hand's breadth from the eyes, the sun on a blade's flat blew it out white and bloomed over 7.7% of the frame, its rougher grime left as dark blots across it. `lab haze` measures it.
  - Nothing large may flash or pulse faster than about 1.5 times a second: 3+ flashes a second is a photosensitivity hazard. A hit's flash on a body is full at most every 0.7s (`hitFlash` in `util.ts`). A pattern moving across the view flashes too: Maelstrom's wide ribbons passing a point 4 times a second, and its lashes of lightning at every damage tick, each flashed a sixteenth of the screen 3 to 4 times a second.
  - **Measure an effect** with `npm run lab -- cast <skill>` (`--class`, against main: commit, swap the skill's files in from `origin/main`, run, swap back): every frame of it drawn and read back in each biome and view, with the biome's foes coming at the hero. Its light is held to the budget from the owner's screenshots (it raises the frame's mean by 50 at most, its brightest sixteenth 150, 5% of the frame bright, as stored), its flashes are counted as WCAG counts them (a sixteenth of the screen each), and the shaders compiled after loading must be none. Read its timeline before changing anything: a mean that leapt as Maelstrom's first tick landed in first person was a dark foe in front of the camera dying, and a bright rock behind it showing.
- **The low-Health look** (`core/lowLife.ts`, the grade in `core/renderer.ts`): under half of his most Health the local hero's frame drains towards grey and blood seeps in from the screen's edges, both growing as the Health falls (the drain to 90% at none), with a heartbeat of 0.9 to 1.4 a second (none with reduced motion). It's all uniforms of the final grade (`setLowLife`), no shader change. The blood is vector shapes painted once on a canvas for the screen's shape (`core/bloodEdges.ts`: a seep with a wavy front in from every edge, deepest in the corners, drips from the top, droplets and clots, from one seed), into a mask whose green is how near to nothing the Health must be for each pixel to show, so the grade shows what the Health reaches; nothing reaches further in than 0.29 of the screen's height (`MOST_IN`). Painted again only once a new size has held for 250 ms. Page elements over the canvas would repaint every frame (see the overlays' gotcha). Measure with `npm run lab -- health` (`--canary`): at none, 65 to 83% of the colour drained and blood on 19 to 26% of the screen, none in its middle.
- **Canvas size:** `fitViewport` in `core/renderer.ts` fits the canvas, render targets and camera on resize *and* every frame, and ignores zero sizes. Moving the window between monitors can report a zero height mid-move (zero-sized render targets: a black screen until the next resize) or change the pixel ratio with no resize event. Read the window size through it, not `innerWidth`/`innerHeight` directly, in anything that feeds the camera. The canvas is the screen's own pixels, and the scene and its post chain render the quality's share of them (`sceneRatio`, `sceneSize`), the last pass scaling it up: anything sized in the scene's pixels (the aim laser's width) reads those, not the canvas's. Check a screen with the lab's `--screen` (`1280x720@3`: a 4K screen at 300%), which puts the page on it as a move between monitors does, and two with `--screens`.
- **HDR output** (`core/hdr.ts`, the pause menu's HDR: Auto or Off): on a display in HDR mode (`dynamic-range: high`) the frame's highlights go past the screen's white, and everything under them shows as on an SDR screen.
  - **The way out:** a WebGL canvas can't be shown in extended range (Chrome 149 has no tone mapping mode for it), so its drawing buffer is made float16 (`drawingBufferStorage`, with `EXT_color_buffer_float` on, or it stays 8-bit) and each frame is copied onto a WebGPU canvas configured `toneMapping: { mode: 'extended' }` (`copyExternalImageToTexture`, on the GPU, at the end of `render()`). The copy takes the colour as it is (`premultipliedAlpha: true`: the frame's alpha is 0, the canvas drawn as light added over the page, and by default the copy divided the colour by it). The WebGL canvas stays under the HDR one, invisible, and still takes the pointer. Both canvases hold extended sRGB, encoded (0.5 shows as 127, past 1 brighter than white). On WebGPU (`webgpu.ts`) the renderer's own canvas is made in extended range as it starts (`initHdrForWebGPU`), and the grade's last node does what the uniforms below do; three configures its canvas once, so a switch shows after a reload there.
  - **The grade** does the rest in uniforms, never a shader change: under `HDR_KNEE` (half of white) the frame is the SDR one as a gamma 2.2 screen shows it (Windows shows SDR content by the sRGB curve, which lifts its shadows), above it the highlights grow by their brightest channel towards the peak (hue kept), all of it at the paper white, and the highlights' cap grows with the room the peak gives over the paper white (`uCap`), or every effect's core would stay at SDR's.
  - **The levels** are shares of the OS's white for SDR content, as no page can read a screen's nits: Brightness (the paper white: lower it if the daylit forest is too bright) and Highlights (the peak), shown only while HDR is on and kept in `last-stand-hdr`. `?hdr=1` puts it on with any display (on an SDR one, past white is clipped), `?hdr=0` never.
  - **Measure** with `npm run lab -- light --hdr` (every pixel under the knee against the SDR frame at the paper white, nothing past the peak, the canvas shown read back against the frame drawn, with `--canary`) and the SDR frames with `light --ab`, which agree with main line for line. What it costs (a float16 drawing buffer and a copy of the frame a frame) hasn't been measured on a phone, where HDR displays are common.
- **The environment's reflection is shadowed** (`shadeEnvironment` in `renderer.ts`, three's lit shaders' chunks patched as the renderer starts): three lights a surface's reflection of the environment whatever stands between it and the sky, and each biome's environment has a bright panel where its sun or moon stands, so the crypt's glossy marble walks shone as bright in a column's shadow as out of it. The sun's shadow on a point now leaves a quarter of that reflection (`ENV_IN_SHADOW`) and half of the ambient light (`AMBIENT_IN_SHADOW`: the marble is lit mostly by its ambient, and with the reflection alone shadowed a box's shadow on a walk still hardly showed). The shadows' edges are soft by a fixed width on the ground (`PENUMBRA` in `quality.ts`: the shadow map's radius scaled by its size, so each quality level blurs as wide). Through the eyes a tree's shadow across a walk now shows (`lab light --ab`: only shaded pixels change; the frames' means fall by 0.010 at most, under the forest's canopy, and by 0.001 in the crypt). A three that changes the chunks logs an error and leaves the reflection unshadowed.
- **Quality presets** (`data/quality.ts`, pause menu, Auto by default) change the scene's pixel ratio, MSAA, shadow map size, which small character parts cast shadows, the floors' relief (its steps a uniform) and how far apart the village's grass stands (`grassSpread`: its cells, tiles and strands uniforms, a geometry swapped) and the lamps' reach and shadows (`lampReach`, `lampShadows`: the list's reaches and its shadows' flag, `setLampQuality` in `lamps.ts`) and whether the village's kept shadow maps are drawn over only as what moves moves (`shadowsOnMove`, `world/shadowsOnMove.ts`) and the share of the scene's pixels bloom draws at (`bloomShare`, `setBloomShare` in `renderer.ts`: its targets sized again and its blurs' steps, uniforms; Low's half each way, from play an S25 Edge's bloom 6 ms of an 80 ms frame: its passes draw a quarter of the pixels, each level as wide on the screen as the next one up was, and by night in the village it adds under half a thousandth of white to the frame's mean either way, `lab village --only bloomOnLow`; WebGL's alone, WebGPU's bloom is its own) and the rounds a skirt's cloth is draped over the limbs a frame (`drapeRounds`, `setDrapeRounds` in `entities/models/armor.ts`: the drape a third of the hero's update, Low's 2 against 4, its last pass off the limbs kept), all live and without shader changes. **Very low** is under Low, for a device that still lags there (from play: "a very low graphics mode, even lower than low... all graphics reduced, effects that use less GPU processing"): a 512 shadow map, only parts 0.3 m across or more casting, the sun's shadow drawn every other frame at any frame rate (`shadowInTurns`, `setShadowInTurns` in `renderer.ts`, WebGL too), the heroes at a fifth of their detail, leg IK within 6 m, no bloom at all (`bloomShare` 0: WebGL leaves its pass out, WebGPU adds its glow at no strength; at a quarter of the scene's pixels each way its glow flickered as the view moved, and from play it still did once its bright pass read every pixel: "maybe remove the bloom ?"), a burst's, a puff's and debris' particles halved (`particles`, `setParticleShare` in `fx/particles.ts`), the sun's shadow of what stands still alone, drawn only when that changes (`stillSunShadow`, `setSunShadowStill` in `world/props.ts`: the heroes and foes cast none by it), a third of the floor's clutter (`clutter`, `setClutterShare` in `world/culling.ts`), and its foliage lean (`leanFoliage`, `world/AGENTS.md`: no weeds, ferns or fallen leaves, the ground's own look where they stood, the grass kept (from the owner: "remove no grass", #610); - **HDR is High's alone** (`QualityPreset.hdr`, `setHdrAllowed` in `hdr.ts`; the pause menu's HDR says "(off below High)" where the level keeps it off): its way out, a float16 drawing buffer copied onto the HDR canvas every frame at the screen's own pixels, was 26 ms of a 38 ms frame on a laptop's integrated GPU with a 4K panel, the scene 4.6 (trace BN6P-FN12), and 7.1 to 9.0 ms of 12 on the owner's 4070 Ti at the same screen (`lab scenecost --frame --screen 1280x720@3`). The copy is far slower than its bytes: making it cheap is its own work.
- **MSAA follows the screen's density** (`samplesOn` in `renderer.ts`): a level's samples divided by the screen's pixels per css px, to 4, 2 or none (High's and Medium's 2 none from a ratio of 2; a 4 would be 2 at 2 and none at 3), as a dense screen's pixels are already as fine as the samples would make an edge, and its samples cost the most (a 4K laptop at 300% drew 6.1 Mpx with 4 samples a pixel, its frame 119 ms, trace 11CW-PY3R). Fitted again when the window moves to a screen of another density; the perf report and the traces say the samples used. WebGPU's are chosen once.
- **A point light lights only where it reaches** (`POINT_LIGHTS_LOOPED` in `renderer.ts`): three works out every point light's lighting at every lit pixel, its colour 0 or not, so each pixel paid for all 20 in view (the biome's 10, the pool's 8 sitting at intensity 0, the hero's own two), each past its `distance` too. The lighting (and a casting light's shadow lookup) is skipped where its colour came out 0 (three's own `directLight.visible`), which changes no pixel (`lab light --ab`: all 18 pictures the same): in the forest at 4.4 Mpx on Low's settings the point lights fell from 2.29 ms of a 3.9 ms scene pass to 0.96 (`lab scenecost --ab`, the owner's 4070 Ti). A laptop's integrated GPU spent 86% of its frame on that pass. On WebGPU the same test passes over them in three's `DynamicLighting` loop (`loopPassesUnreached` in `webgpu.ts`, three's `PointLightDataNode` setup with it): it worked out the physical model's whole direct term for every light at every pixel, and at a phone's pixels the forest's point lights took 13.8 ms of the scene's 18.2, 5.0 after (`lab passes --features --device phone --level verylow`; `--ab=HEAD`'s pictures the same); on the owner's phone the lit shading was 61.5 ms of a 78.7 ms frame (trace V08X-13SR). Give every point light a `distance`: one with none (0) lights every pixel. What's left of their cost is the loop over them, not their lighting: at a 4K laptop's 8.3 Mpx on Low (trace D5RK-S3DD: 16 fps, the GPU 57 ms a frame) the forest's point lights read 1.4 to 1.8 ms of a 4.3 ms scene pass, and their lighting where they reach 0.08 (`lab scenecost`'s "where they reach": the same programs, the lights put out of reach). So an idle light, or a point past a light's reach, is passed over in the unshadowed loop on a test of the distance's square, before three works out its direction, distance and attenuation (`toLight`; the same pixels: `lab light --ab` and `--boot low`, all 18 the same): the scene pass about 15% shorter in the forest and 9% in the crypt (`lab scenecost --ab --fresh`, 4.25 to 3.6 ms and 2.15 to 1.95, on a GPU another session was using). A light the loop passes over is tested on one packed value, read before the light itself (`lightBounds.ts`: each light's place as the camera sees it and its reach's square, 0 for one that lights nothing, in a list every lit material shares, packed anew at each render for its camera; the loop's bound stays a constant): 13% of the scene pass with the GPU loaded, the crypt's 6.49 ms to 5.64 and the forest's 13.78 to 11.98 at 20.7 Mpx on a 4080 laptop (`lab scenecost --screen 2880x1800@2`, ±0.03), the pixels the same (`lab light --ab`). Timed at a phone's 4.5 Mpx, where that GPU idles, it read as nothing (±0.25): time a change of this kind loaded. What was tried and is out: a loop over a list of the lit lights alone, its bound a uniform and each light read through the list (the forest's pass 3.8 ms to 6.5: a loop the compiler can't unroll, over a struct array indexed by a value it can't know, costs more a light than the constant loop it replaced, though it visited half as many), and the opaque draws sorted by program before material (within the noise). A shorter reach on Low saved 0.02 to 0.05 ms more, and the still shadows left unread 0.04 to 0.19, with the brazier lighting its own plinth through its bowl: neither was kept.
- **Bloom on Low draws at half the scene's pixels each way** (`bloomShare`, `setBloomShare` in `renderer.ts`: its targets sized again and its blurs' steps, uniforms): its bright pass, five levels' blurs and composite draw a quarter of the pixels, each level as wide on the screen as the next one up was (from play, an S25 Edge's bloom 6 ms of an 80 ms frame at full res; by night it adds under half a thousandth of white to the frame's mean either way). On WebGPU too (`setBloomShareOnWebGPU` in `webgpu.ts`), and its first level at half the scene's pixels as WebGL's `UnrealBloomPass` draws it (`BLOOM_FIRST_LEVEL`): three's `BloomNode` was set to the scene's full size, so a phone's Very low drew its bright pass and first blur at 3120x1440, and each level was half as wide on the screen as WebGL's (`lab passes --ab --renderer webgpu`: 0.2 to 1% of the pixels, round the fires and glows). **A smaller bloom reads every pixel under it** (`BRIGHT_FROM_SCENE`, `brightTaps`): its bright pass's target is half bloom's size each way and a tap reads 2x2 of the scene's pixels, all of them at High, a quarter at Low and a sixteenth at Very low, so a small bright thing (a dummy's mark, a rune, a flame's tongue) was caught by one frame and missed by the next as the view moved and its whole glow went with it (from play, on main at Very low: "blinding light... even dummies emit it, but it only happens when I'm moving"). WebGL's alone so far: three's bloom node on WebGPU reads as it did. Anything gathered from the scene at a share of its size takes this care: a pass that reads fewer pixels than lie under it flickers as the view moves, and no still picture shows it.
- **A lit program has 40 uniform vectors of room on a phone** (`npm run lab -- programs --limits`): the largest read 184 fragment uniform vectors (20 point lights, their packed bounds, the shadows, the kit's own) against the 224 any WebGL 2 device must give, and an S25 Edge's 256. A desktop gives 1024, so a material that spends more compiles and draws on every PC here and draws nothing on a phone, saying nothing: the heroes' palettes' six tables of 16 rows took them to 272 and the phone's hero was his hair and his shadow (#470, reverted). Tables of values go in a small data texture read by `texelFetch`, not in uniform arrays. Run `--limits` for any change to a material or shader, and read the phone's own report after trying it on a preview: it names the programs its driver refused (`auditPrograms` in `shaders.ts`).
- **Compiling is a first visit's cost, a lit program's most** (`npm run lab -- programs --cost`: each of the boot's programs compiled again alone, salted so no cache answers, its canary the cache): ANGLE's D3D compiler took about 0.56 s over a lit program written out for 16 point lights, 0.05 s over an unlit one. three writes the point lights' loop out once a light (`#pragma unroll_loop_start`, for a light's shadow map); so only the two point lights that cast (each biome's warmest, `staticShadow` in `world/props.ts`; three puts them first) are written out and the rest are lit in a loop (`loopPointLights` in `renderer.ts`: with one casting, three wrote every point light out again): the programs' 40 s of compiling fell to 22 to 27 s, the pictures and the frame's GPU time the same. Keep a material's variants few (a uniform, not a define: the kit's near glow and rim in `materials.ts`, 157 programs to 146), and see what one costs before adding it. A page can't keep a program itself (WebGL has no program binaries): the GPU's process keeps them by their source, so a return reads them back in a few ms, and a deploy that leaves a shader's source as it was keeps its cache. A warm-up asks whether a program is linked only after a fence placed behind its compile has passed (`commandsDone` in `shaders.ts`): each question is a round trip to the GPU's process, and asked while it was reading programs back it held the lobby up 250 ms at a time.
- **Behind the lobby, a warm-up is a step a frame and draws only its part** (`warmUp`'s `step`, `drawn`, `giveUpMs`): each root's compile in slices of about 8 ms, a few meshes at a time (`COMPILE_SLICE_BEHIND_MS`, `onlyThese`: the rest's materials set aside for the call, as three's compile passes over a mesh with none; a part's root compiled in one task held an S25 Edge's village 160 to 215 ms a part, its perf traces, and its longest step is the warm-up's `compileStepMs`, which `stalls` lists), a 2 ms slice of uploads, each program's uniforms read and the draw in a frame of its own (each cached program is read back on the GPU process's main thread, which draws every frame of the browser, and a whole part's in one frame dropped several), its part alone on the warm-ups' layer into a pixel of the target (the scene drawn again for each part held the lobby up), and what it adds kept out of the lobby's frames till its programs are linked: three links a program as it first draws it and waits there, so a foe seated in its crowd (which draws it whatever the camera sees) or an object unculled before its link had the lobby's frame wait for it (a return's lobby drew 18 to 36 frames a second). A program not linked 150 ms after its commands are done is cold: the part is given up, nothing drawn (`cold`). What a draw itself makes (a shadow's depth programs, the outline's masks) still links inside it. `npm run stalls -- --scenario boot` counts the lobby's frames a second as the perf overlay does (`--cold-return` for a return whose fight was never compiled).
- **Shaders ANGLE compiles at a draw** (D3D11; `npm run stalls -- --scenario boot --angle`: `D3DCompile` on the GPU process's main thread): a vertex attribute of integers read as floats is a format D3D11 converts, and a `flat` varying under GL's last-vertex convention needs a geometry shader, and for each ANGLE compiled a shader of its own at the program's first draw, on the main thread, holding every frame up (a first visit's 760 ms). So the bones' indices are floats (`models/shapes.ts`, `handShape.ts`) and a `flat` varying is the first vertex's (`firstVertexProvokes` in `renderer.ts`, WEBGL_provoking_vertex: the crowds' rows, the same at a triangle's every vertex). A return reads such shaders back with the program; keep integer data out of float attributes.
- **Shadows' programs don't hang on the casters' order** (`steadyShadowPrograms` in `shaders.ts`, at the renderer's set-up): three draws every caster that tests no alpha with one shared depth material, copies the caster's `map` onto it, and builds it a new program only when the instancing, instance colours or skinning change between draws, so which variant (with a map, or without) a caster got hung on what was drawn before it, and a new order mid-fight compiled one more. The map, which does nothing without an alpha test, is let go before each such draw. A program compiled mid-fight shows in the perf report by its kind (`depth`: a shadow); `npm run lab -- compiles` breaks every kind of prop and fights a first wave in each biome (`--boot low@518400` as a phone boots) and names the draw that first used it.
- **Shadows' programs are compiled with the rest** (`compileShadows` in `shaders.ts`, from `compileAhead` and `warmUp`): three's compile makes none (its shadow pass makes each as it first draws with it, linked there, the page held up: a first visit's lobby warm-up made 10 inside its draw), so each caster is compiled with a stand-in for the material the pass would draw it with (r185's `getDepthMaterial`: its `customDepthMaterial`, else three's own; for a point light's pass, which a still shadow's is, its `customDistanceMaterial`, else three's distance material, its side, alpha test and map as the pass sets them, the map let go as `steadyShadowPrograms` lets it go), without the scene's fog and environment (the pass draws with neither), and the warm-up waits for their links.
