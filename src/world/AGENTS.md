# `src/world`

The arenas.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- `arena.ts`: every biome built once at boot into a group of its own, and the switch between them. `crypt.ts`, `forest.ts`: the biomes' builders. `props.ts`: what they share (the biome contract, boxes, instancing, the gates' membranes, the environment map, the skies, the static shadow bake, the grass). `culling.ts`: instanced meshes culled cell by cell, what the shadow map alone draws, and work done round each of its draws (`aroundShadows`: a hero's shadow cast as one, `entities/models/castAsOne.ts`) and after it (`afterShadows`: the heroes drawn over the lamps' still shadows, `lampShadows.ts`). `foliage.ts`: the Thornwood's leaf cards. `ezTrees.ts` (with `ezTree.d.ts`, `ezTreeTextures.ts`): its trees. `fire.ts`: a fire burning on a prop (the crypt's braziers, the forest's bonfires). `heroHall.ts`: the hall of heroes' room (the hero selection screen's: a stage the dais's size, plinths, banners in the classes' colours, braziers and torches), built at boot beside the places and shown in the village's place (`Arena.showHall`, the door of the hall on its terrace): as a biome, 10 point lights, two casting the still shadows, the crypt's and the forest's stones (their recipes and programs), its plinths a relief on the stage (`groundHeight` lifts the heroes onto them). `village.ts`: the lobby's village (its own section below), built at boot beside the biomes and shown in their place between runs (`Arena.showVillage`); its plan `villagePlan.ts`, its ways out `villageWays.ts`, its fountain `fountain.ts`, its grass `meadow.ts`.
- `ground.ts`: the walkable floor's height: the dais's tiers, and a biome's relief, what lies on its floor to step onto (`Biome.relief`, set as the arena switches biome): the forest's roots, their tops baked into a 10 cm grid at build (`tubesRelief`, from the curves their tubes follow), so the legs' IK sets feet on them, and foes, arrows and bodies meet them as drawn. `collision.ts`: circles against the props and the wall. `wall.ts`: the wall as it's drawn, which bodies walk up to and what flies meets (`hull.ts`: a shape's convex hull, met fast; `outline.ts`: a hull's outline on the floor, which a body is kept off). `edge.ts`: the wall's face as a shape (the crypt's octagon, the thicket's ring) and its gates' openings. `destructible.ts`: the props that break. `navigation.ts`: how foes find their way.

Adding a biome is in the root's Architecture.

## Gotchas

- **The wall is what's drawn** (`wall.ts`, `Biome.wall`, `G.arena.wall`): a builder adds its wall's meshes and geometries as they stand (`Wall.add`, before `cullInCells`: culling moves an instanced mesh's instances), each as a `wall` (walked up to and flown into), `beyond` (flown into: the forest's trunks, the crypt's tombs and dead trees) or `foliage` (walked up to, flown through: the thicket's bushes swollen partway into their leaves), and the arena seals it once built. Bodies are kept off the thicket's rocks and bushes by their outlines on the floor (`outline.ts`: each hull cut through at a knee's, a hip's and a head's height and between, a convex polygon), and off a line round the ring read at boot by rays out from the middle at those heights, one every quarter of a degree, from the rest (the crypt's walls and buttresses, the gates) and no further than a little past the face (`limit`). Read into that line too, each rock or bush poking in from the thicket hid the floor beside and behind it (the ray from the middle met its front): walking along the thicket the hero met a square face in the open there, and stopped 19 to 23 times a lap round it (`npm run lab -- rim`). The whole line (`reach`, `reachAt`) is still what navigation reads, and the arrows lying on the floor; `roomOut` is how far a body may go straight out, which `slowForWall` brakes by. Kept off the line (`keepIn`: past the line, back onto its nearest point; then a circle off each stretch of it, away from the stretch's nearest point, four times over a frame, so a buttress's side pushes him along it and he settles into a notch between two bushes at once, where kept off its sides in turn once a frame he crept into it for seconds; whether he's past the line is read from the stretch his angle falls in, never a stretch's side alone: a body in a gap between two bushes, past one stretch's end, was taken for through it and put back on its inner side, thrown up to 1.7 m in a frame and held in the gap, `npm run lab -- rim`); a gate's opening reaches its portal, and a gap in the thicket 0.6 m past the face. Kept to a ring at 26.6 m, the hero stopped 0.1 to 3.2 m short of the wall (`npm run lab -- wall`: the crypt's surfaces now within 3 cm, the thicket's leaves within 15 cm short and up to 36 cm into them, never nearer his middle than 8 cm). An arrow's step is cast against the parts in the slices it crosses (`cast`): with no height to the wall, an arrow over the thicket stood in the air at its line (#290; `npm run lab -- beyond --class ranger`). The thicket's bushes and rocks are met by convex hulls (`hull.ts`, `add`'s `hulled`): a bush by each of its three lumps' (`canopyGeo`'s `lumps`: one hull round all three spanned the hollows between them, and an arrow stood in the air over the leaves), walked up to by the same swollen 1.4 times (`leafLumps`, `LEAF_REACH` in `foliage.ts`). Its leaf cards aren't in it: a hull round a lump's cards spanned the air between them, a third of a metre short of the leaves, and the cards as drawn let him in through their gaps, the line as ragged as they are and his legs past their ranges stood against it. Cast against every triangle by three's raycaster the forest's line took 0.7 s at boot, by hulls 0.1 s, and with the outlines 0.16 s (the crypt's 0.02: the outlined parts' hulls are met only short of what else bounds the floor, as the gates' triangles, met one by one, were most of it and were cast twice). The hero slows walking up to the wall (`slowForWall` in `entities/player.ts`: stopped by it in a frame from a run, the legs were thrown past their ranges), his speed into it is let go as it pushes him back, and it holds him from sliding along it within 10° of square on (`WALL_GRIP`: with none, walked into a face nearly square on he slid along it without end; within 31°, along the thicket he stopped at every rock and bush that turned its face 19° toward him; in a corner he slid to and fro at about the speed he turns to his way, a hip turned 103° past its range; `lab wall` names each walk that put a joint past its range). Navigation's grid and its steering read the same line (`reachAt`). `ARENA.radius` (26.6) is still where the skills keep what they place in the arena. A blow brought down onto it stops on it as on a prop (`wallTopAt` in `collision.ts`: its parts but the foliage laid over a top grid in 1 m tiles of 4 cm cells, each made as it's first read, `Wall.solidsOver`; a grid of the whole ring would be 2 million cells a biome): two-handed Power Strike went 2.6 m into the crypt's wall (`lab range --biome crypt --at 24.9,14.4 --facing 1.047 --skill powerStrike --gear twoHanded`).
- **Props sculpted or cast onto a surface (#287):** the crypt's headstone is its slab and rounded top in one extruded outline (`buildHeadstoneGeo`): a half cylinder on a box had a vertex on their shared edge that `lumpy` moved off it, opening a light crack across the face. The forest's stump is sculpted as a distance field (`stump.ts`, after sdf-harness's `tree_stump`: a flaring trunk, six roots into the ground, its top broken in splinters, gnarled) by a worker while the game loads (`preloadStump` from `main.ts`, awaited before the biomes are built; on the page it took 0.2 s); it was a cylinder on six tube roots whose foot stood over the floor on their thin ends. It has a stream of its own: the forest's still draws the 21 numbers the old one did (`OLD_STUMP_DRAWS`), or every prop placed after it would move. The standing stones' ivy and moss are cast onto the stone (`onFace`: a ray in from outside at each point's height and angle): the ivy wound round at 0.62 m, standing off the faces where the stone tapers, and the moss was a dome round where its middle would stand unleaned, a ring standing off it under the pyramidion. Look at a prop with `npm run lab -- live` (`picture --on x,y,z`), and check its faces with `npm run lab -- winding`.
- **Biomes:** every biome is built at boot into its own group, and switching only toggles visibility and sets the shared lights, fog and environment (`BiomeLook`), so it's instant. Each biome must add exactly **10 point lights**, one at each of its four portals (`portalMembrane` in `props.ts`, in the portal's colour, swelling once as foes come through: with a glow on the floor alone, "portals emit no light"), and **2 of them casting** (a different count recompiles every lit shader; dev builds log an error for either), keep the central dais footprint (`world/ground.ts`) and the four gates at the same angles. The lobby is the village, not a biome: a run's biome is the portal the hero went through.
- **The environment maps' programs are compiled before the arena is built** (`compileEnvMaps` in `world/props.ts`, from `main.ts` as the renderer starts), and the maps are made as the first biome is shown (`envMap`, from `setBiome`): a biome's look gives its environment's colours, not a map. three's generator compiles its blur and convolution as it first draws them, so made before the arena the first map waited 0.43 s for the convolution's link (its 256 samples, 0.46 s of a first visit's compile): reached through its private parts (`_setSize`, `_allocateTargets`, `_blurMaterial`, `_ggxMaterial`), checked to be there, and nothing compiled ahead where they've changed. Compile the generator's backdrop too (a stand-in of its kind): it makes it as it first draws. `npm run lab -- programs` lists any that a draw made.
- **Static shadow casters are baked:** `bakeStaticShadows()` in `world/props.ts` merges every non-instanced caster of a biome into one shadow-only mesh (drawn by the shadow map alone, `inShadowsOnly` in `world/culling.ts`: drawn in the frame too with its colour and depth off, it was 16k triangles for nothing). Arena props must not move after the arena is built. The bake keeps positions only, so an alpha-tested mesh (leaf cards) must not cast from a single mesh: it would cast solid quads. Instanced ones cast properly (three's shadow pass honours `alphaTest`).
- **Large instanced meshes are culled cell by cell** (`cullInCells` in `world/culling.ts`): three culls an instanced mesh only whole, by the sphere round all its instances, so the Thornwood's trees, thicket, grass and ferns were drawn whole in every view and in the shadow map, and 661k of the top view's 1.31M triangles lay outside the view of the pass that drew them (`npm run lab -- draws`). Each mesh's instances are sorted into cells at build (a tree each, the thicket in 48 slices of the ring, the grass and ferns in 8 m squares), and before each render (`scene.onBeforeRender`, `cullWhileDrawn`) the cells in the camera's view are packed to the front of its instances, sent to the GPU only when they change: one draw a mesh as before, of what is in view (1.31M triangles to 0.54M, and four fewer draws). Split into meshes of their own instead, the parts cut as many triangles but drew 45 to 60 more times a frame. A mesh that casts gets a copy of its own for its shadow, drawn by the shadow map alone (`inShadowsOnly`: hidden but while the shadow map draws, as three's shadow pass tests layers against the main camera's), its instances outside the shadow camera's box let go at build (`trimShadowCasters`: 40-45% of the trees). A cell is tested only while its mesh is frustum-culled (the warm-up draws everything), and not for a camera that can't see its layer (the first person's arms: packed for theirs, every mesh was packed again the next frame). Don't read a culled mesh's instances by index after the build (a breakable prop's `instanceLook` does): they move. Nothing may vary by `gl_InstanceID` in a culled mesh's shader.
- **The Thornwood's trees come from ez-tree** (`@dgreenheck/ez-tree`, MIT, `world/ezTrees.ts`): its generator's geometry only, with our own bark and leaf-cluster materials. Its build inlines its bark and leaf pictures (16 MB), so `vite.config.ts` imports its source (the `ez-tree` alias) and points its `./textures` module at a stub (`world/ezTreeTextures.ts`): no downloaded texture ships, and the bundle grows 26 KB. Each kind (`TREE_KINDS` in `forest.ts`: four for the forest and one for the giants) is grown once at boot from a preset, seeded, its branch sections and segments capped per level, one level of twigs fewer and its many small leaves fewer, bigger cards with a cluster of leaves each (a preset's whole tree was 4k to 24k triangles; the capped kinds are 1.3k to 4k). The giants are oaks on a long, thick bare bole (`trunk`, `crownStart`, `girth`: as grown an oak's limbs start low and spread 0.6 of its height), 44 to 52 m tall, their crowns a roof 35 to 45 m up over the ring that the sunrays come down through: grown to the old trees' trunk heights, the forest topped out at 30 m, under the sunrays' tops. Each tree is narrowed by its reach from the trunk measured per band of height (`TreeKind.reach`): nothing under `CANOPY_Y` within 2 m of the wall (a giant's low limbs reached 17 m out over the clearing and hid the hero from above), nothing over it more than 2 m past the wall (as the old giants' crowns, over the top-down camera). Measure the canopy with rays straight down over the ring (cover and height by distance from the middle, against main's), look from the clearing up at the sunrays and from the run's top-down view at the wall, and time all trees shown against all hidden in one session (separate boots drifted by 30%; the trees cost nothing measurable). The bark's v is laid down each branch by its girth (`barkAlongByGirth` in `ezTrees.ts`): ez-tree lays one tile round each branch and one up each section, there and back, so a trunk's bark mirrored every 1.3 m and round a thin branch a tile was five times as long as wide. Each kind's `bark` repeat then makes its tiles square, about 0.4 m at a trunk's foot (the oak photo's own size; a giant's 0.6 m), and each prop drawn in bark has its own (`BARK_REPEATS`: stumps, logs, roots, the gates' arches; the gates' `taperedTube` lays u round and v along as a trunk does). Read a surface's tile with `npm run lab -- tiles --maps bark` and look with `--pictures`.
- **The Thornwood's grass** (`buildGrassTuft` in `world/props.ts`): three tuft shapes, each an instanced mesh of its own, sixteen blades a tuft, each a strip tapering in three segments, curving over as it rises and twisting a little, coloured from a dark root to a lighter tip (some yellowed), its normals between its face and up so a tuft is lit round; swayed by the wind in the vertex shader (`windInGrass` in `world/forest.ts`: by the square of the height, along a wave across the clearing, on the game's clock). Each shape draws from a stream of its own, and the old tuft's draws from the biome's stream are still drawn: drawn from it, every prop placed after the grass moved (the lab's measures in the forest with them).
- **The Thornwood's rocks** (the thicket's, the boulders, the gates' boulders, the bonfires' rings, the pebbles) are `boulderGeo` (`world/props.ts`): an icosphere pushed in and out by sdf-harness's solid value noise (`valueNoise3` in `core/patterns.ts`) in lumps and chips, its foot squashed to sit on the ground, each face its own normal, as split stone has. Its UVs are box-mapped in metres (`boxUVs`: each face from the side it faces most), so the `rock` map at repeat 1 is a tile a metre on every face, whichever way it turns: a polyhedron's own UVs wrap a sphere's map round it, stretched 2 to 1 and pinched at its poles. The standing stones are box-mapped too (a cylinder's UVs ran a tile round the 0.35 m plinth, 18 times as long round as up). A rock is turned about the vertical and tilted a little, never over, so its foot stays down. Read them with `npm run lab -- tiles --maps rock --pictures`.
- **Breakable props** (`world/destructible.ts`): everything standing in the arena has hit points and breaks under focused fire (the walls and the boundary don't): player and hostile bolts (`Projectile` world collision), the Void Lance beam, melee swings (`sweep`, whether or not they found a foe) and every area skill (`hurtPropsIn(player, x, z, radius, amount)`: Starfall, Glacial Nova, Maelstrom, Tempest, Crescent, Iron Bellow, Bull Rush; a new area skill calls it next to its `hitEnemy` loop). `PROP_DAMAGE` sets the weights, each prop its own `hp`. A biome builder registers one with `PropSet.add(from, hp, debrisColour, look)` *after* pushing its obstacle circles (`from` is the first index; a log is several circles, one prop), `look` being `instanceLook(mesh, i)` (an instance shrunk away), `meshLook(...meshes)` (hidden) or, for a built assembly, `groupLook(group, updaters, from)`: build it into a `THREE.Group` and it hides its meshes, stops the `updaters` it added, and only darkens its point lights, never removes them (the light count must stay 10). Anything that can go is kept out of `bakeStaticShadows` (`userData.noBake`), so a new prop's meshes must be registered through one of these. Breaking takes the circles out of `G.arena.obstacles` and bumps `state.rev`, which rebuilds the navigation grid. Damage only counts where `simulates()`; the host reports each prop's health (`prop` event) and a guest applies it (`setPropLife`), so bump `PROTOCOL` if that changes. Broken props stay broken from wave to wave and come back when the next run starts or in the lobby (`G.arena.restoreProps()`). A prop breaks into pieces shaped like what it was (`fx/rubble.ts` throws them, `fx/rubbleGeo.ts` is their geometry and painted textures; `PropSet.add`'s last argument is its `RubbleKind`, `stone` by default: `stone` faceted shards with weathered outsides and fresh fracture faces, `wood` tapered splinters, flakes, bark strips and torn stubs on a grain atlas, `shroom` glowing dome fragments of cap (skin above, gills below) and torn fibrous stem pieces on a mushroom atlas, `ember` charred sticks and glowing coals; a new kind is a recipe of those shapes there). The pieces tumble, bounce, topple onto their broad face (a dome fragment lies convex side up; nothing rests on its end), stay about 6-9s and shrink away. Each kind of piece casts a shadow only where the quality preset's `minCasterRadius` allows (`setRubbleShadows`; every populated pool is a draw call in each shadow pass). A glow is only an emissive colour or map on a standard material, and every program is warmed in `warmup.ts`. A damaged prop shows a notched stone gauge (`gaugeArt` in `ui/overlay/bars.ts`, not the foes' red bar) for 5s after the last blow, then fades; it sits no higher than 2.2m and is clamped to the screen (`anchorLabel`'s `clamp`), so it stays in view close to a tall prop.
- **Trees in the play area are wooden props** (`treeSpots` in `world/props.ts`): the crypt's small dead trees (`SMALL_DEAD` in `crypt.ts`, 4.5 to 6 m to the top of their bark as the forest's young oaks are, sized by the grown bark's top (`barkTop`: scaled by the tree's height of 1 they stood under 4 m, "too short"), grown by ez-tree as the dead trees beyond the wall are (those as tall as the forest's round its ring, 9 to 22 m, narrowed by their reach to keep their limbs outside the wall), on a bare bole: as an oak grows, its limbs forked from 0.6 m up, and an arrow at a man's chest met forks and the air between them; stood upright, as a tilt of 3 degrees moved its 8 cm trunk off its foot) and the Thornwood's young oaks (`SAPLING` in `forest.ts`, 4.5 to 6 m, grown thicker than the ring's small oaks: as those, a trunk at a man's chest was 6 cm across and an arrow at it slipped past), six each, one to each sixth of the ring (drawn anywhere in it, three stood in one corner of the forest), clear of the gates' lanes, the props, the dais, the crypt's ring walk and the bonfires, each one circle round its trunk's foot (`TreeKind.trunkFoot`), breakable, its arrows standing in its bark (a sapling's look casts on its bark alone: its leaves let an arrow through). They're placed last, from streams of their own (placed among the props, they moved the weeds, litter and ferns placed round the obstacles). Any tree whose foot stands inside the wall must be one: `npm run lab -- trees --class ranger` shoots each one's trunk and fails on a tree that isn't a wooden prop. Each prop on the floor has a soft shadow under it where it meets the floor (`contactShadows` in `props.ts`, one instanced quad a circle, hidden as its prop breaks): the sun's shadow map alone left a fallen drum's foot lit as the floor round it.
- **Props a run shapes anew** (`Biome.roll`, `G.arena.rollProps(seed)` as a run begins): the crypt's columns and the forest's stumps, built again from the run's seed, which the host sends its guests with the run's start (`sendStart`, `PROTOCOL` 17): each game judges its own hits on them, so every game builds the same. Each crypt column is a mesh of its own (a shared instance's height was its geometry squashed, its break with it), its height (1.6 to 4.4 m) and its break rolled (`rollBreak`: the break's slope and the way it falls, its wave round the rim, its chips, its face's roughness); the forest's stumps are dealt out of a pool of 8 sculpts the stump's worker makes as the game loads (`POOL` in `stump.ts`, each its own girth, break height, slant and splinters), each its own size and turn. The roll bumps the props' revision, so the navigation's grid is made again, and the still shadows are drawn again.
- **Still shadows** (`staticShadow`, `drawStillShadows` in `props.ts`): each biome's two warmest lights (the braziers, the bonfires) cast the shadows of what stands in it, drawn by a render of their own into a pixel only when the biome is shown or a prop breaks, comes back or is rolled (`G.arena`'s update, by the props' revision), the heroes, the foes and everything else hidden meanwhile: a shadow drawn once of a body would stay where it stood. With none a point light lit everything round it through the props, its reflection on the crypt's marble through a column ("shadows on ground for reflected lights still dont work"). Three's shadow pass called alone threw (it reads the render's state). A body passing a brazier casts no shadow from it. What the shadow map alone draws is shown for that render only where it stands in the biome (`drawingStill` in `culling.ts`), and no work runs round its draw: a hero's shadow cast as one (`aroundShadows`) was shown and drawn into the braziers' shadows where he stood, its skinned program for a point light compiled then; a mesh of a body that holds a light (it can't be hidden, its light with it) casts nothing meanwhile.
- **The crypt's dressing:** its columns are broken off at their tops (`breakTop` in `crypt.ts`: the shaft, fluted first, clamped under a sloping break chipped at its rim, `fractureFace` a rough face over it laid from the shaft's own top row: laid from the round rim before the flutes were cut, it stood off the grooves and the sky showed between, "there are gaps"; `breakAt` a plane sloping across the shaft, its wave and chips growing out from the middle and the chips eased into each other round the rim: the slope taken round by the angle alone twisted the face about its middle, and a vertical slit ran down through every column's top, "still seeing holes in concrete pillars"; `npm run lab -- columns` welds every column of a dozen rolls and fails on an open edge or a cliff across a face); its banners are velvet, as sdf-harness's preset has it (a `MeshPhysicalMaterial`'s sheen), embroidered in gold with one sigil (`bannerCloth` in `core/textures.ts`); its dead trees beyond the wall are ez-tree's (`DEAD_TREES`), the wall meeting their trunks alone; every dead tree's limbs are grown thicker and tapering less (`Grow.limbs` in `ezTrees.ts`: as grown bare, ez-tree's limbs were twigs, "dead trees need bigger branches"). The small dead trees grow from the earth: the floor's shader turns the cobbles to bare soil within 1.4 m of each one's foot (`dampGround`'s `earth`, the `soil` map: no program of its own; at 2.4 m "could be smaller", and the forest floor's fallen leaves "cannot come from anywhere" in the crypt), its edge ragged by the damp's noise and washed round the stones, the grout filled first, then the stones dirtied, then only soil, and each has a foot of its own drawn in its bark, its trunk flared and roots arching out into the soil (`buildTreeFootGeo`, hidden and met by arrows with its tree, `small dead roots`): stood on the cobbles, its trunk rose out of them as a post ("they need to grow from ground, not from stone").
- **Fires on props** (`buildFire` in `world/fire.ts`): flames, a bed of coals, embers and smoke, and the flicker of the biome's light over them. The flames are a volume: a box over the bed, each pixel's ray marched through it (`STEPS`, its steps started at random a pixel: in step the slices showed, and by a pattern's offsets its stripes did), gathering their light and losing some of what's behind to their soot (`SOOT_PER_METRE`: summed whole, a fire looked down on was a ball of cream). Sheets turned to the camera turned with it as it went round them ("fire rotates when camera rotates"). Their shape is a body wide at its foot and drawn in to a tip, bent aside by a tiling volume of noise made once at boot (`flameNoise`, a 32³ `Data3DTexture`), more as it rises (by the square of its height: bent as much half way up, the whole fire leant out past a brazier's rim), and each tongue cut at a height of its own: the detail is in the shape, as noise in their light evened out along each ray into a smooth cone. The march runs through a cylinder as far round as they bend (`FLAME_REACH`, from `widthAt` and `bendAt`, which mirror the shader: cut short, a tongue's side was cut straight), the box unturned over the bed; its noise's offsets wrap (`% 1`), so they stay small however long a game runs. A fire a metre across puffs about 1.5 times a second, and one swell moves its flames' height and its light together. Close up the flames cost 0.2 to 0.35 ms of a frame on a desktop GPU (`lab fire --cost`). The bed and a log's charred end are `emberMat`: a lit standard material, black char and grey ash glowing through Worley cracks (most of them dark: lit throughout, they read as lava), a bed's lumps over their sides too but not their tops, its sheen cut to a fifth (at a standard material's 4%, the char a metre under the light read as salmon clay). A fire takes the biome's own point light, one of its ten: never add one for it. Its flames' light may be less than a fire's own (`FireSpec.glow`, the `uGlow` uniform): from the hall of heroes' low, near camera a ray through a brazier's foot crosses the most flame, and at the crypt's it burned white there, which the owner took for a glaring reflection on the bowl (hiding the bowl and the coals in turn left it, hiding the flames took it away); its braziers burn at half. Its flames fade with the prop under it (`seeThroughFade`), and its updater stops when the prop breaks (`groupLook`). A bonfire's logs are one bark mesh and one char mesh (`leanLog` in `forest.ts`). The forest clears the ground round its bonfires (`clearOfFires`): no fallen leaf within the ring of stones, and a fern's roots clear of the flames by its fronds' reach (0.9 m a unit of its scale: grown beside a bonfire, ferns spread over the flames), a spot inside moved out past the clearing rather than drawn again (another draw moves every prop placed after it). `npm run lab -- fire` measures each biome's fire against main's (`tools/lab/COMMANDS.md`): its look, whether it turns with the camera, the plants over it; the candles still burn the old way.
- **Enemy navigation** (`world/navigation.ts`): obstacles are circles (`Obstacle`). Enemies walk straight at the player while the line is clear (with `MARGIN` to spare), else along a flow field: a Dijkstra from the player over a 0.5m clearance grid, one per size class, rebuilt at most one per frame. Every movement goes through `seek` in `enemyAI.ts`, which bends it round obstacles ahead (`steerClear`), so enemies curve round props instead of touching them. Ranged enemies and bosses only fire with a clear shot and walk round the obstacle otherwise. New obstacles need nothing more than an entry in the biome's `obstacles`, with `h`, the height of the prop's top: projectiles fly level and pass over obstacles lower than them, and an enemy's clear-shot check (`lineClear` with a height) agrees, so ranged enemies shoot over low cover. Movement ignores `h`.

## The village

The lobby (`village.ts`, laid out in `data/village.ts`, its objects at work in `game/village.ts`): a green hamlet built once at boot and shown in the biomes' place between runs.

- **The grass's rings stay put as the view turns** (`focusOf` in `meadow.ts`): their middle is where the camera's sight meets the ground, from a sight near level as far as one 0.35 down would (`FOCUS_STEEP`): followed onto the ground as far as it went, a sight just under level threw it from 2 m to 24 m before him as the head bobbed through the eyes, and the dense ring with it ("grass a near circle that keeps updating in front of me"). `lab village` tilts the view through the eyes and fails a jump over 2 m a frame (it was 22).
- **The grass is drawn only where it grows** (`buildMeadow` in `meadow.ts`): the mask is read back once on the CPU into a grid of 0.5 m cells, any grass in each grown by 1.6 m (the blades read the mask aside and lean off their cells), and a ring's tile with none in it is never listed, as one outside the view isn't: over the square, the roads and what's built every blade was drawn as nothing.
