# `src/world`

The arenas.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- `arena.ts`: every biome built once at boot into a group of its own, and the switch between them. `crypt.ts`, `forest.ts`: the biomes' builders. `props.ts`: what they share (the biome contract, boxes, instancing, the gates' membranes, the environment map, the skies, the static shadow bake, the grass). `foliage.ts`: the Thornwood's leaf cards. `ezTrees.ts` (with `ezTree.d.ts`, `ezTreeTextures.ts`): its trees.
- `ground.ts`: the walkable floor's height (the dais's tiers). `collision.ts`: circles against the props and the boundary. `edge.ts`: the wall as a shape, for what flies into it. `destructible.ts`: the props that break. `navigation.ts`: how foes find their way.

Adding a biome is in the root's Architecture.

## Gotchas

- **Biomes:** every biome is built at boot into its own group, and switching only toggles visibility and sets the shared lights, fog and environment (`BiomeLook`), so it's instant. Each biome must add exactly **6 point lights** (a different count recompiles every lit shader; dev builds log an error), keep the central dais footprint (`world/ground.ts`) and the four gates at the same angles. Boot warms up the lobby in every biome. The lobby choice is a cookie (`last-stand-biome`); Random rolls a biome when a run starts.
- **Static shadow casters are baked:** `bakeStaticShadows()` in `world/props.ts` merges every non-instanced caster of a biome into one shadow-only mesh. Arena props must not move after the arena is built. The bake keeps positions only, so an alpha-tested mesh (leaf cards) must not cast from a single mesh: it would cast solid quads. Instanced ones cast properly (three's shadow pass honours `alphaTest`).
- **The Thornwood's trees come from ez-tree** (`@dgreenheck/ez-tree`, MIT, `world/ezTrees.ts`): its generator's geometry only, with our own bark and leaf-cluster materials. Its build inlines its bark and leaf pictures (16 MB), so `vite.config.ts` imports its source (the `ez-tree` alias) and points its `./textures` module at a stub (`world/ezTreeTextures.ts`): no downloaded texture ships, and the bundle grows 26 KB. Each kind (`TREE_KINDS` in `forest.ts`: four for the forest and one for the giants) is grown once at boot from a preset, seeded, its branch sections and segments capped per level, one level of twigs fewer and its many small leaves fewer, bigger cards with a cluster of leaves each (a preset's whole tree was 4k to 24k triangles; the capped kinds are 1.3k to 4k). The giants are oaks on a long, thick bare bole (`trunk`, `crownStart`, `girth`: as grown an oak's limbs start low and spread 0.6 of its height), 44 to 52 m tall, their crowns a roof 35 to 45 m up over the ring that the sunrays come down through: grown to the old trees' trunk heights, the forest topped out at 30 m, under the sunrays' tops. Each tree is narrowed by its reach from the trunk measured per band of height (`TreeKind.reach`): nothing under `CANOPY_Y` within 2 m of the wall (a giant's low limbs reached 17 m out over the clearing and hid the hero from above), nothing over it more than 2 m past the wall (as the old giants' crowns, over the top-down camera). Measure the canopy with rays straight down over the ring (cover and height by distance from the middle, against main's), look from the clearing up at the sunrays and from the run's top-down view at the wall, and time all trees shown against all hidden in one session (separate boots drifted by 30%; the trees cost nothing measurable).
- **The Thornwood's grass** (`buildGrassTuft` in `world/props.ts`): three tuft shapes, each an instanced mesh of its own, sixteen blades a tuft, each a strip tapering in three segments, curving over as it rises and twisting a little, coloured from a dark root to a lighter tip (some yellowed), its normals between its face and up so a tuft is lit round; swayed by the wind in the vertex shader (`windInGrass` in `world/forest.ts`: by the square of the height, along a wave across the clearing, on the game's clock). Each shape draws from a stream of its own, and the old tuft's draws from the biome's stream are still drawn: drawn from it, every prop placed after the grass moved (the lab's measures in the forest with them).
- **Breakable props** (`world/destructible.ts`): everything standing in the arena has hit points and breaks under focused fire (the walls and the boundary don't): player and hostile bolts (`Projectile` world collision), the Void Lance beam, melee swings (`sweep`, whether or not they found a foe) and every area skill (`hurtPropsIn(player, x, z, radius, amount)`: Starfall, Glacial Nova, Maelstrom, Tempest, Crescent, Iron Bellow, Bull Rush; a new area skill calls it next to its `hitEnemy` loop). `PROP_DAMAGE` sets the weights, each prop its own `hp`. A biome builder registers one with `PropSet.add(from, hp, debrisColour, look)` *after* pushing its obstacle circles (`from` is the first index; a log is several circles, one prop), `look` being `instanceLook(mesh, i)` (an instance shrunk away), `meshLook(...meshes)` (hidden) or, for a built assembly, `groupLook(group, updaters, from)`: build it into a `THREE.Group` and it hides its meshes, stops the `updaters` it added, and only darkens its point lights, never removes them (the light count must stay 6). Anything that can go is kept out of `bakeStaticShadows` (`userData.noBake`), so a new prop's meshes must be registered through one of these. Breaking takes the circles out of `G.arena.obstacles` and bumps `state.rev`, which rebuilds the navigation grid. Damage only counts where `simulates()`; the host reports each prop's health (`prop` event) and a guest applies it (`setPropLife`), so bump `PROTOCOL` if that changes. Broken props stay broken from wave to wave and come back when the next run starts or in the lobby (`G.arena.restoreProps()`). A prop breaks into pieces shaped like what it was (`fx/rubble.ts` throws them, `fx/rubbleGeo.ts` is their geometry and painted textures; `PropSet.add`'s last argument is its `RubbleKind`, `stone` by default: `stone` faceted shards with weathered outsides and fresh fracture faces, `wood` tapered splinters, flakes, bark strips and torn stubs on a grain atlas, `shroom` glowing dome fragments of cap (skin above, gills below) and torn fibrous stem pieces on a mushroom atlas, `ember` charred sticks and glowing coals; a new kind is a recipe of those shapes there). The pieces tumble, bounce, topple onto their broad face (a dome fragment lies convex side up; nothing rests on its end), stay about 6-9s and shrink away. Each kind of piece casts a shadow only where the quality preset's `minCasterRadius` allows (`setRubbleShadows`; every populated pool is a draw call in each shadow pass). A glow is only an emissive colour or map on a standard material, and every program is warmed in `warmup.ts`. A damaged prop shows a notched stone gauge (`gaugeArt` in `ui/overlay/bars.ts`, not the foes' red bar) for 5s after the last blow, then fades; it sits no higher than 2.2m and is clamped to the screen (`anchorLabel`'s `clamp`), so it stays in view close to a tall prop.
- **Enemy navigation** (`world/navigation.ts`): obstacles are circles (`Obstacle`). Enemies walk straight at the player while the line is clear (with `MARGIN` to spare), else along a flow field: a Dijkstra from the player over a 0.5m clearance grid, one per size class, rebuilt at most one per frame. Every movement goes through `seek` in `enemyAI.ts`, which bends it round obstacles ahead (`steerClear`), so enemies curve round props instead of touching them. Ranged enemies and bosses only fire with a clear shot and walk round the obstacle otherwise. New obstacles need nothing more than an entry in the biome's `obstacles`, with `h`, the height of the prop's top: projectiles fly level and pass over obstacles lower than them, and an enemy's clear-shot check (`lineClear` with a height) agrees, so ranged enemies shoot over low cover. Movement ignores `h`.
