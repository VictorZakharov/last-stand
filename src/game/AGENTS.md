# `src/game`

The run and what goes round it.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- `run.ts`: the run: its waves, scoring, loot rolls, and the choice after every wave to bank or go on (the core loop, the root's Product rules). In co-op the host's game runs it for the party and the guests follow its reports.
- `warmup.ts`: one of everything that brings its own materials made and drawn, so nothing compiles mid-fight (the root's shader rule). The lobby's before it shows: its biome compiled as it's built (`compileTheBiome`, a light standing in for the hero's), its effects, loot and the shown class's skills compiled with the hero as he's built (`compileTheLobby`: compiled with the lobby's warm-up, after the lobby's panels were painted, their links began 0.6 s after they were asked for), and all drawn by `warmLobby` with the hero wearing everything he can, the other biomes left out; another class's skills are warmed with its hero (`heroParts`; `npm run lab -- compiles --switch <class>`); at boot only the lobby's own hero is, and on a first visit (which opens on the hero selection screen) the hall's room with it (`warmLobby(true)`), the other heroes right after the loading screen leaves, a hero at a time, each shown on his plinth once he's warm, his tab's bar and the choice saying how far along he is till then and the choice waiting (`heroShare`, `heroWarming` in `warm.ts`; from play, "we can only load currently selected/default hero... and then load the two others, show some loading bars on those heroes": warmed with the lobby's, a low-end laptop's loading screen held; `npm run lab -- ui --states heroesWarming`); the rest after it (`warmTheRest`), as parts (a foe kind, another biome drawn in place of the lobby's, another class's hero with everything it can wear), built where the lobby's camera never looks and never let act. The fight's foes are built before the lobby shows, as its warm-up waits on the GPU (`buildAhead`: a kind's first build, its geometry sculpted and its crowd's merged, is one task of 30 to 90 ms, and built behind the lobby each held it up as long; a return's reveal came 0.2 to 0.4 s later), and kept out of the scene and their crowd till warmed. `warm.ts`: what's warm, and what waits for it (`whenWarm`): the fight's parts are warmed behind the lobby once the loading screen has left (its logo's exit draws over the lobby till then: `loadingLeft`), one at a time, a step a frame (`core/shaders.ts` `warmUp`'s `step`: a return's lobby kept 126 to 135 frames a second at the least, where with a part's steps back to back it drew 18 to 36), where they come from the GPU's cache; else once Start or a biome pick needs them, under the fight's loading bar (`ui/readying.ts`: about 1 s on a first visit, where the lobby's frames had linked the foes' programs one by one, waiting for each, for 5 s), on WebGL four parts at a time (`PARTS_AT_ONCE`), each lot's programs linked before the next lot's are compiled, the link wait letting the page go every 40 ms (`warmInTasks`, `LINK_SLICE_MS` in `core/shaders.ts`: on the owner's PC a first visit's cold Start took 7.3 s where all at once it took 4.4 and a part at a time 17.4, its longest frame 90 ms where at once it was 1.6 s; compiled all in one go, a laptop's integrated GPU had them all to link at once, the page's own frames waiting behind them, and without KHR_parallel_shader_compile every program was linked in one task; the other heroes' (and the hall of heroes' room) only when Change hero, a save slot or co-op pick needs them, or a first visit's hero selection screen, a hero at a time (a hero's first build held the lobby up 250 ms: his head's hair). Warm is a build that warmed the fight on this machine before (`last-stand.fight-warmed` in local storage: the builds by their code's chunk, so a deploy that changed a shader is new) with its maps kept: kept maps alone said nothing of the programs, and a return after a visit that never fought compiled the fight cold behind the lobby, 0 to 6 frames a second for 3 s (`npm run stalls -- --scenario boot --cold-return`). - `groundItems.ts`: the lobby floor. `stats.ts`: each player's run in numbers, for the scores.
- `village.ts`: the village's objects at work: the one the hero is near named over him (on touch, the Use button names it: its name over it stood under the button in the screen's corner), used by E, the touch Use button, or a click or a tap from anywhere (he walks there first); what it opened put away as he walks off; his skills cast in the training yard alone (`inYard`). `clock.ts`: the hour the village is lit for (the player's, or the co-op host's). `training.ts`: the first visit's training (`data/training.ts`: walk into the yard, strike a dummy, open the map, the stash at the chest, the notice board), kept in a cookie (`last-stand-training`), its tracker `ui/training.ts`; the portals warn until it's done or skipped. A returning player (a class cookie at its first load) never meets it, so the lab's boots don't: `lab village` begins it afresh (`restartTraining`, also on `window.__dev`).

## Gotchas

- **The lobby floor** (`game/groundItems.ts`): gear given up in the lobby (dragged onto the game field, Shift-click, the touch sheet's Drop) is thrown on the floor, not deleted. Clicking its name (drawn with the other overlays and picked on the canvas, `bindPicking`: the press never reaches the game; on touch a tap shows the card, a second tap takes it) puts it back in the stash, if the class can use its base and the stash has room. In co-op every game shows every drop and the host decides who got one first (`gdrop` / `gtake` / `gtaken` in `net/session.ts`). Whatever is left on the floor is lost when a run starts, and a guest leaving the room leaves it to the host. Every drop is drawn by three instanced meshes, one a piece (its shard, the glow under it and its shaft of light), each drop coloured by its rarity and each piece drawn in one pass: as meshes of their own a drop cost 8 draw calls (additive and double-sided, three.js draws each twice), and 120 drops added 10 to 14 ms to a frame.
- **How a wave comes in** (`startWave`, `spawnGroup` in `game/run.ts`): its foes come in groups, each group whole by one gate or split over two, three or four, at random, in packs of random sizes rising at their gates together (`packSizes`), and by the gates the wave has sent fewest packs by, at random among equals (`gatesFor`), so no gate stands idle for a wave once its packs are enough for all four (two gates picked at random a group, often one twice, left two or three idle; dealt round all four one foe a gate, every group came alike). A foe rises 0.5 to 3.5 m in from its gate and up to 3 m to the side of its middle, a spot drawn again while it is in a prop (`riseSpot`: risen inside one, a foe was shoved out of it in a frame as it first moved; by the Thornwood's west gate that was a log). The boss isn't in a group: it comes once the wave's first group has come, as that group would reach the players (`bossAt`: on average over its foes, each one's rise and then a straight walk at its wave's pace to within its reach of the nearest player), give or take `bossJitter`, never before that group is all up, or at once when the rest of the wave has all come and none of it stands; first in, a ranger could bring it down before the rest came. It comes by its gate's middle, 2 m further in (it's nearly as wide as the gate). The pace is the groups': one `groupInterval` after the last, or at once when none of the wave stands. Measure with `npm run lab -- spawns` against `main`.
