# `src/net`

- **Live hero colours (#393, protocol 30):** twelve fixed palette IDs, one saved pick for the player across classes/slots. Settings and Play together share the picker. In a room only the host allocates colours: guests request `colour?`, the host reserves an unused colour and broadcasts a complete `colours` snapshot by slot. A taken live request keeps the current colour; it never evicts its owner. A matching request acknowledgement saves the accepted preference; an older acknowledgement cannot overwrite a newer pick. Guests apply only their host's snapshot, validate unique slots/colours, and retain allocations that arrive before another guest's hello. A peer body waits for its allocation rather than drawing an unassigned native colour. Hello cannot change an existing allocation. Join still substitutes a free colour when the desired one is taken. Any restores the original look solo and chooses an available colour in a party. Leaving restores the saved pick; disconnects release the room allocation. `lab coop --scenarios colours` exercises live changes and races without running combat; its canary introduces a duplicate.

- Protocol 29 includes `tr` on enemy spawn reports and a battlefield biome event. The Training flag survives on guests; an explicit arena swap clears old foes/attacks before its replacement spawn reports play.

Co-op.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

- `role.ts`: who runs what (the checks every gameplay effect goes through). `session.ts`: hosting or joining a room, the shared lobby, each player's own state and reports. `nameTag.ts`: the player's name tag (kept, tidied, checked, compared). `heroColour.ts`: the colour a player's hero wears (the pick kept, the room's, a free one handed out). `sync.ts`: the fight as the host reports it and the guests show it. `timeline.ts`: a partner's reports played out on a timeline of its own. `transport.ts`: the links (our relay server, or `?net=local`). `netlog.ts`: the connection's diagnostics.

## Gotchas

- **Co-op** (`src/net/`, rules in `game/run.ts`, UI in `ui/coop.ts`):
  - **Who runs what.** Every game moves and casts its own player and reports it (`pl` state at `COOP.sendRate` with its life, `ev` actions: cast / fire / channel start and stop, a hit it took, its fall), in the lobby and in runs; the others replay it on a remote `Player` (`local` false, `replay()`, `updateRemote`). The host's game simulates the fight (enemies, loot, waves, downs) and reports it (`net/sync.ts`: events every frame, a snapshot at the send rate); a guest's enemies are copies (`Enemy.net`) that don't think. **Each game judges its own hero's hits, both ways, as its own screen shows them:** what its skills hit it deals there and then (on a guest, on its copies: the foe's life and a kill show at once, and the hits go to the host on `dmg`, which deals them on its foes and says in its snapshots how far it has, so the copy's life is the host's less the hits still on their way), and a foe's blow at it is judged where it stands against the foe as it shows it (`FX.melee`, `FX.slam`, a bolt's projectile on every game: a hit on a partner's copy does nothing). Judged on the host at 300 ms, 4 of 6 arrows that struck a moving foe on the guest's screen missed it there (it had moved on a metre), and 4 of 5 blows the guest dodged on its screen hurt it. A guest predicts its own fall as the host will rule it (`onPlayerDied`).
  - **Every message is stamped with its sender's clock and played out on a timeline** (`net/timeline.ts`): each partner's reports are shown a little behind the freshest one (`Timeline`: the reports' spacing and the lateness 98% of messages come within, its clock eased onto that place at most 5% fast or slow), positions read between reports (`Track`, a cubic through each report's position and velocity), events applied in order when their time comes (`Playout`: a partner's shot leaves as its body turns to it, a foe's blow lands as its swing shows, and is judged against that), past the last report carried on along its way a moment and then eased onto the next (`Smoother`). Shown as each report arrived, every copy jumped forward and stalled with each burst of a jittery link (speeds off by 1 to 1.4 m/s); eased onto a place that wobbled with the link's delay, a copy ran up to 10% fast or slow; stopped dead in a stall, it lost 0.6 m/s on its speed in under 1% of frames. Measure with `npm run lab -- coop` (`--ping`, `--jitter`) against `main`.
  - **Anything with gameplay effect goes through the checks in `net/role.ts`:** `hitEnemy` needs `by` (the casting player: its stats, the kill, leech) and only deals damage where `dealsDamage(by)` (its own game's), as `dazeEnemy` only dazes there and `carryEnemy` only carries there (a guest's hero's charge carries the copies it meets at once and tells the host in its hit rows, `onDealt`, whose game carries and throws them; its own game carries, throws and holds the copies until the host's reports catch up, then eases them on from there, `Smoother.easeFrom`, `src/entities/AGENTS.md`); `hurtPlayer(p, ...)` only on the local player; props wear down where `hurtsProps(by)` (the host's in a run). Skill code runs on every game for its visuals, so keep its damage in `hitEnemy` and its hits on players in `hurtPlayer`.
  - **A foe's evade is judged by the game that judges the attack** (`entities/evade.ts`): its hero's own (`dealsDamage(by)`), so a guest's arrow is ducked or blinked from as its screen shows it, as its hits are. The host's foe evades there and the `evade` fx tells every guest; a guest's copy evades on its own game at once (`Enemy.evade(..., own)`) and the host is told in its hit rows (`HitReport`'s evade, x, z: the host blinks its foe there too if no prop stands on it), and the host's word of it coming back is passed over (`OWN_EVADE_ECHO`). A copy that blinked is gone, untouchable, until the host's reports have it where it went (`copyBack`: within 0.4 m, or 0.6 s), so its slide there between reports is never seen. `npm run lab -- coop --scenarios evade`: at 300 ms every duck and blink on the guest's screen shows on the host's, the copy back 8 to 23 cm from where the host's foe came out. (PROTOCOL 18.)
  - **What the slammers throw is played by `fx` on every game** (`lava`: the thrower, its kind, where it left the hand, where it lands and in how long, its damage; `vine`: its kind, start, way and length; `entities/throws.ts`): the same numbers fly the same chunk and creep the same vine on each, and each game hurts and holds its own hero alone (`hurtPlayer`, `Player.root`). A partner's copy is shown in coils where the vine reaches it on this screen; it stops because its own game's reports stop it. `npm run lab -- coop --scenarios throws`: at 300 ms each chunk lands within 13 cm on the two screens and hurts the guest, and the guest held 90 frames is shown in coils on the host's for all of them. (PROTOCOL 19.)
  - **An enemy attack goes through `fx()` in `enemyAI.ts`** (named `FX` entries with numeric arguments), which guests replay on the host's timeline, with what each game needs to judge its own hero's hit in it (`melee`'s target slot and damage, `slam`'s damage, type and foe, the last for the battle log). New attacks go there.
  - **Hooks, not imports:** the host reports through sinks set by `net/sync.ts` (`setSpawnSink`, `setFxSink`, `setNumberSink`, `setHitSink`, `runHooks`), so solo code has no network dependency and the relay library only loads when a room opens.
  - **Enemies target `e.target`** (the nearest active player, `nearestPlayer`), with a flow field per player slot. Use `G.players` (the local player first) for anything that concerns every player, and `G.player` only for the local one.
  - **A downed player** lasts `COOP.bleedOut` (25 s), its clock stopped while a teammate holds the revive key by it (`updateDowned`, the host's: the guests are told each one's clock in the snapshots), and is raised in `reviveTime` (2.3 s). Its partners see it marked (`markDowned` in `ui/coop.ts`, drawn over every other overlay, `ORDER.marker`): a red ring over the body with its clock running out round it and the seconds in it, beating like a heart as each one goes (harder in the last 8, a glow alone with reduced motion), the revive filling it in gold, how far it is, and off screen the same ring at the screen's edge on the way to it with an arrow pointing there (a small ring over the body alone was lost as soon as the body was off screen); in reach, a prompt low in the middle of the screen with the key to hold and the revive's bar (`promptRaise`; touch has its button). Look at it with `npm run lab -- coop --scenarios down --pictures`.
  - **A remote player has no staff light** (another point light would recompile every shader); its model is already warmed with the other classes.
  - The pause menu doesn't stop a co-op game (`G.menuOpen` with `G.paused` false; the local player goes `idle`), and a host in a background tab keeps stepping on a timer.
  - **The host's countdown waits for every guest it started** (`readying` in `role.ts`, `sendInRun`): a guest sent the start may take long readying the fight (its programs warmed under its loading bar), and the host's first wave came without it, from play "readying the fight for slower device takes forever, and first wave already launched, we should display waiting for player instead.. and dont deploy the wave". The guest says `inrun` once its run has begun; till then the host's countdown holds (`RunState.held`, sent in the snapshot's run row) and both HUDs say "Waiting for players…", at most `COOP.waitForGuests` (120 s), a guest gone leaving it. Hosting or joining waits for the heroes' programs alone. `npm run lab -- coop` starts every run with the guest kept readying 6 s (its fight chilled), past the countdown: the host's must be held, and go on once it's in (`--canary`: the host told no guest readies, its wave found coming). (PROTOCOL 23.)
  - **A room is in its host's lobby** (`village` in `hello`, `onHello` in `session.ts`; from the owner: "make sure coop clients can play with each other in village, those who opted in"): the village beta's lobby and the panels' are different places, so a game in the other lobby is refused (`refuse`, why `lobby`, with which the room's is), and its Play together dialog says so and offers the switch (`lobbySwitchHtml` in `ui/coop.ts`): the page loaded again in the room's lobby as an invite to it (`reloadWithVillage(on, room)`), joined once loaded. An invite's own join (a name tag kept: no dialog asked) that the room refuses opens the dialog on the refusal (`invitedRefused` in `ui/coop.ts`): it went unsaid before, the game just standing in its lobby, which is where a friend's link to a village room leaves every player with the beta off (found with two real games on a local relay, the same for a name tag taken). Guests of one room never differ: each was let in by the host. `npm run lab -- beta` asks a room from the other lobby (refused, the switch offered, clicked, the room joined), with the host in either lobby (`--panels`).
  - Bump `PROTOCOL` in `session.ts` (and the app id in `transport.ts` if the link itself changes) when the messages change: rooms only hold one version.
  - Test locally with two tabs of one browser and `?net=local` (a BroadcastChannel instead of the server): open a room in one, then open the invite link in the other.
  - **A room holds up to `COOP.maxPlayers` (4)**, as the relay's `ROOM_MAX` does. The host hands out the slots (0 is its own, the guests the lowest free one); a guest says slot -1 in its `hello` until the host has given it one (it said its 0 before, and another guest took it for the host's slot), and a guest that meets a partner with a slot for the first time greets it back once (its own hello can reach a joiner before the joiner's link knows it, and be dropped: a third joining a room of three never knew the guest before it). Spawn points stand 1.6 m apart by slot (`placeSpawns`). `npm run lab -- coop --party 4` fills a room of four and judges it (every game has every other in a slot of its own, by its tag: `judgeRoom`), the others standing off the scenarios' action.
  - **Name tags are the player's, not a hero's** (`nameTag.ts`; from the owner: "name tags per player, not per character, online only, when creating a lobby need to enter, should be unique, if other player with same tag joins, they're asked to rename"). Kept once in local storage (`last-stand.name.v1`), whatever the class or save slot, and asked for in the Play together dialog before a room is opened or joined (`ui/coop.ts`; an invite link joins as the kept tag, or opens the dialog with the code filled in if none is kept); solo never asks for one or shows one. A tag is trimmed, its spaces one, at most `TAG_MAX` (16), letters of any script, digits, spaces and `. _ ' -`; two are the same when they differ only in case or accents (`sameTag`). It rides the `hello` (`tag`), and **the host's game decides**: a guest whose tag is the host's or another guest's (or no tag) is refused (`refuse` with `why: 'name'`), and its game leaves the room asked for another (`session.renameAsked`: the dialog says which tag was taken, keeps the room's code and selects the tag to retype). A partner's tag is `Player.tag`, the local player's `session.tag`, and `nameOf` in `ui/coop.ts` names a player by it everywhere players are named (the partners' frames, the label over a partner's head, the room bar and list, the downed marker and the revive prompt, the choice after a wave, the scores).   - **A guest asks the host about a foe it never heard of** (`foes?` / `foes` on `ctl`, `foesAsked` and `foesTold` in `sync.ts`): a snapshot naming a foe it has no copy of and never had (its spawn lost: a guest whose run started late, its fight still warming as the host's began, heard of none of the first spawns) is asked for once a second until the host answers with the foe's spawn report as it stands now; its copy is made where the guest's timeline shows the foe then, already risen (made where the host has it now, it jumped 1.3 m back onto the timeline; made rising, standing still for its rise, 1.5 m after it). From play: "mobs missing in the first 10 sec... but they're firing". `npm run lab -- coop --scenarios wave` lets wave 4 come with the guest hearing of no spawn for its first 6 s: on main 6 of the host's foes were never on the guest's screen.   - **The relay server** (`server/relay/AGENTS.md`): every game meets on it and every message goes through it, with no public relays and no fallback to them; with it down there's no co-op.
  - **The run's end and watching it:** every game goes back to the lobby by itself once the run is over for everyone, a summary shown before the end going with it (`game/AGENTS.md`'s run's end); out of the run a player watches the others (`game/spectate.ts`), all on its own game: nothing goes over the link. The host's `down` for a player already out is passed over (its word of a guest's fall came after the guest had left, and told it "You are down" through the rest of the run). `npm run lab -- coop` plays both.
  - **The scores** (`ui/scoreboard.ts`, held on Tab; on touch a button under the pause button), in a run and a co-op lobby: each player's run in numbers (`game/stats.ts`: damage dealt, taken and blocked, healed, kills, downs, partners raised; the local player's counted as its own game judges its hits, a partner's as its game reports them on `st` once a second, from each run's start), its round trip to the co-op server with the minute before it as a graph (the server is pinged once a second), and for a partner how far behind its own game it's shown (half of both round trips and its timeline's wait). The lab's link reports a round trip as the relay's pings would, so `coop --pictures` shows it (`shoot`'s last moment, the host holding Tab).
  - **Diagnostics** (`net/netlog.ts`, the pause menu's "Show network stats", `ui/netHud.ts`): the link to the server (open, dropped, its round trip), the partners, the session's steps, in a report copied or shared from the overlay or the co-op dialog.

- **Pings** (`game/ping.ts`, on screen `ui/ping.ts`; #392, PROTOCOL 25): Left Shift held places the mark as the aim moves, on the foe Quarry Mark's aim would take (`aimedFoe`, within `PING.foeNear` of its body), else the cursor's spot, over the minimap its spot (the foe there, if one is), and let go pings it there; on touch, a finger held on the minimap, lifted to ping. Every ping is one size (`PING.radius`), a foe's centred on it as this game draws it (`foeAt`: on a guest's copy, `pos` can stand off its body). A ping has no gameplay effect, so every game sends its own straight to every other on `ctl` (`t: 'ping'`: its kind, x, z, a foe's id, which every copy of the foe shares) and shows the others' as they come, one a player (a new one replaces the last); a foe ping whose foe a game has no copy of marks its spot there. `npm run lab -- coop --scenarios ping`: each ping made as meant and shown on both screens, in the world and on the minimap where it points.
- **Text chat** (`net/chat.ts`, on screen `ui/chat.ts`; #394, PROTOCOL 27): a message has no gameplay effect, so every game sends its own straight to every other on `ctl` (`t: 'chat'`, `text`) and shows the others' as they come. Every message is cleaned where it's said and again where it's heard (`cleanChat`: no control or format characters, a bidi override among them, its spaces one, at most `CHAT.most`, 200, whole characters) and held to `CHAT.burst` (5) a player within `CHAT.per` (5 s), by the game that sends it and by each that hears it, never trusted as it comes; it's shown as text alone, never markup. The relay's caps (64 KB a message, 400 messages and 1 MB a second a socket, `server/relay`) are far over it, so the relay is unchanged. A partner joining or leaving is said in it (`chatNote`); leaving the room clears it. `npm run lab -- coop --scenarios chat`.
- **A copy the host no longer has goes** (`dropGhosts`, `dropGhost` in `sync.ts`; #579): the host's foes only ever leave by dying, and every snapshot names every foe standing there, so a guest's copy missing from a second of them (`GHOST_SNAPSHOTS`, 20) dies here too, and any copy still up as the host's wave ends (`clear`) does; a guest's kill counts nothing, so it costs no score or loot. Its death's report had never reached the guest, or came before the copy was made, and the copy stood where its last report left it, still, its bar up, through the choice after the wave and on into the next (from play: "frozen enemies remain frozen after completing a wave"). `npm run lab -- coop --scenarios ghosts` drops every death report on its way to the guest. Guests cannot choose their own mode or unlock range. Every game rejects training damage and rewards locally, including late loot reports and host disconnects.

- Optional portraits (#396, PROTOCOL 24): `Look.av` seeds a partner’s picture and `StateMsg.av` carries changes during lobby and fight. - **Staffs in co-op** (#402, PROTOCOL 32): a weapon's school travels with the hero's gear (`Look.eq`, the item itself). A burn is the lighter's game's, as a daze is: a guest's rides its hit report (`burn`, after the evade's numbers and before a kill's throw), and the host's snapshot flags every burning foe (8) for the others. A healing beam heals where the healed hero is the local one (`healsHere`), each game judging the beam it shows, so no message carries a heal. The hands a lance with no staff is cast from are its caster's keys': a partner's copy is told them as the channel starts (`chs`'s `h`) and as they change (`chh`). `npm run lab -- coop --class mage --scenarios staffs` plays it in two games: the foes the guest's fire staff sets alight shown alight on the host's and losing life there, the hurt host healed under the guest's beam on its own screen, and its frame on the guest's showing it healed.


- **A duel, hero against hero** (#406, `game/duel.ts`, PROTOCOL 33; from the owner: "Add PvP. Should also have a menu where you can select the map, how many players, against which players. And how many matches"): a run with no foes (`RunState.duel`), set up by the host at the village's weapons rack and fought by the room's own players, so the relay is unchanged.
  - **The menu is the host's, shown on every game** (`duelSetup`): any change goes to the guests on `ctl` (`duel`: the setup and who is ready), a newcomer hears it with its welcome, each guest who fights says `dready` and the host tells everyone; any change to the duel asks them again. The host begins it (`dstart`: the setup and the props' seed), its guests readying it as they ready a run (`inrun`, the first round held for them).
  - **The host rules the rounds and says each turn** (`DuelNet`: `dround`, `dfight`, `dwon`, `dend`), every game applying it as it hears it; a guest's game never ends a round. A fall is each hero's own game's, reported as in any run (`died`), and the host counts the fallen `PVP.trade` after the first, so two who fell together draw.
  - **Each game judges its own hero's hits on the other side, the struck hero's game works them out.** An opposing hero stands among the local game's foes as a `Rival` (`entities/rival.ts`: every skill looks through `G.enemies` and strikes through `hitEnemy`, so every skill meets him with nothing of its own changed), which takes no damage: `takeDamage`, `daze` and `carry` tell his own game on `ctl` (`pvp`: rows of amount, type, crit, knock and where from, chill, freeze, daze, a charge's shove, and where he stood on the striker's screen), straight to it, not through the host. There `duelHits` deals each as his screen has him: his armour, block and ward (`hurtByRival`), none out of a round or from his own side, none where he stands more than `PVP.dodged` from where the striker saw him (he blinked or dashed away), each held to `PVP.hitMost` of his life and a second's to twice that whatever the sender says. That is all that bounds a cheating game: a duel is a room's own, with nothing to win.
  - A rival is never the host's foe: left out of its snapshots and of what a guest asks about (`Enemy.standIn`), its hits never go on `dmg`. In a duel the host sends the fight's events alone (its props' wear), no snapshot.
  - A shot is struck by a rival only when its hero is of the other side (`Enemy.struckBy`): replayed, an opponent's own arrow met the stand-in at his own feet. A shot at the local hero flies on through him on his own screen (he is no foe there): the hit comes from the shooter's game.
  - `npm run lab -- coop --scenarios duel` (`--canary`, `--pictures`): the menu the same on both, a match of four rounds fought over the lab's link, every turn and the score the same on both, each fall on both screens, the result each player's own, both back in the village with their gear counting again. 2 v 2 and watching are in the rules and not yet played by a check of four games.
