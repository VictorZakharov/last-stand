# `src/entities/models`

The procedural models, registered by id in `index.ts` (`MODELS`), which class and enemy data name.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../../AGENTS.md).

- **The rig and its motion:** `rig.ts` (the shared humanoid skeleton and pose helpers, the walk cycle), `ik.ts` (the leg IK and the head's look-at), `armIK.ts` (arm IK within a body's ranges), `anatomy.ts` (a body's ranges of motion, and the rig measured against them), `motion.ts` (keyframed curves for the heroes' authored moves).
- **The heroes:** `mage.ts`, `warrior.ts`, `ranger.ts`, dressed from shared parts: `face.ts` and `head.ts` (the head), `hands.ts` (sculpted in `handShape.ts` of `sdf.ts` and `surfaceNets.ts`, by `hands.worker.ts` as the game loads; skinned by `dualQuaternion.ts`, textured by `triplanar.ts`: `docs/agents/heroes.md`), `armor.ts` (plates, straps, fur, skirts), `tunic.ts`, `boot.ts`, `sleeve.ts` (the mage's cloth sleeves), `furSway.ts`, `bow.ts` (the ranger's), `flask.ts` (the draught and the move that drinks it), `weapons.ts` (the warrior's weapons, each made of the parts that make it what it is: a blade's bevels, flats and fuller, its guard, grip and pommel; an axe's bearded head thinning to its edge, with its socket, poll, langets and rivets; a mace's flanges, each a plate thinning to a polished edge and swept to a spur; a maul's banded head and striking faces. Each is a `Sculpt` of its own kind, so a hero built again replays its pieces; drawn as boxes and discs, through the eyes a maul was a stick, an axe a haft with a plate on it and a mace a block. Look at one alone with `npm run lab -- weapons`, and in the hand with `lab sheet --gear <style> --weapon <base>`, worn on the belt with `--gear dual --states cast --skill potion`; each one's striking part along its grip, `Weapon.edge` in `warrior.ts`, is what its swing's trail follows, `Model.swung`), `warriorEyes.ts` (the warrior's moves keyed through the eyes: `docs/agents/heroes.md`).
- **The creatures:** sculpted with `shapes.ts` (`Sculpt`): `brute.ts`, `husk.ts`, `imp.ts`, `mossback.ts`, `sporecaller.ts`, `thornling.ts`, `treant.ts`, `witch.ts`, and the training `dummy.ts`.

**Notes of their own** (`docs/agents/`), read before changing what they cover: `heads.md` (`face.ts`, `head.ts`: the face's field and masks, the hair, beards and necks, the face harness), `heroes.md` (the gear an item shows, the hands and grips, the ranger against his reference sheet, armour and skirts, the engraving, the keyed moves, the draught, the idles), `ranger-bow.md` (`bow.ts` and the shot `ranger.ts` poses), `legs.md` (`ik.ts`, `walkCycle`, the gait in `player.ts` and `enemy.ts`). What is learned about one of those goes in its note.

## Gotchas

- **A pose is decided as a person would hold it, then looked at as the player sees it, and only then measured.** Say what a real body does first (an archer between shots: the arm hanging straight, the bow level, the arrow pointing down), rig that directly in those terms (directions and joint angles, not a search over rotations), and look at it in the game's own views (the lobby's camera, the run's, close-ups from the front, the side and behind) in the state the player sees it in (an arrow on the string, standing and at both ends of a stride) before running any clip measure. Clip measures check a natural pose; they never choose one: a few millimetres of clip in a pose a body would take beat no clip in one it wouldn't (a bow carry chosen from a hundred by its clip count held the arm out from the side with the arrow sticking out sideways, and took hours).
- **Creature geometry** (`entities/models/shapes.ts`): the enemies sculpt their bodies from tapered tubes, lathed limbs, noise-displaced shapes, leaves, fern fronds, branching boughs and winding strands on the rig's joints (`stripRig` removes the stand-in capsules), and `Sculpt` merges each joint's pieces per material into one mesh. `new Sculpt(kind)` caches the merged geometry per kind: later builds of the kind replay it (the builders return at once), so spawning stays cheap. That needs a deterministic build (seeded `mulberry`, never `Math.random`), and the shared geometry is never disposed (`userData.shared`, skipped in `Enemy.dispose`). The mage's bell sleeves are cloth (`models/sleeve.ts`: verlet particles on a tube, pinned by their top ring and hanging by gravity, hoop constraints keeping the section round, collided with the forearm and the body (`bodies`: torso, hips, skirt) and hanging outside the robe's skirts as they lie (`skirts`, `Skirt.pushOut`: kept off an ellipse the size of the hips, a bell hung into the robe draped out over the thighs, standing too); don't add a spring back to the animated shape, it makes them rubbery; each particle kept within the radius it was cut to round the forearm (so the arm never leaves the sleeve), a tether to the top ring and carrying the cloth along with part of the animation's motion stop it streaming off the arm at a run, and each row's slide up the forearm is capped (`SLIP`) so a turn or a lean never bares the hand; stepped in `animate` after the pose; `?cloth=0` holds them at the animated shape), and the warrior's fur sways (`models/furSway.ts`: a spring on each fur mesh in the vertex shader, weighted by the uv's v along the tuft; `?fur=0`). A piece that crosses a joint (a sleeve over the elbow, the neck into the head) goes through `Sculpt.skin` / `skinPair` instead of `add`: analytic skin weights from its position along the bone, one `SkinnedMesh` per joint for a hero (the first-person view hides by joint) and one per material on the root for a creature. Skinned meshes must keep their own copy of the material and their own `customDepthMaterial` (both done in `build()`), or a crowd pays for three rebuilding program parameters at every rigid/skinned switch; `Player.dispose` and `Enemy.dispose` free the skeleton and that copy. `?skin=0` puts them back on one joint. Pieces under 7 cm cast no shadow, and neither does a material passed to `Sculpt.glow` (glows, and leaves). For skin and cloth use `kit.rim` (a rim of light at glancing angles) rather than a physical material's sheen, which costs far more per pixel.
- **Creatures are drawn in crowds** (`models/crowd.ts`): every body of a kind is an instance of its kind's pieces, merged per material into one geometry skinned on its joints, so a kind costs a draw call a material (and one each for its shadow) however many are about. A creature drawn mesh by mesh was 77 draw calls a body with its shadow, and a wave of 38 took 20 ms a frame on a desktop to the crowd's 6 to 9 (#222). Each body's joints' matrices and its looks are a row of a float texture the shaders read by `gl_InstanceID`: the kit's dissolve, frost, char and hit flash (`kit.u`), its edge colour, how strongly each of its materials glows (its own `emissiveIntensity`, which `animate` may pulse), and whether it casts a shadow (not while dissolving). So a foe's look changes through those alone: a colour set on one foe's material, a mesh hidden, a material swapped show on none (the crowd draws its kind's first body's materials). What the crowd draws are `Sculpt`'s shared pieces in a solid kit material (`piecesOf`); a mesh a model makes of its own (the sporecaller's pod, the treant's heart, the witch's orb), a see-through material and the training dummy are drawn on their own as before. A body keeps its own meshes, on a layer no camera draws (`CROWD_LAYER`, `core/layers.ts`), for what reads them: an arrow lodges in them (`fx/stuckArrows.ts`), the outline draws them (`ui/overlay/outline.ts`). Never make a three.js object mid-fight in the crowd: each draws random numbers for its id, and an A/B's pictures of anything random differed after it (the texture grows in place, `grow`). Its programs are compiled at load with the rest (`game/warmup.ts` spawns one of every foe). `?crowd=0` draws each body on its own again. Measure with `npm run lab -- crowd` (the frame), `draws` (the draw calls by what drew them) and `death` (the bodies as drawn); `held` checks Quarry Mark's outline round a crowd's body.
- **A skinned mesh met by a ray** (`models/hulls.ts`, for the aim: `fx/stuckArrows.ts` `castSolid`): three's own test poses every vertex by its bones first, 1 to 9 ms a body, so the aim met only the rigid meshes, and a ray over a boss's skinned limbs went on to the floor behind it. Each skinned geometry is cut into parts (the triangles of one piece it was merged from that one bone moves most of their first corner), each with its box as built and every bone that moves it. A posed vertex is a weighted mean of where each of its bones puts it, so the box round the part's box as each of those bones puts it holds the part as it's drawn; the bones' parts are met by such boxes, and only the triangles of the parts the ray meets are posed and met, exactly. (A box a bone in its own space left the skin bent between two bones out of it, and one round a bone's share of every piece met the gaps between legs.) About 0.07 ms a frame the aim is over a boss. The parts are made once a geometry, at warm-up (`reachOf`, from the `Enemy` constructor), which also gives the foe how far its body reaches (`Enemy.reach`) for the aim's first, cheap test. Every body of a kind shares its geometry and is bound in its build's pose, so its parts too.
- **Hero models** (`models/mage.ts`, `models/warrior.ts`, `models/ranger.ts`) strip the rig's stand-in capsules and dress it from shared parts:
  - **Proportions are a real man's** (ANSUR II, the US Army's survey): 7.5 heads tall, shoulders, chest and hips at real widths plus the clothing or armour on them. Size new gear to that frame; exaggerated shoulders make the head look small.
  - Their parts have notes of their own, with what each was measured against and how it went wrong before: `docs/agents/heads.md` (`models/face.ts` and `models/head.ts`: the face's distance field and masks, the hair, beards and necks, tuning a face with the face harness), `docs/agents/heroes.md` (the gear an item shows, the hands and their grips, the ranger's look against his reference sheet, armour and the skirts' drape, the engraving, the keyed full-body moves, the draught and the idles) and `docs/agents/ranger-bow.md` (`models/bow.ts` and the shot `models/ranger.ts` poses: the draw, the loose, the quiver, the carry, the bow through the eyes). Read the note before changing its part.
  - Hero geometry detail follows the quality preset (`heroDetail`), taken when a hero is built.
- **A body's ranges** (`models/anatomy.ts`): every joint of the hero rig measured as a clinician measures it (`measureBody`, degrees: the arm's raise, the plane it rises in and its turn on the chest, the elbow, the forearm's turn, the wrist's two bends, the hip, knee and ankle, the trunk's and the neck's turn and bends), against the adult norms (`ROM`; the arm's raise by its plane, `elevationLimit`: straight back it stops at 60, 50 behind the side it rises only to about level), and the arms against the head and trunk (`bodyShape`, `overlaps`: ellipsoids from the rig's proportions and the head's skin, the forearms and hands as capsules). The rig's rest pose is standing, arms hanging, palms back (the forearms turned fully over). Posing a body by its end points (a hand to a spot, a foot level) and letting two-bone IK join them puts joints where none can go: the bow's draw elbow went straight back behind the body and the wrist inside the head, the weapon wrists bent 60 to 130 degrees round their handles, swinging feet bent 85 degrees up under bent knees, the trunk twisted 84 degrees. So the IK keeps to the ranges: `armIK.ts` (`solveArm`, `fitArm`) places the elbow, of every place on its circle round the shoulder to wrist line, where the shoulder, the forearm's turn and the wrist are within range and the arm keeps out of the head and trunk, nearest the pose's elbow and last frame's (warm-started from it); a held handle may also slant across the palm and a lower grip turn about its handle (both picked from a few each solve, or with `glide` eased from the last solve's towards the best, found between the ones tried); a hand coming onto a grip may ease its turn on the forearm from its own (`ease`), and an elbow's move a frame may be capped (`maxMove`: past it, it goes round its circle towards the best, a swing and not a jump); the forearm's turn and the wrist stop at their ends, so what the hand holds turns only as far as they allow. Each hero fits every arm after its pose (`fitArm`, third person: the weapon arm first, then a two-hander's lower grip, then the other arm), the hand where the pose put it. The shoulder girdle (`GIRDLE`, `girdlePlace`: the collarbone lifting the shoulder and drawing it forward or back) moves only where the shoulder joint is, so an arm keeps its meaning, and only when an arm asks for it (`ArmGoal.girdle`) and no elbow keeps it in range at rest. The leg IK holds a foot in the air within the ankle's range (it hangs from the shin, `clampAnkle`), turns a planted foot's knee towards it within the hip's turn (and a planted foot the hips turn further from than the hip and ankle allow together pivots on its ball after them, `PIVOT_EARLY`, and again by as much as its twist on the shin, measured after the leg is solved, is past `ANKLE_TWIST`: with the leg stretched out behind, the hips swinging back over it at a reversal twisted it 13 degrees past), keeps a foot in the air within the hip's turn and the ankle's twist of the hips (wheeling round onto a shot behind, a stepping foot kept the way it left the ground and the hip turned 56 degrees), and caps the pelvis's turn towards the way the body goes so it and the pose's own twist stay within the trunk's (`TRUNK`; past it the legs step more across the pelvis's line, as a person's do). Dev builds report each joint taken past its range once per action (`watchBody`, from `Player.animateBody`; not through the eyes, whose arms keep hand-placed poses). `?rom=0` lets the joints go past their ranges again, for A/B. Measure with `npm run lab -- range` (the range probe ported into the lab: `--gear` for a weapon style, `--skill` for a skill cast standing; still to port: each skill cast on the move, and the arms' depth in the body), check its conventions on a bare rig in known poses first, and film a change against `?rom=0` with the same gear (the default profile rolls its weapons). What is still past a range: a two-hander's lower wrist through the swings, 14 to 17 degrees by the weapon (both hands on one handle: the weapon would have to tilt between them; `docs/agents/heroes.md`), and a few feet rolled onto their edge and hips opened 5 to 15 degrees in wide stances.
- **Leg IK and head look-at** (`models/ik.ts`; `?ik=0` turns both off and restores the walk cycle's own legs and gait for A/B): `LegIK` runs at the end of a model's `animate` (heroes and every walking enemy, not the floating witch) and takes the legs over, and `LookAt` turns the chest, neck and head onto the nearest foe (heroes) or the target (enemies). How a foot is planted, swung, landed and set off, a walk against a run, going round, stops, steps by the dais's edges, the body and arms swinging with the legs and the stance a pose asks for are in `docs/agents/legs.md`, with what each was measured against and how it went wrong before: read it before changing any of them (`models/ik.ts`, `walkCycle` in `rig.ts`, the gait in `player.ts` or `enemy.ts`), and measure with `npm run lab -- gait`, `stops`, `feet` and `form` against `main`.
