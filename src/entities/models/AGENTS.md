# `src/entities/models`

The procedural models, registered by id in `index.ts` (`MODELS`), which class and enemy data name.

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../../AGENTS.md).

- **The rig and its motion:** `rig.ts` (the shared humanoid skeleton and pose helpers, the walk cycle), `ik.ts` (the leg IK and the head's look-at), `glance.ts` (an idle hero's casual look round, which the look-at applies: `docs/agents/heroes.md`), `armIK.ts` (arm IK within a body's ranges), `anatomy.ts` (a body's ranges of motion, and the rig measured against them), `motion.ts` (keyframed curves for the heroes' authored moves).
- **The heroes:** `mage.ts`, `warrior.ts`, `ranger.ts`, dressed from shared parts: `face.ts` and `head.ts` (the head), `hands.ts` (sculpted in `handShape.ts` of `sdf.ts` and `surfaceNets.ts`, by `hands.worker.ts` as the game loads; skinned by `dualQuaternion.ts`, textured by `triplanar.ts`: `docs/agents/heroes.md`), `armor.ts` (plates, straps, fur, skirts), `tunic.ts`, `boot.ts`, `sleeve.ts` (the mage's cloth sleeves), `furSway.ts`, `bow.ts` (the ranger's), `flask.ts` (the draught and the move that drinks it), `weapons.ts` (the warrior's weapons, each made of the parts that make it what it is: a blade's bevels, flats and fuller, its guard, grip and pommel; an axe's bearded head thinning to its edge, with its socket, poll, langets and rivets; a mace's flanges, each a plate thinning to a polished edge and swept to a spur; a maul's banded head and striking faces. Each is a `Sculpt` of its own kind, so a hero built again replays its pieces; drawn as boxes and discs, through the eyes a maul was a stick, an axe a haft with a plate on it and a mace a block. Look at one alone with `npm run lab -- weapons`, and in the hand with `lab sheet --gear <style> --weapon <base>`, worn on the belt with `--gear dual --states cast --skill potion`; each one's striking part along its grip, `Weapon.edge` in `warrior.ts`, is what its swing's trail follows, `Model.swung`), `warriorEyes.ts` (the warrior's moves keyed through the eyes: `docs/agents/heroes.md`).
- **The creatures:** sculpted with `shapes.ts` (`Sculpt`): `brute.ts`, `husk.ts`, `imp.ts`, `mossback.ts`, `sporecaller.ts`, `thornling.ts`, `treant.ts`, `witch.ts`, and the training `dummy.ts` (its straw bundles of thin strands, each its own shade, out of the sleeves' tied ends, under its sack and on its head: five smooth cones a bundle read as spikes, #287; made once and shared by every dummy, `strawOnce`, as the lobby's are built again whenever it's shown; it wobbles only when struck, and stands still at rest: an idle sway moved every part each frame, and on WebGPU each moved part's draw is refreshed in full).

**Notes of their own** (`docs/agents/`), read before changing what they cover: `heads.md` (`face.ts`, `head.ts`: the face's field and masks, the hair, beards and necks, the face harness), `heroes.md` (the gear an item shows, the hands and grips, the ranger against his reference sheet, armour and skirts, the engraving, the keyed moves, the draught, the idles), `ranger-bow.md` (`bow.ts` and the shot `ranger.ts` poses), `legs.md` (`ik.ts`, `walkCycle`, the gait in `player.ts` and `enemy.ts`). What is learned about one of those goes in its note.

## Gotchas

- **The mage's hood and capelet** (`mage.ts`, `hood.ts`): the capelet needs to follow the chest through a walk. On PR #315 the loosely held, head-carried join folded through the back (lab `hood` read 13.9 cm); carrying the join by the chest and holding the capelet at 0.3 cleared the walking reads in the tested stride. Moving the side of the join in towards the neck made a shelf in the still view but folded the hood at the shoulder while walking; weakening the front capelet's hold brought a fold back. The reference's separate, flat shoulder layer still needs work: the current front shoulder sits about 1.5 cm off the body against the lab's 1.2 cm limit, and its brow still reads pointed. Check the visual views as well as `lab hood --ref .tmp/references/hood/chatgpt/hood.marks.json --canary`; on this version its forward canary missed the overhang threshold (0.10 E read against 0.12 E), so that reading needs attention too.
- **A sleeve's top and its armhole take the seam's share of the arm from one place** (`models/tunic.ts`, `SetInShoulders.seamShare`; the ranger's coat, the mage's coat and waistcoat). The body's cloth round the armhole and the sleeve's top ring are the same points, each skinned by its own `skinK` along the chest-to-shoulder chain, so the seam is closed only while both give a point the same share. `UNDER` (the cloth under the shoulder joint keeping to the chest, added for the mound under a drawn arm) cut the body's share alone, and the sleeve's top kept the whole of it: with an arm raised the sleeve's underside rose further than the armhole, and the seam opened round the armpit and behind the shoulder, up to 11 cm on both heroes, the world seen through the coat at every draw and cast (from play: "a hole where the armpit area is... visible when the archer does any movement for drawing a bow or shooting it"). Now each column of the sleeve's cap starts from the seam's share at its own point of the edge (`seamY`). A change to either side's share goes through `seamShare`; check with `npm run lab -- seams --class ranger` (and `mage`), which reads every sewn point as drawn through each skill.
- **A pose is decided as a person would hold it, then looked at as the player sees it, and only then measured.** Say what a real body does first (an archer between shots: the arm hanging straight, the bow level, the arrow pointing down), rig that directly in those terms (directions and joint angles, not a search over rotations), and look at it in the game's own views (the lobby's camera, the run's, close-ups from the front, the side and behind) in the state the player sees it in (an arrow on the string, standing and at both ends of a stride) before running any clip measure. Clip measures check a natural pose; they never choose one: a few millimetres of clip in a pose a body would take beat no clip in one it wouldn't (a bow carry chosen from a hundred by its clip count held the arm out from the side with the arrow sticking out sideways, and took hours).
- **Creature geometry** (`entities/models/shapes.ts`): the enemies sculpt their bodies from tapered tubes, lathed limbs, noise-displaced shapes, leaves, fern fronds, branching boughs and winding strands on the rig's joints (`stripRig` removes the stand-in capsules), and `Sculpt` merges each joint's pieces per material into one mesh. `new Sculpt(kind)` caches the merged geometry per kind: later builds of the kind replay it (the builders return at once), so spawning stays cheap. That needs a deterministic build (seeded `mulberry`, never `Math.random`), and the shared geometry is never disposed (`userData.shared`, skipped in `Enemy.dispose`). The mage's bell sleeves are cloth (`models/sleeve.ts`: verlet particles on a tube, pinned by their top ring and hanging by gravity, hoop constraints keeping the section round, collided with the forearm and the body (`bodies`: torso, hips, skirt) and hanging outside the robe's skirts as they lie (`skirts`, `Skirt.pushOut`: kept off an ellipse the size of the hips, a bell hung into the robe draped out over the thighs, standing too); don't add a spring back to the animated shape, it makes them rubbery; each particle kept within the radius it was cut to round the forearm (so the arm never leaves the sleeve), a tether to the top ring and carrying the cloth along with part of the animation's motion stop it streaming off the arm at a run, and each row's slide up the forearm is capped (`SLIP`) so a turn or a lean never bares the hand; stepped in `animate` after the pose; `?cloth=0` holds them at the animated shape), and the warrior's fur sways (`models/furSway.ts`: a spring on each fur mesh in the vertex shader, weighted by the uv's v along the tuft; `?fur=0`). A piece that crosses a joint (a sleeve over the elbow, the neck into the head) goes through `Sculpt.skin` / `skinPair` instead of `add` (the bones' indices kept as floats, the hands' too: Uint16 ones read by three's float attribute made ANGLE compile a vertex shader at each skinned program's first draw, on the GPU process's main thread, `src/core/AGENTS.md`): analytic skin weights from its position along the bone, one `SkinnedMesh` per joint for a hero (the first-person view hides by joint) and one per material on the root for a creature. Skinned meshes must keep their own copy of the material and their own `customDepthMaterial` (both done in `build()`), or a crowd pays for three rebuilding program parameters at every rigid/skinned switch; `Player.dispose` and `Enemy.dispose` free the skeleton and that copy. `?skin=0` puts them back on one joint. Pieces under 7 cm cast no shadow, and neither does a material passed to `Sculpt.glow` (glows, and leaves). For skin and cloth use `kit.rim` (a rim of light at glancing angles) rather than a physical material's sheen, which costs far more per pixel.
- **Faces point out.** A one-sided material draws only the faces wound towards the camera, so a piece wound inside out shows its inside, lit from the wrong side: the forest's roots drew as black lines on the floor. `taperTube` and `twist` (`shapes.ts`) wind outward; `lathe` takes its profile bottom to top (top to bottom it points in: the creatures' shelf fungi and caps did); never bake a mirror into a geometry (`scale(..., -1)`), as three only turns its front-face test round for a mirrored mesh's matrix, not its vertices (the bow's brass leaf was inside out): turn a symmetric piece half round instead. Before #289 the creatures' tubes were all wound in, and 254 parts of 11 models showed their inside. Check with `npm run lab -- winding` (every model and biome, each mesh's parts by their signed volume).
- **Creatures are drawn in crowds** (`models/crowd.ts`): every body of a kind is an instance of its kind's pieces, merged per material into one geometry skinned on its joints, so a kind costs a draw call a material (and one each for its shadow) however many are about. A creature drawn mesh by mesh was 77 draw calls a body with its shadow, and a wave of 38 took 20 ms a frame on a desktop to the crowd's 6 to 9 (#222). Each body's joints' matrices and its looks are a row of a float texture the shaders read by `gl_InstanceID` (its looks passed on as `flat` varyings, the first vertex's: `firstVertexProvokes` in `core/renderer.ts`, or ANGLE adds a geometry shader to every crowd program on D3D11): the kit's dissolve, frost, char and hit flash (`kit.u`), its edge colour, how strongly each of its materials glows (its own `emissiveIntensity`, which `animate` may pulse), and whether it casts a shadow (not while dissolving). So a foe's look changes through those alone: a colour set on one foe's material, a mesh hidden, a material swapped show on none (the crowd draws its kind's first body's materials). A body dissolves as a screen door where it comes within half a metre of the camera, and nearer than 0.2 m none of it is drawn (`EYE_CLEAR`): through the eyes Bull Rush runs into the foe it throws ahead of it, and the camera stood inside a husk, half the frame its glow (lab `cast bullRush --views first --what`). Its shadow stays whole; `?crowd=0`'s bodies don't dissolve. What the crowd draws are `Sculpt`'s shared pieces in a solid kit material (`piecesOf`); a mesh a model makes of its own (the sporecaller's pod, the treant's heart, the witch's orb), a see-through material and the training dummy are drawn on their own as before. A body keeps its own meshes, on a layer no camera draws (`CROWD_LAYER`, `core/layers.ts`), for what reads them: an arrow lodges in them (`fx/stuckArrows.ts`), the outline draws them (`ui/overlay/outline.ts`). Never make a three.js object mid-fight in the crowd: each draws random numbers for its id, and an A/B's pictures of anything random differed after it (the texture grows in place, `grow`). Its programs are compiled at load with the rest (`game/warmup.ts` spawns one of every foe). `?crowd=0` draws each body on its own again. Measure with `npm run lab -- crowd` (the frame), `draws` (the draw calls by what drew them) and `death` (the bodies as drawn); `held` checks Quarry Mark's outline round a crowd's body.
- **A hero's cloth is dyed by its materials' colours alone** (`models/dye.ts`, `Model.dye`, the palette in
  `data/heroColours.ts`): each hero names the materials of his cloth (`new Dye(native).cloth(...)`, the main cloth
  first: the mage's coat, shirt and linings, the warrior's tunic and shield's paint, the ranger's coat, hem and hat's
  felt), and a colour sets each one's `color` and rim light (a uniform's value) to its hue, at a saturation and
  lightness by each one's against the main cloth's (its hue offset kept, the ranger's felt turned olive in gold), so
  the shades between them hold; his own colour (`native`) puts back what he was built in. Never a map, a define or
  another material: a dye compiles nothing (165 programs before six colours drawn, 165 after) and on WebGPU the
  merged meshes read it live off their base (`standInFor`). A skinned part's copy of a material is found by the
  shader patch it shares (`onBeforeCompile`) and set too. A dyed material under a colour map takes the map's colour
  with it: the shield's paint under the wood's brown map came out brown in every colour, so it takes the wood's
  relief alone. Look at a colour with `lab live start --wear helm,chest` and `live eval
  "__G.player.setColour('gold')"`, then `live picture`.
- **A worn item glows in its rarity's colour from Rare up** (`models/itemGlow.ts`, `ITEM_GLOW` in `core/materials.ts`, #404; from play: "slight glow, not much, no light source", over a player's colour, "green on green should work", "should not make it flat", "subtly animated (slowly)", "glow should be on the object, not around it", "tendrils need to animate too, not just pan", "make sure glow is continuous/looped and not jumping"). Light added over the item's own surface, faint, as thin strands of light (`auraTendrils`: warped fractal noise in the body's own space, `vFxPos`, so it sticks to the item as he moves, lit where it crosses its middle) rising up it and changing shape as they go, brighter over part of it at a time, with a faint even light under them (`face`). Light wherever the noise lay over a level (the look first chosen on a flat panel) made broad blobs, which on the mage's robe read as camouflage (an adversarial review's FAIL); strands everywhere alike made a net. It loops every `cycle` (16 s) with no join: its noise's lattice wraps in time (each of two slices of a 3D field its own offset, blended, a slice apart) and its rise and turns are even numbers of lattice cells a cycle (its warp runs at half the scale, so an odd `turns`, 3, left it 1.5 cells and it jumped as each cycle began: the owner saw it), so the end of a cycle runs into its start (a crossfade of two cycles showed its join too). `lab glow` judges the step as the cycle starts over against those beside it: a frame a cycle on against now can't see a join, the shader given only the share of the cycle. Not an outline or a fill: a rim drawn round the item read as the ranger's mark, and a strength of 0.25 read as paint over the dye ("I didn't ask to put paint on him"), so it's 0.07 of the rarity's colour (0.12 was too busy for four players in co-op: "more subtle... might look too distracting"), and the weapon's 10 times that (`ITEM_GLOW.weapon`), its brightest strands past the bloom's threshold for a soft light round them ("a bit of glow on weapon only, in addition to the shader"). It's a setting, on by default (the pause menu's Settings, Gear glow, `setGearGlow`, the cookie `last-stand-gear-glow`): off, its strength is 0, a uniform's value, so it switches live and compiles nothing. No light, no program: an item's parts draw with copies of their kit materials (`kitCopy`: the same hook and key, so one program and the dye finds them; their own `kitLooks.aura`, read by WebGL's hook off the material it compiles for, `this`, and by WebGPU's `materialReference`). Where the hero's looks draw from palettes (WebGL, below) a copy is a row of its original's palette and costs nothing; elsewhere (WebGPU, a part no palette holds) an item that doesn't glow (under Rare, or the setting off) draws in the kit's own (`ItemGlows.set` swaps them; the setting's buttons set each hero's gear again). Each hero names his items' parts (`ItemGlows.add`: the warrior's weapons, shield, helm, harness, gloves; the mage's staff, book, hood, longcoat, bracers; the ranger's bow, cap, coat's cloth and bracers) and sets them in `setGear` from `Gear.rarity`. A copy is made as the hero is built and joins `kit.mats` (on WebGPU a merged hero's parts are told apart by them, and merged again as a part's material changes). A material a wrapper calls the hook of with its own material (the hands' copies: `dualQuaternion`, `triplanar`, `glowOutline`) can't be copied (the copy's hook would read the original's looks): it's given a glow of its own in place (`inPlace`), as the gloves are. Check with `npm run lab -- glow --class <hero>` (each slot's glow against the frame without it, close up, in green: seen, faint, not one flat light, its hue, none on a Magic item, its pixels moving slowly, no join as its cycle starts over, no program; `--canary`; `--tune itemGlow.strength=...` to compare), and look with `lab live start --wear helm,chest,hands --rarity rare`.
- **A hero's looks are drawn from palettes** (`models/palette.ts`, `drawFromPalettes` from `Player` and the warm-up; WebGL; `?palette=0` each look in its own material; from play: "3ms is half of our 144hz budget its crazy high for just the hero", "why do we need 48 material switches a frame for plain gear? why not 1"). **What a hero costs three is the materials he draws in, not his draws or triangles**: a draw in another material than the one before has every uniform sent again, 13 to 24 us on a desktop where a draw in the same material takes 2 to 4, and the hero was 1.0 ms of a render's 2.4 ms of JS (9 of 21 ms with the CPU slowed four times; a phone's 3.0 ms of 8.8, trace 05C8-3E1V). His 77 scene draws merged by material into 46 skinned meshes (`drawAsOne.ts` run on WebGL: built, measured, taken out) took as long. So the kit's materials that differ only in what a table can hold (colour, roughness, metalness, the normal map's depth, the maps' repeat, their glow, rim, hood's shade, an item's glow) share one material, a palette of up to 16 looks, and each mesh's vertices name their look's row (`aLook`, on a wrap of its geometry that shares its attributes; a cloth's geometry, read by what moves it, takes it in place). The kit's materials stay what everything writes to (`Dye`, `ItemGlows`, a gem's pulse in `animate`, the hood's shade): each palette reads its rows off them once a frame, as its first draw comes up. What reads a hero's look by his meshes reads `looksOn(mesh)`, never `mesh.material` (the hood's shade set the palette's copy of `userData.shade`, a plain object, and the boot threw). A palette's shader is the kit's own with its looks read off the rows (`readRows`: it throws where the kit's shader has lost what it replaces), a program of its own a kind (`|palette` in its key), compiled at load with the hero. Looks share a palette only where everything else of them is alike (`signatureOf`: the maps' pictures, the side, see-through, vertex colours, skinned or not, shadows received): the mage's looks are some 25 palettes, half of them of one look (the same maps drawn one-sided, inside out and two-sided are three; so are parts that receive no shadow, vertex colours, each gem's clear coat), and the hair's both sides alone is no palette's; he switches material 33 times a frame where 58 (scene and shadow maps, any gear: an Epic item's copies are rows), in 28 materials where 53, the warrior 31 in 28, the ranger 34 in 29. A geometry's groups are nothing to one material (a box's six kept three looks out); a mesh of several materials (a coat and its lining) takes its vertices' rows by their group, each material its look's palette. The two hands' looks share palettes too (`wrapsKit`: their shader wraps the kit's, skinned by dual quaternions with its maps read triplanar, so a hand's palette leaves the normal map's depth and the repeat to its looks, alike; what sets a hand apart, its glowing outline and its glove's glow, are the kit's looks, in its rows); any other wrapper may keep a state of its own a material (the fur's lag), and stays out. Not what a palette's parts receive of shadows: made to receive them all, to join the glows' palette to the rest, the ranger's arrows shadowed themselves, darker along their length (4% of a picture's pixels against main's), so looks that differ in it stay apart. His pictures against main's differ in a few pixels at seams, where the draws' order between coplanar parts changed (`lab sheet --ab`, 0.00 to 0.02% of the pixels). Fewer still needs the looks' maps in one texture array (seven sets of them keep the textured looks apart) and the rigid and skinned parts in one program. `npm run lab -- draws --wear helm,chest,hands` says a hero's switches and fails over 37 (`--canary`).
- **A hero's shadow is cast as one** (`models/castAsOne.ts`, `Player.shadow`; #270): his rigid pieces of one front-sided material that never change shape (`castsAsOne`: no custom depth material, no alpha test, positions `StaticDrawUsage`) are merged into one skinned mesh the shadow map alone draws (`inShadowsOnly`), each piece a bone of its own that is the piece itself, drawn with `SKIN_DEPTH` (compiles nothing). It's made again only when a piece is shown or hidden or the quality cuts it from casting (`applyShadowDetail`), never per frame; a piece on a layer the camera doesn't see (the body through the eyes) is shrunk to nothing through its bone's inverse (three makes a skeleton's matrices again as it draws: zeroed before, the hidden body still cast), and the pieces cast alone are taken off for the draw and put back after (`aroundShadows` in `world/culling.ts`). The mesh hangs from the player's object, not the model: the first person sorts the model's meshes onto its layers once. A geometry whose positions change (a cloth, a skirt, a bowstring) must say so (`DynamicDrawUsage`), or it casts as it was merged; one that changes anyway is let go of to cast alone, with an error in dev. It saves 31 to 36 shadow draws a hero, about 0.1 ms of the ranger's and the mage's shadow pass in Chrome, the warrior's within the noise: the draws it saves were cheap ones. Check its look with `npm run lab -- shadows` (each weapon style, armoured and bare, each view); `?castAsOne=0` casts each piece alone.
- **On WebGPU a hero is drawn as one** (`models/drawAsOne.ts`, `Player.drawn`; `?drawAsOne=0` each part alone): three's WebGPU path costs about three times its WebGL path a draw, and a moving part writes all its uniforms at each one, so a hero (77 of the forest lobby's 134 scene draws, 22 of its 46 shadow draws) was most of why WebGPU's frame took twice WebGL's. His parts are merged by material into meshes standing still at the scene's origin, every vertex reading its joints' matrices off one texture (`jointedPosition`), and those meshes sit in a render bundle (`BundleGroup`), recorded once: his 31 draws are one command in the pass, and all his moving writes one texture. One mesh casts the merged parts' shadows; a cloth, whose positions change, still draws and casts alone. In turns under the profiler, the frame's JS read 15.6 and 19.7 ms against 20.5 and 23.3 (the machine loaded; compare in the same run). Merged as skinned meshes, half the draws gained 4%: three writes a skinned mesh's bones into its own uniforms at each draw. What's shown changes without a merge (each rebuilt the whole hero, 800 ms as the hall of heroes opened, and at every arrow a ranger took): a merged part hidden is shrunk by its gate (`jointGate`), a part shown that isn't merged draws alone, and only `SETTLED` views in a row of that merges again. A material on a merged mesh is a stand-in (`standInFor`) with an own property reading each of its base's: three keys a build by its material's own properties (inherited alone, materials with and without a map shared a build, its map null: the page never loaded), and a copy would miss a glow's pulse. A compile calls the scene's `onBeforeRender` and no `onAfterRender`, so what's set round a view (`aroundViews`) must not trust it was undone: believed, the hero's merged meshes stood where they first drew, his cloth walking off without him. A hand or a fur mesh draws alone (`userData.drawsAlone`): a hand's bones blend as dual quaternions and its maps read triplanar (`models/handNodes.ts`), and each fur mesh has its own lag. A merged mesh in the scene shows whatever its hero does: `view` looks as far up as the scene (the hall's heroes are kept out of it, and drew over the lobby).
- **A skinned mesh met by a ray** (`models/hulls.ts`, for the aim: `fx/stuckArrows.ts` `castSolid`): three's own test poses every vertex by its bones first, 1 to 9 ms a body, so the aim met only the rigid meshes, and a ray over a boss's skinned limbs went on to the floor behind it. Each skinned geometry is cut into parts (the triangles of one piece it was merged from that one bone moves most of their first corner), each with its box as built and every bone that moves it. A posed vertex is a weighted mean of where each of its bones puts it, so the box round the part's box as each of those bones puts it holds the part as it's drawn; the bones' parts are met by such boxes, and only the triangles of the parts the ray meets are posed and met, exactly. (A box a bone in its own space left the skin bent between two bones out of it, and one round a bone's share of every piece met the gaps between legs.) About 0.07 ms a frame the aim is over a boss. The parts are made once a geometry, at warm-up (`reachOf`, from the `Enemy` constructor), which also gives the foe how far its body reaches (`Enemy.reach`) for the aim's first, cheap test. Every body of a kind shares its geometry and is bound in its build's pose, so its parts too.
- **Hero models** (`models/mage.ts`, `models/warrior.ts`, `models/ranger.ts`) strip the rig's stand-in capsules and dress it from shared parts:
  - **Proportions are a real man's** (ANSUR II, the US Army's survey): 7.5 heads tall, shoulders, chest and hips at real widths plus the clothing or armour on them. Size new gear to that frame; exaggerated shoulders make the head look small.
  - Their parts have notes of their own, with what each was measured against and how it went wrong before: `docs/agents/heads.md` (`models/face.ts` and `models/head.ts`: the face's distance field and masks, the hair, beards and necks, tuning a face with the face harness), `docs/agents/heroes.md` (the gear an item shows, the hands and their grips, the ranger's look against his reference sheet, armour and the skirts' drape, the engraving, the keyed full-body moves, the draught and the idles) and `docs/agents/ranger-bow.md` (`models/bow.ts` and the shot `models/ranger.ts` poses: the draw, the loose, the quiver, the carry, the bow through the eyes). Read the note before changing its part.
  - Hero geometry detail follows the quality preset (`heroDetail`), taken when a hero is built.
  - **A hero's builder is a generator** (`mageSteps`, `warriorSteps`, `rangerSteps`; `buildMage` and the rest build one whole, `models/steps.ts`): it yields between its pieces, so a hero is built behind the live lobby a piece a frame (`game/behind.ts`), where one built whole held a frame 100 to 170 ms. His head is one more such builder (`headSteps`, `yield*` from his; `buildHead` builds it whole), its geometry made by a worker as the game loads (`headShapes`, `preloadHeads`, `head.worker.ts`: no material, canvas or scene in it) and kept across visits: made in a build, its hair's cap, locks or beard took 200 to 270 ms a piece on the 4080. A `yield;` goes between statements of the builder's own body (a loop's pass, a block's step, a piece of its own generator: the mage's coat's `cut`), never inside a closure, and never between a `Sculpt` of a `kind` and its `build()` (its replay is the module's flag). Keep each piece under a few ms: `lab behind` names the slowest.
- **A body's ranges** (`models/anatomy.ts`): every joint of the hero rig measured as a clinician measures it (`measureBody`, degrees: the arm's raise, the plane it rises in and its turn on the chest, the elbow, the forearm's turn, the wrist's two bends, the hip, knee and ankle, the trunk's and the neck's turn and bends), against the adult norms (`ROM`; the arm's raise by its plane, `elevationLimit`: straight back it stops at 60, 50 behind the side it rises only to about level), and the arms against the head and trunk (`bodyShape`, `overlaps`: ellipsoids from the rig's proportions and the head's skin, the forearms and hands as capsules). The rig's rest pose is standing, arms hanging, palms back (the forearms turned fully over). Posing a body by its end points (a hand to a spot, a foot level) and letting two-bone IK join them puts joints where none can go: the bow's draw elbow went straight back behind the body and the wrist inside the head, the weapon wrists bent 60 to 130 degrees round their handles, swinging feet bent 85 degrees up under bent knees, the trunk twisted 84 degrees. So the IK keeps to the ranges: `armIK.ts` (`solveArm`, `fitArm`) places the elbow, of every place on its circle round the shoulder to wrist line, where the shoulder, the forearm's turn and the wrist are within range and the arm keeps out of the head and trunk, nearest the pose's elbow and last frame's (warm-started from it); a held handle may also slant across the palm and a lower grip turn about its handle (both picked from a few each solve, or with `glide` eased from the last solve's towards the best, found between the ones tried); a hand coming onto a grip may ease its turn on the forearm from its own (`ease`), and an elbow's move a frame may be capped (`maxMove`: past it, it goes round its circle towards the best, a swing and not a jump); the forearm's turn and the wrist stop at their ends, so what the hand holds turns only as far as they allow. Each hero fits every arm after its pose (`fitArm`, third person: the weapon arm first, then a two-hander's lower grip, then the other arm), the hand where the pose put it. The shoulder girdle (`GIRDLE`, `girdlePlace`: the collarbone lifting the shoulder and drawing it forward or back) moves only where the shoulder joint is, so an arm keeps its meaning, and only when an arm asks for it (`ArmGoal.girdle`) and no elbow keeps it in range at rest. The leg IK holds a foot in the air within the ankle's range (it hangs from the shin, `clampAnkle`), turns a planted foot's knee towards it within the hip's turn (and a planted foot the hips turn further from than the hip and ankle allow together pivots on its ball after them, `PIVOT_EARLY`, and again by as much as its twist on the shin, measured after the leg is solved, is past `ANKLE_TWIST`: with the leg stretched out behind, the hips swinging back over it at a reversal twisted it 13 degrees past), keeps a foot in the air within the hip's turn and the ankle's twist of the hips (wheeling round onto a shot behind, a stepping foot kept the way it left the ground and the hip turned 56 degrees), and caps the pelvis's turn towards the way the body goes so it and the pose's own twist stay within the trunk's (`TRUNK`; past it the legs step more across the pelvis's line, as a person's do). Dev builds report each joint taken past its range once per action (`watchBody`, from `Player.animateBody`; not through the eyes, whose arms keep hand-placed poses). `?rom=0` lets the joints go past their ranges again, for A/B. The IK stopping the joints at their ends means a pose that asks too much of an arm is held there, every joint at its stop and none past it: `range` says an arm at two or more stops at once (two-handed Power Strike's end, the forearm at 89.9° of its 90 and the wrist at 30° of its 30, looked "broken" and nothing reported it; every hero has such frames, #307). Measure with `npm run lab -- range` (the range probe ported into the lab: `--gear` for a weapon style, `--skill` for a skill cast standing; still to port: each skill cast on the move, and the arms' depth in the body), check its conventions on a bare rig in known poses first, and film a change against `?rom=0` with the same gear (the default profile rolls its weapons). What is still past a range: a two-hander's lower wrist through the swings, 14 to 17 degrees by the weapon (both hands on one handle: the weapon would have to tilt between them; `docs/agents/heroes.md`), and a few feet rolled onto their edge and hips opened 5 to 15 degrees in wide stances.
- **The arm's search and what wakes it** (`solveArm` in `armIK.ts`): the girdle's places, each searched over the hand's rolls and slants, are searched only while an arm needs the girdle or it is still off its rest, and it counts as at rest within `G_HOME`: eased alone, a float took about 1700 frames to reach nothing, so at 144 frames a second every shot left that search on for 12 s, and shooting never let it stop. A ranger shooting (`npm run lab -- range --class ranger --scenarios shoot --ab`) read a median of 2.3 ms a frame's pose and p90 3.8, now 1.7 and 2.1, its worst 6 to 7 where 9 to 44 (a commit against itself agrees, so the A/B isn't what moves them). Places, rolls and slants are tried cheapest of themselves first, so more are passed over; that alone showed no steady gain. Between a pose's steps only its joints are walked (`updateJoints` in `rig.ts`), as what it reads there is joints: about 7% of `animate`, every bit of the pose the same (`poses --ab`).
- **Leg IK and head look-at** (`models/ik.ts`; `?ik=0` turns both off and restores the walk cycle's own legs and gait for A/B): `LegIK` runs at the end of a model's `animate` (heroes and every walking enemy, not the floating witch) and takes the legs over, and `LookAt` turns the chest, neck and head onto the nearest foe (heroes) or the target (enemies).
