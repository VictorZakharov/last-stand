# `src/perf`

Performance reports a slow game sends with its player's consent, for agents to read and fix (`npm run lab -- trace`).

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md); the service they go to is `server/traces/AGENTS.md`.

- `recorder.ts`: always on, from the boot to the page's end, in every scene (the boot, the hero selection screen, the lobby, a fight, a loading bar's wait). Each frame's times and counts go into a ring of columns (`RING`: about the last minute), beside the boot's steps, the long animation frames and marks (a scene changing, a wave, a biome, a hero built, a cast or channel of the local hero's, a loading bar's end, the window's size and full screen as they change: a phone's trace held up 212 ms in a resize's listeners, and nothing in it said the window had changed, trace Z8GY-RHSN). An update stage's column is named apart from the fixed columns (`stageColumn`: the stage `particles` took the particles' count's column, kind and all, and the report read 0.0999 particles; `lab recorder` checks every count's kind). `perfHud.ts` hands it every frame: its timings run whether the overlay shows or not.
- `webgpuPasses.ts`: WebGPU's GPU time split by pass, as WebGL's is by its timer queries: three times every render pass and tells its inspector each one's timestamp id as it begins (`beginRender`), so each is named by it (the sun's shadow, the scene, the post chain, what's drawn over the frame, other) and every frame's split goes to the recorder (`gpu <part>` columns; the report prints each part's history under the GPU's, where every frame has it) and to `lab perf` (the GPU by part). Named by the call's order in its frame, the scene's pass read as the shadow's in every frame the sun's shadow drew (three draws it inside the scene's call); names let go of all at once lost the frame being read every 50 or so. From the owner's phone: its GPU took 11.5 ms a frame paused and 24 in the lobby, the same view (traces N5TH-2XC9, R5FJ-9TNN), which the lab's GPU drew alike (4.6 against 4.8 ms at the phone's pixels): the split says which pass grows there.
- `detect.ts`: pure. Once a second the last stretch is judged: over 20 s of play (a paused game, the pause menu, a hidden tab, a warm-up, a loading bar and the 3 s after a scene starts left out), a 1% low under 30 frames a second (the display's jitter taken off its frame, never added: a steady 60 on an S25 Edge's 120 Hz screen drops a frame now and then, 33.4 ms, and read raw it was 29.9 a second and sent as slow, trace R2XY-H2DW; and none at all where it's no longer than 2.2 of the game's own frames, a refresh missed now and then: a steady 60 on a 60 Hz screen whose Chrome ticked at another screen's 145 Hz showed a frame for 5 ticks now and then, 34.7 ms, a low of 29, and was sent as slow, trace T9NG-CZ79, `ONE_MISSED`), or over a fifth of the display's frames missed with the mean under 50 (the display's rate is the fastest second seen); over every frame shown but a hidden tab's, stalls (one of a second or more, or three of 250 ms); a boot whose own work (its time less the time it waited on its files) took over 15 s; a loading bar up over 8 s.
- **A slow boot's trace waits for the play after it** (`BOOT_TAIL_MS`, 45 s, in `recorder.ts`): taken at the reveal, a laptop's held its last second alone, 17 frames, while its player saw 5 frames a second for a minute, and the session's one ask went on it (trace JGNM-1NCF). It's taken 45 s after the reveal with the detector's verdict on that play beside the boot's time (`Sent because: boot, the boot took 18.4 s; the play after it: ...`), anything else found slow meanwhile left to it. `lab recorder` checks it (its canary: taken at the reveal).
- `consent.ts`: the prompt (`#perf-ask`, `styles/_perfAsk.scss`) and what the player chose (`localStorage` `last-stand-perf-traces`; the pause menu's Slow-game reports: Ask, Always, Off).
- `upload.ts`: the snapshot packed with the device, the settings and the build, gzipped by the browser and posted to `<relay host>/traces`.
- `traceFormat.ts`: the packed format (pure, no imports: Node runs it as it is, the lab and the service's tests import it). `traceReport.ts`: a trace read as the perf overlay's report reads (pure: `lab trace`).
- `profiler.ts`: the pause menu's Profile this spot (Performance tab, `ui/profileTab.ts`): the game's frames held (`holdFrames` in `core/frameLoop.ts`), the frame where the player stands drawn again and again with each part of it taken out and put back in turns (`PROFILE_TURNS`: 6 rounds of 3 frames a side, short turns so the GPU's clocks drifting fall on both sides alike), one timed frame in each of the browser's frames (drawn back to back the page never painted its progress), waited for (WebGL: a pixel read back; WebGPU: its queue's work done, `gpuDone` in `core/webgpu.ts`) and timed by the browser's clock, or on WebGPU by the GPU's own timestamps with nothing waited for, the passes of each draw's node frames summed once three has read them (`PassReader` in `webgpuPasses.ts`, the overlay's reads held meanwhile: drawn alone and waited out, the phone's 12 ms frame read 37, its GPU idle between frames and clocked down, trace 58GW-MY55; a read three skips leaves its queries to the next, and summed read by read its frames came to 1 to 4 frames' time, 430N-GTXH): timed round the queue's wait, the phone's frame read 74 ms where the browser showed it at 60 a second, its shadow maps' part -38 ms (trace 4ADF-XJBB). The parts are listed by what they take. The parts are `sceneParts.ts`'s: half the scene's pixels, the point lights where they reach, the shadow maps' drawing, the lit shading against a plain colour, what's cut out (leaves, grass), what's blended (smoke, glows, effects), the fires' flames (`userData.fireFlames`), the particles, the hero. Then, on WebGPU alone, the lit shading's own parts (`shadingParts.ts`): its environment, the shadows the surfaces take, the fog and the surface maps (normal, roughness, metalness, occlusion: each mesh given a copy of its material without them, as a map set to null on a material WebGPU had made a node material of was read as null and the frame threw). Each changes the lit pipelines, made once by its first frame and kept: 94 in the lab's crypt, the profile 24 s there. From the owner's phone: the lit shading was 41 ms of a 69 ms frame at Very low (trace 4NS4-8PG2), where this PC at the phone's pixels reads 1 to 4, so the phone times what of it costs that. On WebGL they'd compile every program in view in the frames timed: listed as not timed. **Each of the frame's parts keeps the scene's programs**: one that changes a program's key (a light hidden, `castShadow` off, the environment taken off) made three compile every program in view again on its first frame, 66 s of a 69 s profile in the lab and a 4070 Ti's page answering nothing until Chrome offered to kill it; now 4 programs (the plain colour's), the profile about 3.5 s there, and `lab profile` fails over 8 (24 pipelines on WebGPU), the lit shading's own parts' left out. Then what the page puts over the scene is timed another way, by the frame's interval from one of the browser's frames to the next with nothing waited for (`pageParts`, `PAGE_TURNS`: means, as an interval steps by the display's refresh): the panels (the HUD, the lobby's, the overlays) and the pause menu with its blur, each shown alone in turns (`profiling-panels`, `profiling-pause` on the body). A draw waited for leaves out the browser putting the page together: a phone drew its lobby's scene in 8.8 ms (trace 05C8-3E1V) and showed 47 to 53 frames a second there, 30 to 38 under the pause menu. While it runs every other panel is hidden (`body.profiling`: the pause menu blurred the whole screen behind it, a phone GPU's work each frame), a pane at the top says how far it is and what's left, and Cancel or Esc stops it, every part put back. Built for a phone whose frame halved by the bonfire and under Maelstrom, which this PC's GPU can't play: its tiled GPU pays for cut-out and blended pixels as a desktop's doesn't, so the device times itself. **On WebGPU too** (`?renderer=webgpu`, `&meshlets=1`), the same report, its first line naming the renderer: three's passes draw their scene once a node frame, which its own animation loop begins at each of the browser's frames, so each of the game's frames begins one of its own (`newNodeFrame` in `renderWebGPU`: the lab's clock holds that loop, and the profile drew the scene once alone, every part reading the post chain; on a phone the game went on with the scene's picture stuck, "everything stuck, but not frozen", till the profiler's draws moved it; `lab profile` walks the hero with three's loop held, and with `--canary` with no node frame at all, which must read stuck); a light's shadow is drawn by its own `autoUpdate` there, never the renderer's (the shadow maps' part sets each light's, and holds the sun's, `holdSunShadow` in `core/renderer.ts`); a biome's point lights are drawn by a rig copied from them each render (`rigPointLights`), so their reach is set on the biome's own (`drawnFrom`); the particles are instanced quads, not points (`userData.particles`); and what it compiles is counted as pipelines made, at a draw or not (`pipelinesMade`). three lets a pipeline go once nothing draws with it, and made it again at the next turn: while it runs they're kept (`holdPipelines`), and the plain colour's 12 to 16 are made once, in its first frame, which is never timed (a compile of them ahead, `compileAsync`, made pipelines the draws didn't take). The canary's second frame waits for the GPU to be done with the first: WebGPU takes each frame as it's sent, and drawn back to back the second's CPU work ran beside the first's GPU work (1.3 times one). On `&meshlets=1` half the scene's pixels isn't timed, and the report and the tab say so (`ScenePart.untimed`): each resize sent every GPU-driven batch through three's node builder again, 420 to 670 ms of the main thread a time and its frames slower after, 12 times a profile, and settled 6 frames both its sides still read high. Its result is sent as a trace (`sendProfile` in `consent.ts`: `why: profile`, the lines in `reason.profile`, how long it took and the programs it compiled in the first), at once under Always, by its Send button under Ask, never under Off, counted in `MOST_A_DAY`; `lab trace` prints it under Profile. Run it in the lab with `npm run lab -- profile` (`--device`, `--renderer webgpu|meshlets`, `--canary`: the frame drawn twice; a cancelled run checked each time, and the result sent as Send sends it to a traces service of the lab's own and read back as `lab trace` reports it), the tab and the pane with `lab ui --states pausePerformance,profiling`.

## Gotchas

- **Never during a fight, never blocking.** A slow stretch found is kept as a snapshot at once (only copied: 1.9 ms for a full minute in the lab's browser, `lab recorder --frames 3600`), and the player is asked once a calm moment has lasted 1.5 s: the choice after a wave (the prompt under it: both stand at the top middle, and the prompt hid its buttons), the summary, the lobby, the hero selection screen. A fight starting hides it, and it's shown again at the next calm. The packing and gzip wait for the calm too.
- **Asked at most once a session; three Not nows and it stops asking.** Always keeps sending without asking, 10 minutes apart. **However asked, at most 10 sends in any 24 hours** (`MOST_A_DAY`), each counted as it's tried and kept in `localStorage` across reloads; the traces server holds each address to the same from each site (`localStorage` is the site's own, and so is the server's count: each PR's preview and commit build has its own 10). Off never snapshots anything (`recorder.wanted`: nothing is copied unless it could go somewhere). In co-op each game asks its own player about its own frames.
- **Nothing about the player**: no names, items, rooms, invite codes or address beyond the page's path and its render switches (`KEPT_SWITCHES`). The prompt says what's in it and its size.
- **Compact, no text repeated**: each column a byte or two a frame (a time as a step of 8% on a log scale from 0.05 ms, under it 0, each within 4%; the frame's interval to a fiftieth of a ms; counts as they are), split into byte planes so gzip finds their runs, and every mark's and long frame's text once in a string table. Real traces at 120 frames a second come to 40 to 44 KB a minute gzipped, about 5.8 B a frame. A trace over the 1 MB the service takes is still cut to its newest half until it fits (`packTrace`).
- **Tune the packing on real traces, not made-up values**: take one from a game in the installed Chrome with `npm run stalls -- --scenario <scene> --read "window.__traceNow()"` (`__traceNow` is in every build: the packed snapshot in base64) and read it with `npm run lab -- trace --file tools/stalls/out/read-N.json --sizes`: each part's weight, and the trace packed each other way the format can (`Packing`: the steps, the floor, columns as each frame's change) against its own. On four (a fight, the lobby, the hero screen, a stash sweep), 8% steps beat 4% by 11 to 17%, values as they are beat each frame's change by 6 to 8%, and 12% steps would save 10% more at 6% each. A trace whose header names no packing was packed the format's first way (`FIRST_PACKING`).
- **The service worker never sees an upload**: it answers GETs of the game's own files alone (`scripts/pwa.ts`), so a POST to another host passes it by, under any build's worker. The request is a plain one (no headers of its own, no cookies), so no preflight.
- **Measure it with `npm run lab -- recorder`**: the recorder's calls timed each frame in a fight (0.02 to 0.03 ms a frame on the owner's PC, against a budget of 0.05), the trace packed, sent to a traces service of the lab's own and read back with its token; `--canary` makes each frame's recording 0.2 ms slower, which it must catch. The prompt's states are `lab ui --states perfAsk,perfAskBreak,perfAskTouch`. The detector's and the format's unit tests are in `tools/lab/test/trace.test.mjs`.
- The lab's frames run back to back without yielding a task, and the GPU's timer queries come back only after one, so `lab recorder` sees few GPU times or none (`lab perf` yields after each frame for them): a player's paced frames have them.
