# `src/perf`

- Manual recordings include one-second input arrival/handler/press/movement counts, movement delivery timing and focus/fullscreen/keyboard-lock state, without key values, coordinates or typed text. `recordingBoundary` resets counters at Start and flushes the last partial second before either explicit or bounded Stop freezes its marks; otherwise stopping just after a recovered burst would discard the evidence. A native queue can hold a key before its DOM timestamp is created: no delayed-delivery mark does not prove there was no native input delay. `device.fullscreen` is HTML fullscreen; `device.installed` historically also matches fullscreen display mode, including an ordinary Chrome fullscreen window. `?keyboardLock=0` is retained as an allowed diagnostic switch and disables only the native lock, not fullscreen.

- Manual performance recording (`ui/recordingTab.ts`, the pause menu's Performance tab) selects Start through Stop from the existing ring, including menu/paused frames. It stops at 60 s or before the ring overwrites its start at high uncapped rates. Start is selected by both frame count and the actual start-mark reference: timestamps alone included an earlier frame/mark sharing Start's timestamp, while filtering at the first frame lost resolution and keyboard marks emitted immediately after Start. The stopped snapshot is immutable and retries send the same compressed bytes. No half-history trimming for manual recordings: show the compressed size and refuse a body over `TRACE_MAX_BYTES`. A stopped snapshot is at most 44 columns × 16,383 frames × 8 bytes (about 5.5 MiB), only while packing; the ongoing ring is reused, not duplicated. No GPU resources are allocated for capture.
- `manualHeld` suppresses detector snapshots and the automatic consent prompt/send path throughout capture and review. Starting clears the old pending automatic trace and boot-tail wait; a pack already in progress must not resurrect it. Send/discard releases suppression without changing the saved policy. Explicit Send recording does not use automatic prompt/session/client-day gates, and remains available after an earlier automatic report; existing service size/rate checks still apply. Profile-this-spot must not auto-send via Always while a manual recording is held. `lab recorder` exercises the actual controls, failure/retry, exact boundaries, both capture limits, suppression, and upload/readback against its own service.
- Keyboard delivery timing records only delay and destination tag, never which key or typed content (`core/keyDelivery.ts`, `main.ts`). Focus transitions and resolution choices are marks. An animating scene with queued keyboard events is not by itself proof of a long game task; compare the event-delivery marks with frame/LOAF timings before claiming a cause.

Performance reports a slow game sends with its player's consent, for agents to read and fix (`npm run lab -- trace`).

The rules across the game are the [root AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md); the service they go to is `server/traces/AGENTS.md`.

- `recorder.ts`: always on, from the boot to the page's end, in every scene (the boot, the hero selection screen, the lobby, a fight, a loading bar's wait). Each frame's times and counts go into a ring of columns (`RING`: about the last minute), beside the boot's steps, the long animation frames and marks (a scene changing, a wave, a biome, a hero built, a cast or channel of the local hero's, a loading bar's end, the window's size and full screen as they change: a phone's trace held up 212 ms in a resize's listeners, and nothing in it said the window had changed, trace Z8GY-RHSN). An update stage's column is named apart from the fixed columns (`stageColumn`: the stage `particles` took the particles' count's column, kind and all, and the report read 0.0999 particles; `lab recorder` checks every count's kind). `perfHud.ts` hands it every frame: its timings run whether the overlay shows or not.
- `detect.ts`: pure. Once a second the last stretch is judged: over 20 s of play (a paused game, the pause menu, a hidden tab, a warm-up, a loading bar and the 3 s after a scene starts left out), a 1% low under 30 frames a second (the display's jitter taken off its frame, never added: a steady 60 on an S25 Edge's 120 Hz screen drops a frame now and then, 33.4 ms, and read raw it was 29.9 a second and sent as slow, trace R2XY-H2DW; and none at all where it's no longer than 2.2 of the game's own frames, a refresh missed now and then: a steady 60 on a 60 Hz screen whose Chrome ticked at another screen's 145 Hz showed a frame for 5 ticks now and then, 34.7 ms, a low of 29, and was sent as slow, trace T9NG-CZ79, `ONE_MISSED`), or over a fifth of the display's frames missed with the mean under 50 (the display's rate is the fastest second seen); over every frame shown but a hidden tab's, stalls (one of a second or more, or three of 250 ms); a boot whose own work (its time less the time it waited on its files) took over 15 s; a loading bar up over 8 s.
- **A slow boot's trace waits for the play after it** (`BOOT_TAIL_MS`, 45 s, in `recorder.ts`): taken at the reveal, a laptop's held its last second alone, 17 frames, while its player saw 5 frames a second for a minute, and the session's one ask went on it (trace JGNM-1NCF). It's taken 45 s after the reveal with the detector's verdict on that play beside the boot's time (`Sent because: boot, the boot took 18.4 s; the play after it: ...`), anything else found slow meanwhile left to it. `lab recorder` checks it (its canary: taken at the reveal).
- The eight worst pre-reveal long animation frames are retained separately from the rolling play history (`bootStalls.ts`). Their timings are from navigation and their existing script labels go into `game.bootStalls`, printed by `lab trace` as Loading stalls. No trace-version change or new personal information: old readers ignore this optional string. Without this, the 45 s of play after reveal discarded the loading freeze being reported. `lab recorder` plants a loading stall before the play window and verifies it survives the actual upload path.
- The play window keeps its worst 200 long frames, not merely its last 200. Otherwise many 50–100 ms frames on a slow device evicted a five-second freeze before the delayed report was sent. Expire rows outside the minute, then discard the shortest when full; never retain old worst rows indefinitely.
- `sceneNote.ts`: where a report was taken and what the hero was doing, a line kept with each snapshot (his place and facing, standing, walking, casting or channelling which skill, the camera's view, zoom and turn): `lab trace` prints it as `Scene:`. From the owner: "so you know where exactly it was taken? angle of the character, what they were doing".
- **A report names the programs this device refused** (`auditPrograms` in `core/shaders.ts`, asked as the report is put together, 40 ms at most): a production build never asks whether a program linked, so one a phone's driver refused drew nothing and said nothing (#470: an S25 Edge's hero his hair and his shadow, every desktop check passed). `lab trace` prints them under `SHADERS FAILED TO LINK`. Try a material or shader change on the phone's preview and read its report before calling it ready.
- `consent.ts`: the prompt (`#perf-ask`, `styles/_perfAsk.scss`) and what the player chose (`localStorage` `last-stand-perf-traces`; the pause menu's Slow-game reports: Ask, Always, Off).
- `upload.ts`: the snapshot packed with the device, the settings and the build, gzipped by the browser and posted to `<relay host>/traces`.
- `traceFormat.ts`: the packed format (pure, no imports: Node runs it as it is, the lab and the service's tests import it). `traceReport.ts`: a trace read as the perf overlay's report reads (pure: `lab trace`).
- `profiler.ts`: the pause menu's Profile this spot (Performance tab, `ui/profileTab.ts`): the game's frames held (`holdFrames` in `core/frameLoop.ts`), the frame where the player stands drawn again and again with each part of it taken out and put back in turns (`PROFILE_TURNS`: 6 rounds of 3 frames a side, short turns so the GPU's clocks drifting fall on both sides alike), one timed frame in each of the browser's frames (drawn back to back the page never painted its progress), waited for (WebGL: a pixel read back; WebGPU: its queue's work done, `gpuDone` in `core/webgpu.ts`) and timed by the browser's clock, the parts listed by what they take. The parts are `sceneParts.ts`'s: half the scene's pixels, the point lights where they reach, the shadow maps' drawing, the lit shading against a plain colour, what's cut out (leaves, grass), what's blended (smoke, glows, effects), the fires' flames (`userData.fireFlames`), the particles, the hero. Then, on WebGPU alone, the lit shading's own parts (`shadingParts.ts`): its environment, the shadows the surfaces take, the fog and the surface maps (normal, roughness, metalness, occlusion: each mesh given a copy of its material without them, as a map set to null on a material WebGPU had made a node material of was read as null and the frame threw). Each changes the lit pipelines, made once by its first frame and kept: 94 in the lab's crypt, the profile 24 s there. From the owner's phone: the lit shading was 41 ms of a 69 ms frame at Very low (trace 4NS4-8PG2), where this PC at the phone's pixels reads 1 to 4, so the phone times what of it costs that. On WebGL they'd compile every program in view in the frames timed: listed as not timed. **Each of the frame's parts keeps the scene's programs**: one that changes a program's key (a light hidden, `castShadow` off, the environment taken off) made three compile every program in view again on its first frame, 66 s of a 69 s profile in the lab and a 4070 Ti's page answering nothing until Chrome offered to kill it; now 4 programs (the plain colour's), the profile about 3.5 s there, and `lab profile` fails over 8 (24 pipelines on WebGPU), the lit shading's own parts' left out. Then what the page puts over the scene is timed another way, by the frame's interval from one of the browser's frames to the next with nothing waited for (`pageParts`, `PAGE_TURNS`: means, as an interval steps by the display's refresh): the panels (the HUD, the lobby's, the overlays) and the pause menu with its blur, each shown alone in turns (`profiling-panels`, `profiling-pause` on the body). A draw waited for leaves out the browser putting the page together: a phone drew its lobby's scene in 8.8 ms (trace 05C8-3E1V) and showed 47 to 53 frames a second there, 30 to 38 under the pause menu. While it runs every other panel is hidden (`body.profiling`: the pause menu blurred the whole screen behind it, a phone GPU's work each frame), a pane at the top says how far it is and what's left, and Cancel or Esc stops it, every part put back. Built for a phone whose frame halved by the bonfire and under Maelstrom, which this PC's GPU can't play: its tiled GPU pays for cut-out and blended pixels as a desktop's doesn't, so the device times itself. **On WebGPU too** (`?renderer=webgpu`, `&meshlets=1`), the same report, its first line naming the renderer: three's passes draw their scene once a node frame, which its own animation loop begins at each of the browser's frames, so each of the game's frames begins one of its own (`newNodeFrame` in `renderWebGPU`: the lab's clock holds that loop, and the profile drew the scene once alone, every part reading the post chain; on a phone the game went on with the scene's picture stuck, "everything stuck, but not frozen", till the profiler's draws moved it; `lab profile` walks the hero with three's loop held, and with `--canary` with no node frame at all, which must read stuck); a light's shadow is drawn by its own `autoUpdate` there, never the renderer's (the shadow maps' part sets each light's, and holds the sun's, `holdSunShadow` in `core/renderer.ts`); a biome's point lights are drawn by a rig copied from them each render (`rigPointLights`), so their reach is set on the biome's own (`drawnFrom`); the particles are instanced quads, not points (`userData.particles`); and what it compiles is counted as pipelines made, at a draw or not (`pipelinesMade`). three lets a pipeline go once nothing draws with it, and made it again at the next turn: while it runs they're kept (`holdPipelines`), and the plain colour's 12 to 16 are made once, in its first frame, which is never timed (a compile of them ahead, `compileAsync`, made pipelines the draws didn't take). The canary's second frame waits for the GPU to be done with the first: WebGPU takes each frame as it's sent, and drawn back to back the second's CPU work ran beside the first's GPU work (1.3 times one). On `&meshlets=1` half the scene's pixels isn't timed, and the report and the tab say so (`ScenePart.untimed`): each resize sent every GPU-driven batch through three's node builder again, 420 to 670 ms of the main thread a time and its frames slower after, 12 times a profile, and settled 6 frames both its sides still read high. Its result is sent as a trace (`sendProfile` in `consent.ts`: `why: profile`, the lines in `reason.profile`, how long it took and the programs it compiled in the first), at once under Always, by its Send button under Ask, never under Off, counted in `MOST_A_DAY`; `lab trace` prints it under Profile. Run it in the lab with `npm run lab -- profile` (`--device`, `--renderer webgpu|meshlets`, `--canary`: the frame drawn twice; a cancelled run checked each time, and the result sent as Send sends it to a traces service of the lab's own and read back as `lab trace` reports it), the tab and the pane with `lab ui --states pausePerformance,profiling`.

## Gotchas

- **Never during a fight, never blocking.** A slow stretch found is kept as a snapshot at once (only copied: 1.9 ms for a full minute in the lab's browser, `lab recorder --frames 3600`), and the player is asked once a calm moment has lasted 1.5 s: the choice after a wave (the prompt under it: both stand at the top middle, and the prompt hid its buttons), the summary, the lobby, the hero selection screen. A fight starting hides it, and it's shown again at the next calm. The packing and gzip wait for the calm too.
- **Sent by default** (#567, from play: "by default, it should be sending, unless toggled off in settings"): a player who never chose is on Always (`kept` in `consent.ts`); Ask and Off are kept as chosen. Every report sent without asking tells the player, with its code and where to turn it off. The lab's pages boot with it Off (`freshStorage` in `tools/lab/node/browser.mjs`), or their slow headless frames would go to the game's own traces service.
- **A freeze or extreme lag is sent without asking** (`severe` in `detect.ts`: a frame of a second or more, or a 1% low under 15 frames a second; #567, from play: "if game detects lag/freezes, it should send report automatically"): under Ask too, at the next calm moment, and the player told so with its code and where to turn it off (`consent.ts` `sentUnasked`, the pause menu's Slow-game reports says so in its tooltip). Off still sends nothing, a player who said Not now three times isn't sent one either, and the sends without asking keep Always's 10 minutes apart and the day's 10. Slower play that isn't severe still asks first. `lab recorder` plants a 1.2 s freeze under Ask (the lab's clock jumped) and must find it sent to its own traces service unasked, the player told, then three 0.3 s stalls asked about; its canary judges the freeze as before (asked), which must be caught.
- **Asked at most once a session; three Not nows and it stops asking.** Always keeps sending without asking, 10 minutes apart. **However asked, at most 10 sends in any 24 hours** (`MOST_A_DAY`), each counted as it's tried and kept in `localStorage` across reloads; the traces server holds each address to the same from each site (`localStorage` is the site's own, and so is the server's count: each PR's preview and commit build has its own 10). Off never snapshots anything (`recorder.wanted`: nothing is copied unless it could go somewhere). In co-op each game asks its own player about its own frames.
- **Nothing about the player**: no names, items, rooms, invite codes or address beyond the page's path and its render switches (`KEPT_SWITCHES`). The prompt says what's in it and its size.
- Keyboard marks name only the event destination (`game`, or an editing element's tag), on a change, and page focus/blur. They contain no key codes, field values or typed text. A lively scene with mouse input but no keyboard response can now be distinguished from a blocked main thread or a focused field (the reports before this had no keyboard evidence).
- **Compact, no text repeated**: each column a byte or two a frame (a time as a step of 8% on a log scale from 0.05 ms, under it 0, each within 4%; the frame's interval to a fiftieth of a ms; counts as they are), split into byte planes so gzip finds their runs, and every mark's and long frame's text once in a string table. Real traces at 120 frames a second come to 40 to 44 KB a minute gzipped, about 5.8 B a frame. A trace over the 1 MB the service takes is still cut to its newest half until it fits (`packTrace`).
- **Tune the packing on real traces, not made-up values**: take one from a game in the installed Chrome with `npm run stalls -- --scenario <scene> --read "window.__traceNow()"` (`__traceNow` is in every build: the packed snapshot in base64) and read it with `npm run lab -- trace --file tools/stalls/out/read-N.json --sizes`: each part's weight, and the trace packed each other way the format can (`Packing`: the steps, the floor, columns as each frame's change) against its own. On four (a fight, the lobby, the hero screen, a stash sweep), 8% steps beat 4% by 11 to 17%, values as they are beat each frame's change by 6 to 8%, and 12% steps would save 10% more at 6% each. A trace whose header names no packing was packed the format's first way (`FIRST_PACKING`).
- **The service worker never sees an upload**: it answers GETs of the game's own files alone (`scripts/pwa.ts`), so a POST to another host passes it by, under any build's worker. The request is a plain one (no headers of its own, no cookies), so no preflight.
- **Measure it with `npm run lab -- recorder`**: the recorder's calls timed each frame in a fight (0.02 to 0.03 ms a frame on the owner's PC, against a budget of 0.05), the trace packed, sent to a traces service of the lab's own and read back with its token; `--canary` makes each frame's recording 0.2 ms slower, which it must catch. The prompt's states are `lab ui --states perfAsk,perfAskBreak,perfAskTouch`. The detector's and the format's unit tests are in `tools/lab/test/trace.test.mjs`.
- The lab's frames run back to back without yielding a task, and the GPU's timer queries come back only after one, so `lab recorder` sees few GPU times or none (`lab perf` yields after each frame for them): a player's paced frames have them.
